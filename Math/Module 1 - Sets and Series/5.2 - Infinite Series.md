# 5.2 - Infinite Series

> [!definition] Infinite Series
> An infinite series is a summation (denoted with $\sum$) across an [[5.1 - Infinite Sequences#^cb5e23]]. For a function $a_n$ an infinite series can be written as
> $$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} a_{n}=a_{1}+a_{2}+\dots$$

^07b41c

## Partial Sums

> [!definition] Partial Sum
> What if we only added a finite $k$ number of infinite terms? The $k$th **partial sum** of the [[#^07b41c]] $\sum_{n=1}^\infty a_{n}$ is
> $$S_{k}=\sum_{n=1}^ka_{n}=a_{1}+a_{2}+\dots+a_{k}$$

^e2db1a

If you know the expression for $S_n$ you can find the expression for $a_n$ by subtracting $S_{n-1}$.

$$
\begin{align}
S_{n}&=a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}+\dots+a_{n-1}+a_{n} \\
-\ S_{n-1}&=a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}+\dots+a_{n-1} \\
&S_{n}-S_{n-1}=a_{n}
\end{align}
$$

## Convergence and Divergence

> [!def] Sequence of Partial Sums
> The sequence of partial sums is an [[5.1 - Infinite Sequences#^cb5e23]] where index $k$ tells us the value of the $k$th [[#^e2db1a]] of an infinite series.
> $$\{S_{k}\}=\left\{k\in\mathbb{Z}^+:\sum_{n=1}^ka_{n}\right\}$$

^33a5b4

As $k\to\infty$, $\left\{S_{n}\right\}$ should converge to the **value of the infinite series**. One can think of this as being $S_{\infty}$.  So if $\left\{S_{n}\right\}$ converges to a number $S \in \mathbb{R}$ then the infinite series evaluates to $S$. If an infinite series evaluates to some finite value $S$, we say it **converges**.

> [!def] Convergent Series
> If the [[#^33a5b4]] of an infinite series is a [[5.1 - Infinite Sequences#^654999]] then the series **converges** to $L$.
>$$
>\lim_{ n \to \infty }S_{k}=L
>$$
