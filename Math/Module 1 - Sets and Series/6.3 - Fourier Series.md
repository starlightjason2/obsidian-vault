# 6.3 - Fourier Series

> [!def] Fourier Basis Functions
> The Fourier [[2.7 - Function Spaces#^basis-functions]] functions $e^{in\omega t}=\cos(n\omega t)+i\sin(n\omega t)$ (see [[3.6 - Complex Numbers#^eulers-formula]]) form an [[2.2 - Subspaces#^orthogonal-basis]] spanning every [[3.7 - Periodic Functions#^periodic-function]] in [[3.7 - Periodic Functions#^l2-space]]. For period $T=\frac{2\pi}{\omega}$ and infinite $n\in \mathbb{Z}$,
> $$L^{2}=\overline{span}\{e^{in\omega t} \}$$
>

^fourier-basis-functions

## Fourier Series

> [!def] Fourier Series
> The **Fourier series** $f(t)$ is an [[5.2 - Infinite Series#^infinite-series]] of the [[#^fourier-coefficients]] $\left\{a_{n}, \ b_{n} \right\}=\left\{c_{n} \right\}$ times the [[#^fourier-basis-functions]] $\left\{\sin n\omega t, \ \cos n\omega t \right\}=\left\{e^{i\omega t} \right\}$ for $n\in \mathbb{Z}$.
>
> $$f(t)=\frac{1}{2}a_{0}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{a_{n}\cos(n\omega t)+b_{n}\sin (n\omega t)}=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}{c_{n}e^{in\omega t}}$$

^fourier-series

> [!def] Fourier Coefficients
> The coefficients for the [[#^fourier-series]] form an [[5.1 - Infinite Sequences#^cb5e23]] $\left\{a_{n} \right\}, \ \left\{b_{n} \right\}$.
>
> $$
> \begin{align}
> a_{0}&=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}f(t)\,dt \\
> a_{n}&=\frac{2}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\cos(n\omega t)f(t)\,dt \\
> b_{n}&=\frac{2}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\sin(n\omega t)f(t)\,dt \\
> \end{align}
> $$
>
> Or, with [[3.6 - Complex Numbers#^eulers-formula]], $\left\{a_{n} \right\}, \ \left\{b_{n} \right\}=\left\{c_{n} \right\}$.
> $$c_{n}=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}f(t)e^{-in\omega t}\,dt$$

^fourier-coefficients

> [!thm] Fourier's Theorem
> Any [[3.7 - Periodic Functions#^periodic-function]] $f(t)\in L^{2}$ with period $T$ and angular frequency $\omega$ can be expressed as a [[#^fourier-series]].
>
> $$f(t)=a_{0}+\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}{a_{n}\cos(n\omega t)+b_{n}\sin (n\omega t)}=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}{c_{n}e^{in\omega t}}$$

^fouriers-theorem

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^fouriers-theorem]]

Consider a [[#^fourier-series]] $f(t)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}{c_{n}e^{in\omega t}}$ for period $T=\frac{2\pi}{\omega}$. We can change into the [[#^fourier-basis-functions]] with a [[2.7 - Function Spaces#^functional-inner-product]].

Multiply both sides by $e^{-in'\omega t}$ and integrate from $t=0$ to $t=T$.

$$
\int_{0}^{T}f(t)\,e^{-in'\omega t}\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}\left( \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}{c_{n}e^{in\omega t}} \right)e^{-in'\omega t}\,dt
$$

With integral properties, this becomes

$$
\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}{\int_{0}^{T}c_{n}\,e^{i(n-n')\omega t}\,dt}=\begin{cases}
c_{n}T & n=n' \\
0 & n\neq n'
\end{cases}
$$

So for $n=n'$,

$$
c_{n}=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}f(t)\,e^{-in'\omega t}\,dt
$$
`\end{proof}`
