# 2.1 - Vectors

> [!def] Column Vector
> A [[2.1 - Vector Spaces#^f36929]] $\mathbf{v}$ in [[3.6 - Complex Numbers#^8288b7]] $\mathbb{C}^{m}$ is equivalent to an $m\times 1$ [[2.2 - Matrices#^5bf009]], also written as a **ket** $\ket{v}$. Equal to the [[2.2 - Matrices#^673394]] of a [[#^c8ab29]].
>
> In standard [[2.6 - Coordinate Sytems#^000d2e]], the $j$th component is the magnitude along the $j$th [[2.2 - Subspaces#^8f5ebd]] vector $\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}_{j}$.
>
>
> $$
> \mathbf{v}\in \mathbb{C}^{m}\equiv\begin{bmatrix}
> v_{1} \\
> \vdots \\
> v_{m}
> \end{bmatrix}=v_{1}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}_{1}+\dots+v_{n}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}_{m}\equiv\ket{v}
> $$
>
>

> [!def] Row Vector
> A [[2.1 - Vector Spaces#^f36929]] $\mathbf{v}$ in [[3.6 - Complex Numbers#^8288b7]] $\mathbb{C}^{n}$ equivalent to an $1\times n$ [[2.2 - Matrices#^5bf009]], also written as a **bra** $\bra{v}$. Equal to the [[2.2 - Matrices#^673394]] of a [[#^41fef0]].
>
> $$
> \mathbf{v}^{\dagger}\equiv\begin{bmatrix}
> v_{1}  & \dots  & v_{n}
> \end{bmatrix}\equiv \bra{v} 
> $$
>
>
>

> [!def] Linear Combination
> A **linear combination**, or **superposition**, of a [[2.1 - Vector Spaces#^f36929]] [[2.0 - Sets#^742d66]] $\{\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{n} \}$ is a sum of any multiples of members of the set.
>
> $$\mathbf{v}=c_{1}\mathbf{v}_{1}+\dots +c_{n}\mathbf{v}_n$$

## Inner Product

> [!def] Inner Product
> Let $(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})\in \mathbb{C}^{n}$ each be a [[2.1 - Vector Spaces#^f36929]] in a . The **inner product** $\mathbf{v}\cdot \mathbf{w}$ is equal to the [[2.2 - Matrices#^7f9d75]] of a [[2.1 - Vectors#^c8ab29]] and a [[2.1 - Vectors#^41fef0]].
>
> Can also be expressed as a *bra* times a *ket*.
>
>
> $$\mathbf{v}\cdot \mathbf{w}\equiv \braket{v | w} \equiv\begin{bmatrix}
\bar{w}_{1} & \dots & \bar{w}_{n}
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}
v_{1} \\
\vdots \\
v_{n}
\end{bmatrix}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}\,\bar{w}_{i}$$
>

^efaf9e

> [!thm] Properties of Inner Product
> These are the properties of the [[2.1 - Vectors#^efaf9e]] $\mathbf{v\cdot \mathbf{u}}$, where $\mathbf{v}, \ \mathbf{u}$ are separated by angle $\theta$.
>
> 1. $$\mathbf{v}\cdot \mathbf{u}=\lvert{\mathbf{v}}\rvert\,\lvert{\mathbf{u}}\rvert\,\cos\theta$$
> 2. $$\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{u}$$
> 3. $$\mathbf{u}\cdot(\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{w})=\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{w}$$
> 4. $$a(\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v})=(a\mathbf{u})\cdot \mathbf{v}=\mathbf{u}\cdot(a\mathbf{v})$$
> 5. $$\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{u}=\lvert{\mathbf{u}}\rvert^{2}$$

### Norm

> [!def] Norm
> The norm of a $\left\lvert{\mathbf{v}}\right\rvert$ characterizes Euclidean distance from the origin.
>
> In [[3.6 - Complex Numbers#^8288b7]] $\mathbb{C}^{n}$, the norm squared is the [[2.1 - Vectors#^efaf9e]] of $\Ket{v}$ with its [[2.2 - Matrices#^673394]] $\bra{v}$.
>
> Equivalent to the Pythagorean theorem in [[2.2 - Subspaces#^19a139]].
>
> $$\left\lvert{\begin{bmatrix}
v_{1} \\
\vdots \\
v_{n}
\end{bmatrix}}\right\rvert\equiv\sqrt{\left\lvert{v_{1}}\right\rvert^{2}+\dots+\left\lvert{v_{n}}\right\rvert^{2}}=\sqrt{\braket{v |v }}$$
>
>

> [!def] Unit Vector
> A unit vector has a [[#^57eeb7]] of $1$. For any vector $\mathbf{v}$, we can find it's unit vector $\,\hat{\boldsymbol{v}}$.
> $$\,\hat{\boldsymbol{v}}=\frac{\mathbf{v}}{\lvert{\mathbf{v}}\rvert }$$

### Dot Product

> [!def] Dot Product
> The [[2.1 - Vectors#^efaf9e]] of two vectors $\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w}$ in [[2.2 - Subspaces#^19a139]] is called a **dot product**.
>
> $$\mathbf{v}\cdot \mathbf{w}\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{n}v_{i}\,w_{i}=\lvert{\mathbf{v}}\rvert\lvert{\mathbf{w}}\rvert\cos \theta$$
>

^b212ec

> [!thm] Properties of Dot Product
> These are the properties of the [[2.1 - Vectors#^b212ec]] $\mathbf{v\cdot \mathbf{u}}$, where $\mathbf{v}, \ \mathbf{u}$ are separated by angle $\theta$.
>
> 1. $$\mathbf{v}\cdot \mathbf{u}=\lvert{\mathbf{v}}\rvert\,\lvert{\mathbf{u}}\rvert\,\cos\theta$$
> 2. $$\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{u}$$
> 3. $$\mathbf{u}\cdot(\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{w})=\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{w}$$
> 4. $$a(\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v})=(a\mathbf{u})\cdot \mathbf{v}=\mathbf{u}\cdot(a\mathbf{v})$$
> 5. $$\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{u}=\lvert{\mathbf{u}}\rvert^{2}$$

> [!thm] Scalar Orthogonal Projection
> The [[#^57eeb7|Norm]] of the **orthogonal projection** of $\mathbf{v}$ onto $\mathbf{\boldsymbol{u}}$ can be found with the [[#^efaf9e]] $\mathbf{v}\cdot \hat{\boldsymbol{u}}$.
> $$\left\lvert{proj_{\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}}(\mathbf{v})}\right\rvert=\mathbf{v}\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}$$
>

^0ab8b3

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^0ab8b3]]
Let $\mathbf{w}$ be the projection of $\mathbf{v}$ onto unit vector $\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}$. We know $\mathbf{w}$ must be parallel to $\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}$, so $\mathbf{w}$ is a scalar multiple of $\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}$.

$$
\begin{align}
|\mathbf{w}| &= |\mathbf{v}|\cos\theta = |\mathbf{v}| \cdot \frac{\mathbf{v}\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}}{|\mathbf{v}||\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}|} \\
&= |\mathbf{v}| \cdot \frac{\mathbf{v}\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}}{|\mathbf{v}| \cdot 1} = \mathbf{v}\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}
\end{align}
$$

Therefore, the magnitude of the orthogonal projection of $\mathbf{v}$ onto unit vector $\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}$ is found with the dot product $\mathbf{v}\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}$.

`\end{proof}`

#### Orthogonality

> [!def] Orthogonal Vectors
> A set of vectors $\{\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{k} \}$ is **orthogonal** if for every vector $\mathbf{v}_{i}$ and every other vector $\mathbf{v}_{j}$, the [[2.1 - Vectors#^efaf9e]] $\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}=0$.
> $$\forall \,(i\neq j), \ \mathbf{v}_{i}\cdot \mathbf{v}_{j}=0 \text{ and } \ \mathbf{v}_{i}\neq 0\iff \mathbf{v}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{j}\text{ are orthogonal}$$

### Outer Product

> [!def] Outer Product
> Let $(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})\in \mathbb{C}^{n}$ each be a [[2.1 - Vector Spaces#^f36929]]. The **outer product** $\mathbf{v}\otimes \mathbf{w}$ is equal to the [[2.2 - Matrices#^7f9d75]] of a [[2.1 - Vectors#^41fef0]] and a [[2.1 - Vectors#^c8ab29]].
>
> Can also be expressed as a *ket* times a *bra*.
>$$\mathbf{v}\otimes \mathbf{w}\equiv\ket{v}\bra{w}\equiv\begin{bmatrix}
v_{1} \\
\vdots \\
v_{n}
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}
\bar{w}_{1} & \dots & \bar{w}_{n}
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}
v_{1}\bar{w}_{1} & \dots & v_{1}\bar{w}_{n} \\
\dots & \vdots & \dots \\
v_{n}\bar{w}_{1} & \dots & v_{n}\bar{w}_{n}
\end{bmatrix}$$

^4898e4

> [!thm] Vector Orthogonal Projection
> The **orthogonal projection** of $\mathbf{v}$ onto [[#^066f80]] $\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}$ can be found with the [[#^4898e4]] $\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}\otimes \hat{\boldsymbol{u}}$. Equal to the [[2.1 - Vectors#^0ab8b3]] times $\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}$.
>
>
> $$proj_{\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}}(\mathbf{v})=(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}\otimes \hat{\boldsymbol{u}})\cdot \mathbf{v}=(\mathbf{v}\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})\,\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}$$

^b64e03

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^b64e03]]
By definition, the [[#^4898e4]] is $\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}\otimes\hat{\boldsymbol{u}} = \hat{\boldsymbol{u}}\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}^{\dagger}$ and the [[2.1 - Vectors#^efaf9e]] is $\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}^{\dagger}\mathbf{v}=\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}\cdot \mathbf{v}$.

$$
\begin{align}
(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}\otimes\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})\mathbf{v} &= (\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}^{\dagger})\mathbf{v} = \hat{\boldsymbol{u}}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}^{\dagger}\mathbf{v}) \\
&= \hat{\boldsymbol{u}}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}\cdot\mathbf{v})= (\mathbf{v}\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})\,\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}
\end{align}
$$

`\end{proof}`

## Cross Product

> [!def] Cross Product
> Let $\mathbf{u}, \ \mathbf{v}$ each be a [[2.1 - Vector Spaces#^f36929]] with [[2.2 - Subspaces#^09a800]] $n\in \{3,7 \}$ The cross product outputs a vector orthogonal to both multiplicands, equal to the following [[2.4 - Determinants#^add7e3]].
>
> $$
> \mathbf{u}\times \mathbf{v}\equiv\begin{vmatrix}
> \,\hat{\boldsymbol{x}} &\,\hat{\boldsymbol{y}} &\,\hat{\boldsymbol{z}} \\
> \mathbf{u}_{x} &\mathbf{u}_{y} &\mathbf{u}_{z} \\
> \mathbf{v}_{x} &\mathbf{v}_{y} &\mathbf{v}_{z}
> \end{vmatrix}
> $$
>
> By the **right hand rule**, place your right hand in the direction of $\mathbf{u}$ and curl your fingers towards $\mathbf{v}$. Your thumb points in the direction of the cross product.

^982dd9

> [!thm] Properties of the Cross Product
> For any two vectors $\mathbf{v}, \ \mathbf{u}$ separated by angle $\theta$, these are the properties of the [[#^982dd9]] $\mathbf{v\times \mathbf{u}}$.
>
> 1. $$\lvert{\mathbf{u}\times \mathbf{v}}\rvert\,=\lvert{\mathbf{u}}\rvert\,\lvert{\mathbf{v}}\rvert\,\sin\theta$$
> 2. $$\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v}=-(\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{u})$$
> 3. $$\mathbf{u}\times(\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{w})=\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{w}$$
> 4. $$c\,(\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v})=c\,\mathbf{u}\times \mathbf{v}=\mathbf{u}\times c\,\mathbf{v}$$
> 5. $$\mathbf{u}\cdot(\mathbf{v}\times \mathbf{w})=(\mathbf{u}\times \mathbf{v})\cdot \mathbf{w}=\begin{vmatrix}u_{x} &u_{y} &u_{z} \\v_{x} &v_{y} &v_{z} \\w_{x} &w_{y} &w_{z}\end{vmatrix}$$
> 6. $$\mathbf{u}\times(\mathbf{v}\times \mathbf{w})=(\mathbf{u}\cdot \mathbf{w})\,\mathbf{v}-(\mathbf{u}\cdot \mathbf{v})\,\mathbf{w}$$

> [!thm] Area from Cross Product
> The $A$ of a parallelogram defined by the sides $\mathbf{u}$ and $\mathbf{v}$, is found with the [[#^982dd9]].
> $$A=\lvert{\mathbf{u}\times \mathbf{v}}\rvert $$
