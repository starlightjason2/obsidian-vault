# 2.2 - Matrices

> [!def] Matrix
> An $m\times n$ **matrix** $\mathbf{A}=[a_{ij}]$ is a table of numbers with $m$ rows and $n$ columns, where $a_{ij}$ represents the element at row $i$ and column $j$.
>
> Furthermore, $\mathbf{A}$ is the [[2.5 - Linear Transformations#^matrix-representation]] of a transformation of Cartesian space to the new basis $\left\{\mathbf{a}_{n} \right\}$.
>
> $$\mathbf{A}=\begin{bmatrix}
a_{ij}
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}
a_{11} & \dots & a_{1n} \\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\
a_{m1} & \dots & a_{mn}
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf{a}_{1} & \dots & \mathbf{a}_{n}
\end{bmatrix}$$
>
>
>

## Matrix Operations

> [!def] Equality of Matrices
> A [[#^5bf009]] $\mathbf{A}$ is equal to another matrix $\mathbf{B}$ if every element is equal and $\mathbf{A}, \ \mathbf{B}$ are the same shape.

> [!def] Identity Matrix
> The **identity** matrix $\mathbf{I}$ is an $n\times n$ [[2.2 - Matrices#^5bf009]] of the [[2.2 - Subspaces#^8f5ebd]] vectors in $\mathbb{R}^{n}$.
>$$
>\mathbf{I}=\begin{bmatrix}
1 & … & 0 \\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\
0 & \dots & 1\\
\end{bmatrix}
>$$

### Hermitian Adjoint

> [!def] Hermitian Adjoint
> Given a [[#^5bf009]] $\mathbf{A}$, the **Hermitian Adjoint** $\mathbf{A}^{\dagger}$ is the [[2.2 - Matrices#^653a35]] of the [[3.6 - Complex Numbers#^complex-conjugate]] of $\mathbf{A}$.
> $$\mathbf{A}^{\dagger}\equiv(\bar{\mathbf{A}})^{T}$$
>

^hermitian-adjoint

### Linear Combinations

> [!def] Matrix Linear Combinations
> Let $\mathbf{A}=[a_{ij}], \ \mathbf{B}=[b_{ij}]$ each be an $m\times n$ [[#^5bf009]]. A linear combination of $\mathbf{A}, \ \mathbf{B}$ is the [[2.1 - Vectors#^linear-combination]] of their column vectors.
> $$c_{1}\,\mathbf{A}+c_{2}\,\mathbf{B}=\begin{bmatrix}
c_{1}\,a_{11}+c_{2}\,b_{11} & \dots & c_{1}\,a_{1n}+c_{2}\,b_{1n}\\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\
c_{1}\,a_{m1}+c_{2}\,b_{m1} & \dots & c_{1}\,a_{mn}+c_{2}\,b_{mn}
\end{bmatrix}$$

### Products

> [!def] Matrix-Vector Product
> Let there be an $m\times n$ [[2.2 - Matrices#^5bf009]] $\mathbf{A}$ and a [[2.1 - Vector Spaces#^vector]] $\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$. The $i$th entry of $\mathbf{A}\,\mathbf{x}$ is the sum of the products of $x_{j}$ and the [[3.6 - Complex Numbers#^complex-conjugate]] $\bar{a}_{ij}$.
>
> $$
> \begin{bmatrix}
> a_{11} & \dots & a_{1n} \\
> \vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\
> a_{m1} & \dots & a_{mn}
> \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}
> x_{1} \\
> \vdots \\
> x_{n}
> \end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}
> \sum_{i=0}^{n}{\bar{a}_{1i}\,x_{i}} \\
> \vdots \\
> \sum_{i=0}^{n}{\bar{a}_{mi}\,x_{i}}
> \end{bmatrix}
> $$

^matrix-vector-product

> [!def] Matrix Product
> Let there be an $m\times n$ [[#^5bf009]] $\mathbf{A}$ and an $n\times p$ matrix $\mathbf{B}$. The matrix product $\mathbf{C}=\mathbf{A}\,\mathbf{B}$ is an $m\times p$ matrix where column $\mathbf{C}_{j}$ is the [[#^matrix-vector-product]] $\mathbf{A}\,\mathbf{B}_{j}$.
>
> Alternatively, the $ij$th element is the [[2.1 - Vectors#^efaf9e]] of the $i$th [[2.1 - Vectors#^row-vector]] $\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{T}$ of $\mathbf{A}$ and the $j$th [[2.1 - Vectors#^column-vector]] of $\mathbf{B}$.
> $$
> \mathbf{A}\,\mathbf{B}=\begin{bmatrix}
a_{11}b_{11}+\dots a_{1n}b{_{1m}} & \dots & a_{1n}b_{1n}+\dots a_{1n}b{_{1m}} \\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\
a_{m1}b_{m1}+\dots a_{mn}b_{mn} & \dots & a_{mn}b_{mn}+\dots a_{mn}b_{mn}
\end{bmatrix}
> $$

^edd82a

### Powers

> [!def] Matrix Power
> Let there be a square matrix $\mathbf{A}$. The **matrix power** $\mathbf{A}^{n}$ is the the [[#^edd82a]] of $\mathbf{A}$ to itself $n$ times, where $n\in \mathbb{Z}^{+}$.
>
> We define $\mathbf{A}^{0}$ to be the [[2.2 - Matrices#^369c51]].

^matrix-power

> [!def] Matrix Exponential
> The exponential of a matrix $\mathbf{A}$ is defined with the [[6.2 - Taylor Series#^ef8304]] with a [[#^matrix-power]] in each term.
> $$e^{\mathbf{A}}\equiv\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\frac{\mathbf{A}^{n}}{n!}}$$

^matrix-exponential

### Inverse

> [!def] Inverse Matrix
> Given a [[#^5bf009]] $\mathbf{A}$, the [[#^edd82a]] with the **inverse** $\mathbf{A}^{-1}$ must be the [[2.2 - Matrices#^369c51]].
> $$
> \mathbf{A}\,\mathbf{A}^{-1}=\mathbf{I}
> $$

^inverse-matrix

> [!def] Invertible Matrix
> An **invertible** matrix $\mathbf{A}$ has an existing [[#^inverse-matrix]] $\mathbf{A}^{-1}$.

^invertible-matrix

> [!thm] Finding the Inverse
> The [[2.3 - RREF and Systems of Equations#^9a9cd2]] of $\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{A} & \mathbf{I}\end{bmatrix}$ will contain the [[#^inverse-matrix]] $\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{I} & \mathbf{A}^{-1}\end{bmatrix}$.

> [!thm]
> The [[#^inverse-matrix]] of the [[#^edd82a]] $\mathbf{A}\,\mathbf{B}$ is
> $$(\mathbf{A}\,\mathbf{B})^{-1}=\mathbf{B}^{-1}\,\mathbf{A}^{-1}$$

### Transpose

> [!def] Transpose
> Given an $m\times n$ [[#^5bf009]] $\mathbf{A}=[a_{ij}]$, the **transpose** $\mathbf{A}^{T}=[a_{ji}]$ is an $n\times m$ matrix with the rows and columns **swapped**.
>$$\begin{bmatrix}
a_{11} & \dots & a_{1n} \\
a_{21} & \dots & a_{2n} \\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\
a_{m1} & \dots & a_{mn}
\end{bmatrix}^{T}=\begin{bmatrix}
a_{11} & a_{21}& \dots & a_{m1} \\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\
a_{1n} & a_{2n} & \dots & a_{mn}
\end{bmatrix}$$

^653a35

> [!thm] Properties of the Transponse
> Let $\mathbf{A}^{T}, \ \mathbf{B}^{T}$ each be the [[#^653a35]] of $\mathbf{A}, \ \mathbf{B}$.
> 1. $$(\mathbf{A}^{T})^{T}=\mathbf{A}$$
> 2. $$(\mathbf{A}\,\mathbf{B})^{T}=\mathbf{B}^{T}\,\mathbf{A}^{T}$$
> 3. $$(r\,\mathbf{A}+s\,\mathbf{B})^{T}=r\,\mathbf{A}^{T}+s\,\mathbf{B}^{T}$$

> [!thm] Inverse of Transpose
> The [[#^653a35]] of the [[#^inverse-matrix]] is the inverse of the transpose.
>
> $$(\mathbf{A}^{-1})^{T}=(\mathbf{A}^{T})^{-1}$$
