# 2.2 - Matrices

> [!def] Matrix
> An $m\times n$ **matrix** $\mathbf{A}$ is a [[2.5 - Linear Transformations#^f63d2d]] of [[3.6 - Complex Numbers#^8288b7]]  $\mathbb{C}^{n}\to \mathbb{C}^{m}$. We represent $\mathbf{A}=[a_{ij}]$ as a table of numbers with $m$ rows and $n$ columns, where $a_{ij}$ represents the element at row $i$ and column $j$.
>
> $\mathbf{A}$ represents the [[2.5 - Linear Transformations#^e93306]] of Cartesian space to $\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{a}_{1} & \dots & \mathbf{a}_{n}\end{bmatrix}$.
>
> $$\mathbf{A}=\begin{bmatrix}
a_{ij}
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}
a_{11} & \dots & a_{1n} \\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\
a_{m1} & \dots & a_{mn}
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf{a}_{1} & \dots & \mathbf{a}_{n}
\end{bmatrix}$$
>
>
>

## Matrix Operations

> [!def] Equality of Matrices
> A [[#^5bf009]] $\mathbf{A}$ is equal to another matrix $\mathbf{B}$ if every element is equal and $\mathbf{A}, \ \mathbf{B}$ are the same shape.

> [!def] Identity Matrix
> The **identity** matrix $\mathbf{I}$ is an $n\times n$ [[2.2 - Matrices#^5bf009]] of the [[2.2 - Subspaces#^8f5ebd]] vectors in $\mathbb{R}^{n}$.
>$$
>\mathbf{I}=\begin{bmatrix}
1 & … & 0 \\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\
0 & \dots & 1\\
\end{bmatrix}
>$$

> $$\mathbf{A}^{\dagger}\equiv(\bar{\mathbf{A}})^{T}$$

### Linear Combinations

> [!def] Matrix Linear Combinations
> Let $\mathbf{A}=[a_{ij}], \ \mathbf{B}=[b_{ij}]$ each be an $m\times n$ [[#^5bf009]]. A linear combination of $\mathbf{A}, \ \mathbf{B}$ is the [[2.1 - Vectors#^93b383]] of their column vectors.
> $$c_{1}\,\mathbf{A}+c_{2}\,\mathbf{B}=\begin{bmatrix}
c_{1}\,a_{11}+c_{2}\,b_{11} & \dots & c_{1}\,a_{1n}+c_{2}\,b_{1n}\\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\
c_{1}\,a_{m1}+c_{2}\,b_{m1} & \dots & c_{1}\,a_{mn}+c_{2}\,b_{mn}
\end{bmatrix}$$

### Products

> [!def] Matrix-Vector Product
> Let there be an $m\times n$ [[2.2 - Matrices#^5bf009]] $\mathbf{A}$ and a [[2.1 - Vectors#^f36929]] $\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$. The $i$th entry of $\mathbf{A}\,\mathbf{x}$ is the sum of the products of $x_{j}$ and the [[3.6 - Complex Numbers#^29ae56]] $\bar{a}_{ij}$.
>
>$$
>\begin{bmatrix}
a_{11} & \dots & a_{1n} \\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\
a_{m1} & \dots & a_{mn}
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}
x_{1} \\
\vdots \\
x_{n}
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}
\sum_{i=0}^{n}{\bar{a}_{1i}\,x_{i}} \\
\vdots \\
\sum_{i=0}^{n}{\bar{a}_{mi}\,x_{i}}
\end{bmatrix}
>$$

^7f9d75

> [!def] Matrix Product
> Let there be an $m\times n$ [[#^5bf009]] $\mathbf{A}$ and an $n\times p$ matrix $\mathbf{B}$. The matrix product $\mathbf{C}=\mathbf{A}\,\mathbf{B}$ is an $m\times p$ matrix where column $\mathbf{C}_{j}$ is the [[#^7f9d75]] $\mathbf{A}\,\mathbf{B}_{j}$.
>
> Alternatively, the $ij$th element is the [[2.1 - Vectors#^efaf9e]] of the $i$th [[2.1 - Vectors#^c8ab29]] $\bar{\mathbf{w}}^{T}$ of $\mathbf{A}$ and the $j$th [[2.1 - Vectors#^41fef0]] of $\mathbf{B}$.
> $$
> \mathbf{A}\,\mathbf{B}=\begin{bmatrix}
a_{11}b_{11}+\dots a_{1n}b{_{1m}} & \dots & a_{1n}b_{1n}+\dots a_{1n}b{_{1m}} \\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\
a_{m1}b_{m1}+\dots a_{mn}b_{mn} & \dots & a_{mn}b_{mn}+\dots a_{mn}b_{mn}
\end{bmatrix}
> $$

### Powers

> [!def] Matrix Power
> Let there be a square matrix $\mathbf{A}$. The **matrix power** $\mathbf{A}^{n}$ is the the [[#^edd82a]] of $\mathbf{A}$ to itself $n$ times, where $n\in \mathbb{Z}^{+}$.
>
> We define $\mathbf{A}^{0}$ to be the [[2.2 - Matrices#^369c51]].

^de90e2

> [!def] Matrix Exponential
> The exponential of a matrix $\mathbf{A}$ is defined with the [[6.2 - Taylor Series#^ef8304]] with a [[#^de90e2]] in each term.
> $$e^{\mathbf{A}}\equiv\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\frac{\mathbf{A}^{n}}{n!}}$$

### Inverse

> [!def] Inverse Matrix
> Given a [[#^5bf009]] $\mathbf{A}$, the [[#^edd82a]] with the **inverse** $\mathbf{A}^{-1}$ must be the [[2.2 - Matrices#^369c51]].

> \mathbf{A}\,\mathbf{A}^{-1}=\mathbf{I}

> [!def] Invertible Matrix
> An **invertible** matrix $\mathbf{A}$ has an existing [[#^a2f166]] $\mathbf{A}^{-1}$.

### Transpose

> [!def] Transpose
> Given an $m\times n$ [[#^5bf009]] $\mathbf{A}=[a_{ij}]$, the **transpose** $\mathbf{A}^{T}=[a_{ji}]$ is an $n\times m$ matrix with the rows and columns **swapped**.
>$$\begin{bmatrix}
a_{11} & \dots & a_{1n} \\
a_{21} & \dots & a_{2n} \\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\
a_{m1} & \dots & a_{mn}
\end{bmatrix}^{T}=\begin{bmatrix}
a_{11} & a_{21}& \dots & a_{m1} \\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\
a_{1n} & a_{2n} & \dots & a_{mn}
\end{bmatrix}$$

^653a35

> [!thm] Properties of the Transponse
> Let $\mathbf{A}^{T}, \ \mathbf{B}^{T}$ each be the [[#^653a35]] of $\mathbf{A}, \ \mathbf{B}$.
> 1. $$(\mathbf{A}^{T})^{T}=\mathbf{A}$$
> 2. $$(\mathbf{A}\,\mathbf{B})^{T}=\mathbf{B}^{T}\,\mathbf{A}^{T}$$
> 3. $$(r\,\mathbf{A}+s\,\mathbf{B})^{T}=r\,\mathbf{A}^{T}+s\,\mathbf{B}^{T}$$

> [!thm] Inverse of Transpose
> The [[#^653a35]] of the [[#^a2f166]] is the inverse of the transpose.
>
> $$(\mathbf{A}^{-1})^{T}=(\mathbf{A}^{T})^{-1}$$

## Matrix Spaces

> [!def] Rank
> The **rank** of a [[2.2 - Matrices#^5bf009]] $\mathbf{A}$ is equal to the [[2.2 - Subspaces#^09a800]] of the [[2.2 - Matrices#^279020]] or the [[2.2 - Matrices#^661cf7]]. It's also equal to the number of [[2.3 - RREF and Systems of Equations#^4357b7]] columns or rows.
>
> $$rank(\mathbf{A})\equiv dim(col(\mathbf{A}))\equiv dim(row(\mathbf{A}))$$

^0863db

> [!thm] Rank-Nullity Theorem
> For any $m\times n$ matrix $\mathbf{A}$, the [[2.2 - Matrices#^0863db]] plus the [[2.2 - Subspaces#^09a800]] of the [[#^3cb942]] is equal to the number of columns $n$.
> $$rank(\mathbf{A})+dim(null(\mathbf{A}))=n$$

### Row Space

> [!def] Row Space
> The [[2.2 - Subspaces#^d0ac06]] of the *rows* $\{\mathbf{a}_{i} \}$ of an $m\times n$ [[2.2 - Matrices#^5bf009]] $\mathbf{A}$ is the **row space** $row(\mathbf{A})$.
> $$col\left(\begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf{a}_1 \\ \vdots \\\mathbf{a}_m
\end{bmatrix}\right)\equiv span(\{\mathbf{a}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{a}_{n}\})$$

^661cf7

> [!thm] Basis of the Row Space
> The [[2.3 - RREF and Systems of Equations#^4357b7]] rows of $\mathbf{A}$ form a [[2.2 - Subspaces#^ffe859]] for the [[#^661cf7]] $row(\mathbf{A})$.

### Column Space

> [!def] Column Space
> The [[2.2 - Subspaces#^d0ac06]] of the *columns* $\{\mathbf{a}_{j} \}$ of an $m\times n$ [[2.2 - Matrices#^5bf009]] $\mathbf{A}$ is the **column space** $col(\mathbf{A})$.
>
> $$col([\mathbf{a}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{a}_{n}])\equiv span(\{\mathbf{a}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{a}_{n} \})$$
>

### Null Space

> [!def] Null Space
> For an $m\times n$ [[2.2 - Matrices#^5bf009]] $\mathbf{A}$, the **null space** (or **kernel**) $ker(\mathbf{A})$ is the set of all solutions to $\mathbf{A}\,\mathbf{x}=0$.
>
> $$null(\mathbf{A})=\{\mathbf{x}:\mathbf{A}\,\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{0} \}$$
>

^3cb942

> [!thm] Null Space is a Subspace
> The [[#^3cb942]] is a [[2.2 - Subspaces#^420b21]].

^9e9fe8

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^9e9fe8]]
Let there be two vectors $(\mathbf{x}_{1}, \ \mathbf{x}_{2})\in ker(\mathbf{A})$. Therefore, $\mathbf{A}\,\mathbf{x}_{1}=0$, $\mathbf{A}\,\mathbf{x}_{2}=0$.

$$
\begin{align}
A(c_{1}\,\mathbf{x}_{1}+c_{2}\,\mathbf{x}_{2})=c_{1}\,\mathbf{A}\,\mathbf{x}_{1}+c_{2}\,\mathbf{A}\,\mathbf{x}_{2}=\mathbf{0}
\end{align}
$$

Therefore, $(c_{1}\,\mathbf{x}_{1}+c_{2}\,\mathbf{x}_{2})\in ker(\mathbf{A})$, so $ker(\mathbf{A})$ is a subspace.
`\end{proof}`
