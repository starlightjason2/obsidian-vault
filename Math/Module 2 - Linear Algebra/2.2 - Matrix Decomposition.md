# 2.2 - Matrix Decomposition

## Similar Matrices

> [!def] Similar Matrices
> Two $n\times n$ matrices $\mathbf{A}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ are similar ($\mathbf{A}\sim \mathbf{B}$) IFF there exists an [[2.2 - Matrices#^invertible-matrix]] $\mathbf{P}$ such that $\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{P}\,\mathbf{B}\,\mathbf{P}^{-1}$.
> $$(\exists\,\mathbf{P}:\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{P}\,\mathbf{B}\,\mathbf{P}^{-1})\implies \mathbf{A}\sim \mathbf{B}$$

^similar-matrices

> [!thm] Eigenvalues of Similar Matrices
> Two [[#^similar-matrices]] $\mathbf{A}, \  \mathbf{B}$ have the same [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^spectrum]].

> [!def] Eigenbasis Matrix
> A [[2.2 - Matrix Decomposition#^4fddab]] $\mathbf{\hat{A}}$ expressed in the [[2.2 - Subspaces#^orthonormal-basis]] of it's own [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^eigenspace]].
>
> $$
>  \mathbf{\hat{A}}\Ket{a_{n}}=a_{n}\Ket{a_{n}} \text{ where } \Ket{a_{n}} : \Braket{a_{m} |a_{n} }=\delta_{mn}
> $$
>
>

## Diagonalization

> [!def] Diagonal Matrix
> A diagonal matrix is an $n\times n$ [[2.2 - Matrices#^5bf009]] with non-zero values for each diagonal index $ii$ and zeroes elsewhere.
>$$\mathbf{A}=\begin{bmatrix}
\lambda_{11} & \dots & 0 \\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\
0 & \dots & \lambda_{nn}
\end{bmatrix}$$

^35b063

> [!def] Diagonalizable Matrix
 > Let there be a matrix $\mathbf{A}$ and any diagonal matrix $\mathbf{D}$. $\mathbf{A}$ can be **diagonalized** if $\mathbf{A}, \ \mathbf{D}$ are [[#^similar-matrices]].
$$(\exists\,\mathbf{P}:\mathbf{D}=\mathbf{P}\,\mathbf{A}\,\mathbf{P}^{-1})\implies \mathbf{A} \ \text{can be diagonalized}$$

^6089b9

> [!thm] Eigenvectors of Diagonalizable Matrices
> If $\mathbf{A}$ is a [[#^6089b9]], then there exist $n$ linearly independent [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^basic-eigenvector]] $\{\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{n} \}$ each with a unique [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^eiegenvalue]] $\lambda_{i}$.
>$$
>\mathbf{A}\,\mathbf{P}=\begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf{x}_{1} & \dots & \mathbf{x}_{n}
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}
\lambda_{1} & \dots & 0 \\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\
0 & \dots & \lambda_{n}
\end{bmatrix}
>$$

> [!thm] Diagonalizability Test
> A matrix $\mathbf{A}$ is a [[#^6089b9]] if for each eigenvalue, the [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^algebraic-multiplicity]] is equal to the [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^geometric-multiplicity]]. In other words, each eigenvalue must correspond to a unique independent eigenvector.
>
> $$AM_{\lambda_{i}}=GM_{\lambda_{i}}$$
>

> [!thm] Diagonalized Matrix
> Let matrix $\mathbf{A}$ be a [[#^6089b9]], with a [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^spectrum]] $\{\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n} \}$ of $n$ unique eigenvalues.
>
> Let $\mathbf{P}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{x}_{1} & \dots & \mathbf{x}_{n}\end{bmatrix}$ represent the [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^eigenspace]] of $\mathbf{A}$. The matrix $\mathbf{D}$ is a diagonal matrix of eigenvalues, where $\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{P}\,\mathbf{D}\,\mathbf{P}^{-1}$.
> $$\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{P}\,\mathbf{D}\,\mathbf{P}^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf{x}_{1} & \dots & \mathbf{x}_{n}
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}
\lambda_{1} & \dots & 0 \\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\
0 & \dots & \lambda_{n}
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf{x}_{1} & \dots & \mathbf{x}_{n}
\end{bmatrix}^{-1}=\sum_{n}{a_{n}\ket{a_{n}} \bra{a_{n}} }$$

### Properties of Diagonal Matrices

> [!thm] Eigenvalues of a Diagonal Matrix
> Let there be a [[2.2 - Matrix Decomposition#^35b063]] $\mathbf{A}$. Each diagonal value $\lambda_{ii}$ is an [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^eiegenvalue]] with a respective [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^eiegenvector]] of the $i$th [[2.2 - Subspaces#^8f5ebd]] $\mathbf{e}_{i}$.
>$$
>\mathbf{A}\,\mathbf{e}_{i}=\lambda_{ii}\,\mathbf{e}_{i}
>$$

> [!thm] Powers of a Diagonal Matrix
> Given a [[2.2 - Matrix Decomposition#^4fddab]] $\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{P}\mathbf{D}\mathbf{P}^{-1}$, the [[2.2 - Matrices#^matrix-power]] $\mathbf{A}^{x}=\mathbf{P}\,\mathbf{D}^{x}\,\mathbf{P}^{-1}$, where $\mathbf{D}^{x}$ is the diagonal matrix of the exponential of each eigenvalue.
>$$\mathbf{A}^{x}=\mathbf{P}\,\mathbf{D}^{x}\,\mathbf{P}^{-1}=\mathbf{P}\begin{bmatrix}
(\lambda_{1})^{x} & \dots & 0 \\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\
0 & \dots & (\lambda_{n})^{x}
\end{bmatrix}\mathbf{P}^{-1}$$

> [!thm] Exponential of a Diagonal Matrix
> Given a [[2.2 - Matrix Decomposition#^4fddab]] $\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{P}\mathbf{D}\mathbf{P}^{-1}$, the [[2.2 - Matrices#^matrix-exponential]] is
>
>$$e^{\mathbf{A}}=\mathbf{P}\begin{bmatrix}
e^{\lambda_{1}} & \dots & 0 \\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\
0 & \dots & e^{\lambda_{n}}
\end{bmatrix}\,\mathbf{P}^{-1}$$

^a74c8d

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^a74c8d]]
Use [[#^fb35b7]] satisfy $\mathbf{A}^{n}=\mathbf{P}\,\mathbf{D}^{n}\,\mathbf{P}^{-1}$, since the inner $\mathbf{P}^{-1}\,\mathbf{P}$ pairs cancel. Substituting into the [[2.2 - Matrices#^matrix-exponential|Matrix Exponential]] series:

$$
\begin{align}
e^{\mathbf{A}}&= e^{\mathbf{P}\mathbf{D}\mathbf{P}^{-1}}\\
&=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\frac{\mathbf{P}\,\mathbf{D}^{k}\,\mathbf{P}^{-1}}{k!}}
\\ 
&=\mathbf{P}\begin{bmatrix}
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\frac{(\lambda_{1})^{k}}{k!}} & \dots & 0 \\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\
0 & \dots & \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\frac{(\lambda_{n})^{k}}{k!}}
\end{bmatrix}\,\mathbf{P}^{-1}
\end{align}
$$
`\end{proof}`
