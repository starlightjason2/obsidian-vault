# 2.2 - Matrix Decomposition

> [!def] Similar Matrices
> Two $n\times n$ matrices $\mathbf{A}$ and $\mathbf{B}$ are similar ($\mathbf{A}\sim \mathbf{B}$) IFF there exists an [[2.2 - Matrices#^9daed1]] $\mathbf{P}$ such that $\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{P}\,\mathbf{B}\,\mathbf{P}^{-1}$.
> $$(\exists\,\mathbf{P}:\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{P}\,\mathbf{B}\,\mathbf{P}^{-1})\implies \mathbf{A}\sim \mathbf{B}$$

^6e655e

> [!thm] Eigenvalues of Similar Matrices
> Two [[#^6e655e]] $\mathbf{A}, \  \mathbf{B}$ have the same [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^b87cb6]].

> [!def] Eigenbasis Matrix
> A [[2.2 - Matrix Decomposition#^4fddab]] $\mathbf{\hat{A}}$ expressed in the [[2.2 - Subspaces#^c6eb2b]] of it's own [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^8c43f5]]. 
>
> $$
>  \mathbf{\hat{A}}\Ket{a_{n}}=a_{n}\Ket{a_{n}} \text{ where } \Ket{a_{n}} : \Braket{a_{m} |a_{n} }=\delta_{mn}
> $$
>
>

## Diagonalization

> [!def] Diagonalizable Matrix
 > Let there be a matrix $\mathbf{A}$ and any diagonal matrix $\mathbf{D}$. $\mathbf{A}$ can be **diagonalized** if $\mathbf{A}, \ \mathbf{D}$ are [[#^6e655e]].
$$(\exists\,\mathbf{P}:\mathbf{D}=\mathbf{P}\,\mathbf{A}\,\mathbf{P}^{-1})\implies \mathbf{A} \ \text{can be diagonalized}$$

^6089b9

> [!thm]
> If $\mathbf{A}$ is a [[#^6089b9]], then there exist $n$ linearly independent [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^90b851]] $\{\mathbf{x}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{x}_{n} \}$ each with a unique [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^888169]] $\lambda_{i}$.
>$$
>\mathbf{A}\,\mathbf{P}=\begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf{x}_{1} & \dots & \mathbf{x}_{n}
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}
\lambda_{1} & \dots & 0 \\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\
0 & \dots & \lambda_{n}
\end{bmatrix}
>$$

> [!thm] Diagonalizability Test
> A matrix $\mathbf{A}$ is a [[#^6089b9]] if for each eigenvalue, the [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^dbcea1]] is equal to the [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^323406]]. In other words, each eigenvalue must correspond to a unique independent eigenvector.
>
> $$AM_{\lambda_{i}}=GM_{\lambda_{i}}$$
>

> [!thm] Diagonalized Matrix
> Let matrix $\mathbf{A}$ be a [[#^6089b9]], with a [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^b87cb6]] $\{\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n} \}$ of $n$ unique eigenvalues.
>
> Let $\mathbf{P}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{x}_{1} & \dots & \mathbf{x}_{n}\end{bmatrix}$ represent the [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^8c43f5]] of $\mathbf{A}$. The matrix $\mathbf{D}$ is a diagonal matrix of eigenvalues, where $\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{P}\,\mathbf{D}\,\mathbf{P}^{-1}$.
> $$\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{P}\,\mathbf{D}\,\mathbf{P}^{-1}=\begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf{x}_{1} & \dots & \mathbf{x}_{n}
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}
\lambda_{1} & \dots & 0 \\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\
0 & \dots & \lambda_{n}
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}
\mathbf{x}_{1} & \dots & \mathbf{x}_{n}
\end{bmatrix}^{-1}=\sum_{n}{a_{n}\ket{a_{n}} \bra{a_{n}} }$$
