# 2.2 - Special Matrices

## Diagonal Matrices

> [!def] Diagonal Matrix
> A diagonal matrix is an $n\times n$ [[2.2 - Matrices#^5bf009]] with non-zero values for each diagonal index $ii$ and zeroes elsewhere.
>$$\mathbf{A}=\begin{bmatrix}
\lambda_{11} & \dots & 0 \\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\
0 & \dots & \lambda_{nn}
\end{bmatrix}$$

^35b063

### Properties of Diagonal Matrices

> [!thm] Eigenvalues of a Diagonal Matrix
> Let there be a [[2.2 - Special Matrices#^35b063]] $\mathbf{A}$. Each diagonal value $\lambda_{ii}$ is an [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^888169]] with a respective [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^b325d4]] of the $i$th [[2.2 - Subspaces#^8f5ebd]] $\mathbf{e}_{i}$.
>$$
>\mathbf{A}\,\mathbf{e}_{i}=\lambda_{ii}\,\mathbf{e}_{i}
>$$

> [!thm] Powers of a Diagonal Matrix
> Given a [[2.2 - Matrix Decomposition#^4fddab]] $\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{P}\mathbf{D}\mathbf{P}^{-1}$, the [[2.2 - Matrices#^de90e2]] $\mathbf{A}^{x}=\mathbf{P}\,\mathbf{D}^{x}\,\mathbf{P}^{-1}$, where $\mathbf{D}^{x}$ is the diagonal matrix of the exponential of each eigenvalue.
>$$\mathbf{A}^{x}=\mathbf{P}\,\mathbf{D}^{x}\,\mathbf{P}^{-1}=\mathbf{P}\begin{bmatrix}
(\lambda_{1})^{x} & \dots & 0 \\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\
0 & \dots & (\lambda_{n})^{x}
\end{bmatrix}\mathbf{P}^{-1}$$

> [!thm] Exponential of a Diagonal Matrix
> Given a [[2.2 - Matrix Decomposition#^4fddab]] $\mathbf{A}=\mathbf{P}\mathbf{D}\mathbf{P}^{-1}$, the [[2.2 - Matrices#^8805f7]] is
>
>$$e^{\mathbf{A}}=\mathbf{P}\begin{bmatrix}
e^{\lambda_{1}} & \dots & 0 \\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\
0 & \dots & e^{\lambda_{n}}
\end{bmatrix}\,\mathbf{P}^{-1}$$

^a74c8d

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^a74c8d]]
Use [[#^fb35b7]] for the [[6.2 - Taylor Series#^ef8304]].

$$
\begin{align}
e^{\mathbf{A}}&= e^{\mathbf{P}\mathbf{D}\mathbf{P}^{-1}}\\
&=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\frac{\mathbf{P}\,\mathbf{D}^{k}\,\mathbf{P}^{-1}}{k!}}
\\ 
&=\mathbf{P}\begin{bmatrix}
\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\frac{(\lambda_{1})^{k}}{k!}} & \dots & 0 \\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\
0 & \dots & \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}{\frac{(\lambda_{n})^{k}}{k!}}
\end{bmatrix}\,\mathbf{P}^{-1}
\end{align}
$$
`\end{proof}`

## Hermitian Matrix

> [!def] Hermitian Matrix
> Any [[2.2 - Matrices#^5bf009]] where $\mathbf{A}$ is equal to the [[2.2 - Matrices#^673394]] $\mathbf{A}^{\dagger}$.
> $$\mathbf{A}\text{ is Hermitian}\iff \mathbf{A}=\mathbf{A}^{\dagger}$$

^6146ae

> [!thm] Eigenvalues of Hermitian are Real
> Each [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^888169]] of a [[#^6146ae]] is a real number.
> $$\hat{A}\ket{a} =a\ket{a}\implies a\in \mathbb{R}$$

^46739a

Let $a$ be an [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^888169]], and $\ket{a}$ be an [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^b325d4]]. Take the [[2.2 - Matrices#^673394]] of both sides of the eigenvalue/eigenvector equation.

$$
\begin{align} \\
\hat{A}\ket{a}&=a\ket{a} \\
\bra{\bar{a}} \hat{A}^{\dagger} &=a\bra{\bar{a}}
\end{align}
$$

By definition, if $\hat{A}$ is a Hermitian, then $\hat{A}=\hat{A}^{\dagger}$, so

$$
\begin{align} \\
\bra{\bar{a}} \hat{A} &=a\bra{\bar{a}}
\end{align}
$$

$$
\begin{align}
\hat{A}\ket{a}&=a\ket{a} \\
\bra{a}\hat{A}\ket{a}&=\bra{a}a\ket{a}   \\
\bra{a}\hat{A}^{\dagger}\ket{a}&=a\braket{a|a}
\end{align}
$$

> [!thm] Eigenvectors of Hermitian are Orthogonal
> The set of each [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^b325d4]] of a [[#^6146ae]] forms a complete [[2.7 - Orthogonality#^2f7935]].
> $$\hat{A}\ket{a} =a\ket{a}\implies \{\ket{a} \}\text{ is a complete orthogonal set}$$

^fa32aa

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^fa32aa]]
We know that [[#^46739a]], and so $\bar{a}=a$. Taking the [[2.2 - Matrices#^673394]] of both sides of the eigenvalue equation,

$$
\begin{align}
\hat{A}\ket{a_{1}}=a_{1}\ket{a_{1}}\implies \bra{a_{1}} \hat{A}=\bra{a_{1}}a_{1} \\
\hat{A}\ket{a_{2}}=a_{2}\ket{a_{2}}\implies \bra{a_{2}} \hat{A}=\bra{a_{2}}a_{2}
\end{align}
$$

$$
\begin{align}
\bra{a_{1}}\hat{A}\ket{a_{2}}&=\bra{a_{1}}a_{2}\ket{a_{2}}=a_{1} \braket{a_{1}|a_{2}}\\
\bra{a_{2}}\hat{A}\ket{a_{1}}&=\bra{a_{2}}a_{1}\ket{a_{1}}=a_{2} \braket{a_{2}|a_{1}}=a_{2} \braket{a_{1}|a_{2}}
\end{align}
$$

Furthermore, $a_{1}\neq a_{2}\implies \braket{a_{1}|a_{2}}=0$

`\end{proof}`

## Commuting Matrices

> [!def] Commutator
> Let $A, \ \hat{B}$ each be a [[2.7 - Function Spaces#^3fa82b]]. The **commutator** $[\hat{A},\hat{B}]$ measures the difference between applying each in different order. 
> $$[\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}]\equiv\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}-\mathbf{B}\mathbf{A}$$

^cb348c

> [!def] Commuting Matrices
> **Commuting matrices** $\mathbf{A}, \ \mathbf{B}$ have a [[#^cb348c]] of $[\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}]=0$.

^17b8a7

> [!thm] Commuting Matrices share Eigenvectors
> Each [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^90b851]] with a Non-[[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^98842b]] of [[#^17b8a7]] $\mathbf{A}, \ \mathbf{B}$ is the same between $\mathbf{A}$ and $\mathbf{B}$.
> $$[\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}]=0\implies \begin{align}
\mathbf{A}\,\mathbf{x}&=\alpha\,\mathbf{x} \\
\mathbf{B}\,\mathbf{x}&=\beta\,\mathbf{x}
\end{align} \ \ \text{ for unique } \alpha, \ \beta$$

^f5881e

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^f5881e]]
$$
\begin{align}
\mathbf{M}\,\mathbf{v}&=\lambda\,\mathbf{v} \\
\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{N}\,\mathbf{v})&=\mathbf{N}(\,\mathbf{M}\,\mathbf{v} )\\
\mathbf{M}\,\mathbf{N}\,\mathbf{v}&=\lambda\,(\mathbf{N}\,\mathbf{v})
\end{align}
$$
Therefore, $\mathbf{N}\,\mathbf{v}$ is an [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^b325d4]] of $\mathbf{M}$ with the same *unique* eigenvalue $\lambda$. Therefore, $\mathbf{N}\,\mathbf{v}\propto \mathbf{v}$, so $\mathbf{v}$ is also an eigenvector of $\mathbf{N}$.

`\end{proof}`
