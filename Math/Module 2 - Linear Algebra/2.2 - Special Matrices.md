# 2.2 - Special Matrices
## Hermitian Matrix

> [!def] Hermitian Matrix
> Any [[2.2 - Matrices#^5bf009]] where $\mathbf{A}$ is equal to the [[2.2 - Matrices#^hermitian-adjoint]] $\mathbf{A}^{\dagger}$.
> $$\mathbf{A}\text{ is Hermitian}\iff \mathbf{A}=\mathbf{A}^{\dagger}$$

^hermitian-matrix

> [!thm] Eigenvalues of Hermitian are Real
> Each [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^eiegenvalue]] of a [[#^hermitian-matrix]] is a real number.
> $$\hat{A}\ket{a} =a\ket{a}\implies a\in \mathbb{R}$$

^eigenvalues-of-hermitian-are-real

Let $a$ be an [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^eiegenvalue]], and $\ket{a}$ be an [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^eiegenvector]]. Take the [[2.2 - Matrices#^hermitian-adjoint]] of both sides of the eigenvalue/eigenvector equation.

$$
\begin{align}
\hat{A}\ket{a}&=a\ket{a} \\
\bra{\bar{a}} \hat{A}^{\dagger} &=a\bra{\bar{a}}
\end{align}
$$

By definition, if $\hat{A}$ is a Hermitian, then $\hat{A}=\hat{A}^{\dagger}$, so

$$
\begin{align}
\bra{\bar{a}} \hat{A} &=a\bra{\bar{a}}
\end{align}
$$

$$
\begin{align}
\hat{A}\ket{a}&=a\ket{a} \\
\bra{a}\hat{A}\ket{a}&=\bra{a}a\ket{a}   \\
\bra{a}\hat{A}^{\dagger}\ket{a}&=a\braket{a|a}
\end{align}
$$

> [!thm] Eigenvectors of Hermitian are Orthogonal
> The set of each [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^eiegenvector]] of a [[#^hermitian-matrix]] forms a complete [[2.1 - Vectors#^orthogonal-vectors]].
> $\hat{A}\ket{a} =a\ket{a}\implies \{\ket{a} \}\text{ is a complete orthogonal set}$

^eigenvectors-of-hermitian-are-orthogonal

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^eigenvectors-of-hermitian-are-orthogonal]]
We know that [[#^eigenvalues-of-hermitian-are-real]], and so $\bar{a}=a$. Taking the [[2.2 - Matrices#^hermitian-adjoint]] of both sides of the eigenvalue equation,

$$
\begin{align}
\hat{A}\ket{a_{1}}=a_{1}\ket{a_{1}}\implies \bra{a_{1}} \hat{A}=\bra{a_{1}}a_{1} \\
\hat{A}\ket{a_{2}}=a_{2}\ket{a_{2}}\implies \bra{a_{2}} \hat{A}=\bra{a_{2}}a_{2}
\end{align}
$$

$$
\begin{align}
\bra{a_{1}}\hat{A}\ket{a_{2}}&=\bra{a_{1}}a_{2}\ket{a_{2}}=a_{1} \braket{a_{1}|a_{2}}\\
\bra{a_{2}}\hat{A}\ket{a_{1}}&=\bra{a_{2}}a_{1}\ket{a_{1}}=a_{2} \braket{a_{2}|a_{1}}=a_{2} \braket{a_{1}|a_{2}}
\end{align}
$$

Furthermore, $a_{1}\neq a_{2}\implies \braket{a_{1}|a_{2}}=0$

`\end{proof}`

## Projection Matrix

> [!def] Projection Matrix
> An [[2.2 - Matrices#^5bf009]] $\mathbf{P}_{v}$ that gives the same result when acting on any vector twice in succession as when it acts just once.
> $$(\mathbf{P})^{2}\equiv\mathbf{P}$$
>

^projection-matrix

## Commuting Matrices

> [!def] Commutator
> Let $A, \ \hat{B}$ each be a [[2.7 - Function Spaces#^linear-operator]]. The **commutator** $[\hat{A},\hat{B}]$ measures the difference between applying each in different order.
> $$[\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}]\equiv\mathbf{A}\mathbf{B}-\mathbf{B}\mathbf{A}$$

^commutator

> [!def] Commuting Matrices
> **Commuting matrices** $\mathbf{A}, \ \mathbf{B}$ have a [[#^commutator]] of $[\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}]=0$.

^commuting-matrices

> [!thm] Commuting Matrices share Eigenvectors
> Each [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^basic-eigenvector]] with a Non-[[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^degenerate-eigenvalue]] of [[#^commuting-matrices]] $\mathbf{A}, \ \mathbf{B}$ is the same between $\mathbf{A}$ and $\mathbf{B}$.
> $$[\mathbf{A},\mathbf{B}]=0\implies \begin{align}
\mathbf{A}\,\mathbf{x}&=\alpha\,\mathbf{x} \\
\mathbf{B}\,\mathbf{x}&=\beta\,\mathbf{x}
\end{align} \ \ \text{ for unique } \alpha, \ \beta$$

^f5881e

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^f5881e]]
$$
\begin{align}
\mathbf{M}\,\mathbf{v}&=\lambda\,\mathbf{v} \\
\mathbf{M}(\mathbf{N}\,\mathbf{v})&=\mathbf{N}(\,\mathbf{M}\,\mathbf{v} )\\
\mathbf{M}\,\mathbf{N}\,\mathbf{v}&=\lambda\,(\mathbf{N}\,\mathbf{v})
\end{align}
$$
Therefore, $\mathbf{N}\,\mathbf{v}$ is an [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^eiegenvector]] of $\mathbf{M}$ with the same *unique* eigenvalue $\lambda$. Therefore, $\mathbf{N}\,\mathbf{v}\propto \mathbf{v}$, so $\mathbf{v}$ is also an eigenvector of $\mathbf{N}$.

`\end{proof}`
