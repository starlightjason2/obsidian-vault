# 2.3 - RREF and Systems of Equations

> [!def] Linear System of Equations
> A linear system of equations for independent variables $x_{1},x_{2},\dots$ takes the form
> $$\begin{align}
a_{11}\,x_{1}+a_{12}\,x_{2}+\dots+a_{1n}\,x_{n}&=b_{1} \\
a_{21}\,x_{1}+a_{22}\,x_{2}+\dots+a_{2n}\,x_{n}&=b_{2} \\
&\dots \\
a_{m1}\,x_{1}+a_{m2}\,x_{2}+\dots+a_{mn}\,x_{n}&=b_{m}
\end{align}$$
>

^b09447

> [!def] Homogeneous System of Equations
> A [[#^b09447]] for variables $x_{1},x_{2},\dots$ is **homogeneous** if $b_{1},\dots,b_{n}=0$. This represents lines, planes, etc. passing through the origin.
>
> Such systems always have a **trivial solution** $\mathbf{x}=0$.
>
>

## Augmented Matrices

We can represent linear equations with matrices.

> [!def] Augmented Matrix
> The **augmented** [[2.2 - Matrices#^5bf009]] of a [[#^b09447]] contains all of its **coefficients** $\mathbf{A}$ on the left and **constants** $\mathbf{b}$ on the right.
>$$\begin{bmatrix}
a_{11}&\dots&a_{1n} & b_{1}\\
\vdots&\vdots &\vdots &\vdots\\
a_{m1}&\dots&a_{mn} & b_{m}\\
\end{bmatrix}$$
Systems with **equivalent** augmented matrices have the same solutions.

^f1e4ce

> [!def] Inconsistent Matrix
> An [[#^f1e4ce]] containing a row of the form $\begin{bmatrix}0 & \dots & b\end{bmatrix}$, with all zeroes in between, is **inconsistent**. It represents a system with the false equation $0x_{1}+\dots+0x_{n}=b$.

## RREF

### Elementary Operations

Elementary operations are necessary to reduce systems of equations into a solved form.

> [!def] Elementary Row Operations
> Applying the following operations to a [[2.2 - Matrices#^5bf009]] will produce an **equivalent** matrix within it's [[2.2 - Matrices#^661cf7]].
>
> 1. **Swapping** two rows.
> 2. **Multiply** a row by a **non-zero scalar**.
> 3. **Replace** a row by any **multiple** of another row.

^a36ef5

> [!def] Elementary Matrix
> Applying a [[2.2 - Matrices#^edd82a]] on the left will induce a combination of [[#^a36ef5]].
>
> To find the elementary matrix $\mathbf{E}$ for any arbitrary sequence of operations, apply the operations to the [[2.2 - Matrices#^369c51]], and the resulting matrix will be $\mathbf{E}$.

^ac7297

> [!thm] Inverse of Elementary Matrix
> The [[2.2 - Matrices#^a2f166]] of an [[#^ac7297]] is also an elementary matrix.

### Row Echelon Forms

> [!def] Row Echelon Form
> An [[2.2 - Matrices#^5bf009]] is in **reduced row form** if
> 1. Any *non-zero* row is *above* any *zero* rows.
> 2. Each *leading entry* in a row is $1$.
> 3. Each *leading entry* is to the ***right*** of the leading entry in the ***above*** row.
> 
>$$
>\begin{bmatrix}
1 & a_{12} & a_{13} & \dots \\
0 & 1 & a_{23} & \dots \\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\
\end{bmatrix}
>$$
>
>Note that a **leading entry** is the first non-zero number in a row.

^5450e4

> [!def] RREF
> If a matrix is in [[#^5450e4]] and all entries *above and below* a leading entry are $0$, then the matrix is in **reduced-row echelon form (RREF)**.
>$$\begin{bmatrix}
1 & \dots & 0& b_{1}\\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\
0 & \dots & 1 & b_{m}
\end{bmatrix}$$

^9a9cd2

> [!def] Pivot
> Let there be a matrix $\mathbf{A}$ in [[#^9a9cd2]]. The row and column index of each leading $1$ is a **pivot position**. The rows with a leading $1$ are **pivot rows**, and the columns with a leading $1$ are **pivot columns**.

^4357b7

> [!thm] Rank of an Augmented Matrix
> The [[2.2 - Matrices#^0863db]] of an [[#^f1e4ce]] is its number of [[#^4357b7]] columns.

^79362e

## Gaussian Elimination

Notice that in reduced-row echelon form, we can easily see that $\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}$. To solve **any** system of equations, we simply transform matrices into this form, and the solution is evident.

> [!thm] Gaussian Elimination
> To reduce an arbitrary matrix, use [[2.3 - RREF and Systems of Equations#^a36ef5]] in the following algorithm:
>
> 1. Find the first nonzero column from the left, which is the first [[#^4357b7]] column. Switch rows if necessary to place a nonzero number in the first pivot position.
> 
> 2. Use row operations to **zero** the entries below the first pivot position.
> 
> 3. Repeat **steps 1 and 2** with the remaining rows until there are no more rows to modify.
> 
> 4. **Divide** each nonzero row by the **leading entry**, so that each leading entry becomes $1$.
>  
The matrix will then be in [[#^5450e4]]. To convert into [[#^9a9cd2]], continue with the following step.
>
> 5.  Use row operations from right to left to **zero** the entries **above the pivot positions**.

^14451f

> [!thm] Number of Solutions
> Consider the system given by an $m\times n$ [[#^f1e4ce]] $\mathbf{A}\,\mathbf{x}=\mathbf{b}$. Let the [[2.2 - Matrices#^0863db]] of $\mathbf{A}$ be $r$. Then,
> 1. If $n>r$ or $\mathbf{A}$ *is* an [[#^5fd200]], then are **no solutions**.
> 2. If $n=r$, then $RREF(\mathbf{A})=\mathbf{I}$, and there is **one unique solution**.
> 3. If $r<n$, then there are **infinite solutions**.

### Types of Variables

The columns of an RREF matrix correspond to two types of variables.

> [!def] Basic Variable
> Let there be a matrix in [[#^9a9cd2]]. Each [[#^4357b7]] column corresponds to a **basic variable**.

> [!def] Free Variable
> Let there be a matrix in [[#^9a9cd2]]. Each non-[[#^4357b7]] column corresponds to a **free variable**.
