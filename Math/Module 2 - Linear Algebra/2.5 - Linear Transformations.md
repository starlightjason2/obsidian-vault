# 2.5 - Linear Transformations

> [!def] Linearity
> **Linearity** for a function $f(\mathbf{x})$ means that for $(\mathbf{x,}\mathbf{y})\in \mathbb{R}^{n}$ and $(k,p)\in \mathbb{R}$,
>
> $$f(k\,\mathbf{x}+p\,\mathbf{y})=k\,f(\mathbf{x})+p\,f(\mathbf{y})$$

^febc14

> [!def] Linear Transformation
> A function $T:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to \mathbb{R}^{m}$ is a **linear transformation** if it obeys [[#^febc14]].
>
> $$T(k\,\mathbf{x}+p\,\mathbf{y})=k\,T(\mathbf{x})+p\,T(\mathbf{y})$$

^f63d2d

> [!def] Isomorphism
> An **isomorphism** is a [[#^f63d2d]] that is **one-to-one** and **onto**.
>

^a5cca1

> [!thm] Properties of Linear Transformations.
> Let there be a [[#^f63d2d]] $T:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to \mathbb{R}^{m}$.
> 1. $T$ preserves the zero vector. $$T(0\,\mathbf{x})=0\,T(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{0}$$
> 2. $T$ preserves the negative of a vector. $$T(-\mathbf{x})=-T(\mathbf{x})$$
> 3. $T$ preserves any [[2.1 - Vectors#^93b383]]. $$T(c_{1}\,\mathbf{x}_{1}+\dots+c_{k}\,\mathbf{x}_{k})=c_{1}\,T(\mathbf{x}_{1})+\dots+c_{k}\,T(\mathbf{x}_{k})$$
>

> [!def] Inverse Transformation
> Let there be a [[#^f63d2d]] $T:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to \mathbb{R}^{m}$. Then the **inverse** transformation is $T^{-1}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to \mathbb{R}^{n}$ such that
>
> $$T(\mathbf{x})\circ T^{-1}(\mathbf{x})=T^{-1}(\mathbf{x})\circ T(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{x}$$

^585247

## Matrices

> [!thm] Matrix Representation
> Let there be an [[#^a5cca1]] $T:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to \mathbb{R}^{n}$ transforming the [[2.2 - Subspaces#^8f5ebd]] into a new [[2.2 - Subspaces#^ffe859]] $B=\{\mathbf{u}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{u}_{n} \}$.
>
> Then the [[2.2 - Matrices#^5bf009]] $\mathbf{A}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{u}_{1} & \dots & \mathbf{u}_{m}\end{bmatrix}$ **induces** $T$.
>
> $$\mathbf{A}\,\mathbf{x}=T(x)$$

^e93306

> [!thm] Inverse Transformation as an Inverse Matrix
> The [[#^585247]] $T:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to \mathbb{R}^{n}$ is induced by the [[2.2 - Matrices#^a2f166]] $\mathbf{A}^{-1}$.

### Chaining Transformations

> [!def] Composition of Linear Transformations
> Let $T:\mathbb{R}^{k}\to \mathbb{R}^{n}$ and $S:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to \mathbb{R}^{m}$ each be a [[#^f63d2d]]. The **composition** is $S\circ T:\mathbb{R}^{k}\to \mathbb{R}^{m}$.
>
> If $T, \ S$ is induced by $\mathbf{A}, \ \mathbf{B}$, then $\forall\,\mathbf{x}\in \mathbb{R}^{k}$,
>
> $$(S\circ T)(\mathbf{x})=S(T(\mathbf{x}))=\mathbf{B}\,\mathbf{A}\,\mathbf{x}$$
>

> [!thm] Basis Transform as a Matrix
> Let $T$ transform a basis $B_{1}$ to a new basis $B_{2}$. Let $\mathbf{A}$ be the [[#^e93306]] of $T$ and let $\mathbf{B}_{1}, \ \mathbf{B}_{2}$ represent each basis as a matrix. Then we can write,
>
> $$\mathbf{A}\,\mathbf{B}_{1}=\mathbf{B}_{2}$$

## Spacial Transformations

### Rotations

> [!thm] 2D Rotation Matrix
> A rotation [[2.2 - Matrices#^5bf009]] $\mathbf{R}_{\theta}\in \mathbb{R}^{2}$ induces a [[#^f63d2d]] rotating 2D space by angle $\theta$ around the $x$ axis.
>$$\mathbf{R}=\begin{bmatrix}
\cos\theta & -\sin\theta \\
\sin\theta & \cos\theta
\end{bmatrix}$$

^29256b

> [!thm] 3D Rotation Matrix
>
 > A rotation [[2.2 - Matrices#^5bf009]] $\mathbf{R}_{a,\theta}\in \mathbb{R}^{2}$ induces a [[#^f63d2d]] rotating $\mathbb{R}^{3}$ space by angle $\theta$ around axis $a$.
 >
> The basis for $a$ is unchanged from its perspective, and the other bases transform with the [[#^29256b]].
>$$\begin{align}
\mathbf{R}_{x,\theta}&=\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & \cos\theta & -\sin\theta \\
0 & \sin\theta & \cos\theta
\end{bmatrix} \\ \\
>
\mathbf{R}_{y,\theta}&=\begin{bmatrix}
\cos\theta & 0 & -\sin\theta \\
0 & 1 & 0 \\
 \sin\theta &0 & \cos\theta
\end{bmatrix} \\ \\
>
\mathbf{R}_{z,\theta}&=\begin{bmatrix}
\cos\theta & -\sin\theta & 0\\
\sin\theta & \cos\theta & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 1
\end{bmatrix}
\end{align}$$

^c2b270

> [!thm] Eigenvalues of Rotational Matrices
> A [[#^c2b270]] for angle $\theta$ around the $x$, $y$, or $z$ axis has the following [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^b87cb6]] of eigenvalues.
> $$\lambda\in\{1, \ e^{-i\theta}, \ e^{i\theta} \}$$

^a36d5e

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^a36d5e]]

$$
\begin{align}
\det(\lambda\,\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{R}_{z})&=\begin{vmatrix}
\lambda-\cos\theta & -\sin\theta & 0\\
\sin\theta & \lambda-\cos\theta & 0 \\
0 & 0 & \lambda-1
\end{vmatrix} \\
&=(\lambda-\cos\theta)(\lambda-\cos\theta)(\lambda-1)-(-\sin\theta)(\sin\theta)(\lambda-1) \\
&=(\lambda-1)\big((\lambda-\cos\theta)^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta\big) =(\lambda-1)(\lambda^{2}-2\lambda\cos\theta+\cos^{2}\theta+\sin^{2}\theta) \\
&=(\lambda-1)(\lambda^{2}-2\lambda\cos\theta+1)=0\
\end{align}
$$

So $\lambda_{1}=1$, which is the only real eigenvalue. For the other two,

$$
\begin{align}
\lambda^{2}-2\lambda\cos\theta+1=0\implies
\lambda&=\frac{1}{2}\Big({2\cos\theta\pm \sqrt{4\cos^{2}\theta-4}}\,\Big) \\
&={\cos\theta\pm \sqrt{\cos^{2}\theta-1}}=\cos\theta\pm \sqrt{-(\sin^{2}\theta)} \\
&=\cos\theta\pm i\sin\theta
\end{align}
$$

Using [[3.6 - Complex Numbers#^88e10c]], $\lambda_{1}=1, \ \lambda_{2}=e^{i\theta}, \ \lambda_{3}=e^{=i\theta}$. The other rotation matrices have the same eigenvalues by symmetry.
`\end{proof}`

> [!thm] Eigenvectors of Rotation Matrix
> A [[#^c2b270]] for angle $\theta$ around the $x$, $y$, or $z$ axis each following [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^b325d4]] $\mathbf{x}_{}$.
>
> $$\{\mathbf{x}_{1}, \,\mathbf{x}_{2}, \,\mathbf{x}_{3} \}=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 0 \\ 1\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}-i \\ 1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}i \\ 1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix} \right\}$$
>

^71993c

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^71993c]]
We know the [[#^a36d5e]] are $\lambda_{1}=1, \ \lambda_{2}= e^{-i\theta}, \ \lambda_{3}=e^{i\theta}$.

$$
\begin{align}
\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{I}=0&\implies \begin{bmatrix}
\cos\theta-1 & -\sin\theta & 0\\
\sin\theta & \cos\theta-1 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 0
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}
x_{1} \\
x_{2} \\
x_{3}
\end{bmatrix}=0 \implies\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 0 & 0\\
0 & 1 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 0
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}
x_{1} \\
x_{2} \\
x_{3}
\end{bmatrix}=0
\end{align}
$$
$$
\begin{align}
x_{1}&=0 \\
x_{2}&=0 \\
x_{3}&=t
\end{align} \implies \mathbf{x}_{1}=t\begin{bmatrix}
0 \\
0 \\
1
\end{bmatrix} 
$$

$$
\begin{align}
\mathbf{R}-e^{i\theta}\,\mathbf{I}=0&\implies \begin{bmatrix}
\cos\theta-e^{i\theta} & -\sin\theta & 0 \\
\sin\theta & \cos\theta-e^{i\theta} & 0  \\
0 & 0 & 0 
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}
x_{1} \\
x_{2} \\
x_{3}
\end{bmatrix}=0 \implies\begin{bmatrix}
1 & i & 0\\
0 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 0
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}
x_{1} \\
x_{2} \\
x_{3}
\end{bmatrix}=0 \\
\end{align}
$$
$$
\begin{align}
x_{1}+i\,x_{2}=0 \\
x_{2}=t \\
x_{3}=s
\end{align}\implies \mathbf{x}_{2}=t\begin{bmatrix}
-i \\
1 \\
0
\end{bmatrix}+s\begin{bmatrix}
0 \\
0 \\
1
\end{bmatrix}
$$

$$
\begin{align}
\mathbf{R}-e^{-i\theta}\,\mathbf{I}=0&\implies \begin{bmatrix}
\cos\theta-e^{-i\theta} & -\sin\theta & 0 \\
\sin\theta & \cos\theta-e^{-i\theta} & 0  \\
0 & 0 & 0 
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}
x_{1} \\
x_{2} \\
x_{3}
\end{bmatrix}=0 \ \implies \begin{bmatrix}
1 & -i & 0\\
0 & 0 & 0  \\
0 & 0 & 0  \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}
x_{1} \\
x_{2} \\
x_{3}
\end{bmatrix}=0 \\
\end{align}
$$
$$
\begin{align}
x_{1}-i\,x_{2}=0 \\
x_{2}=t \\
x_{3}=s
\end{align}\implies \mathbf{x}_{3}=t\begin{bmatrix}
i \\
1 \\
0
\end{bmatrix}+s\begin{bmatrix}
0 \\
0 \\
1
\end{bmatrix}
$$

$$\{\mathbf{x}_{1}, \,\mathbf{x}_{2}, \,\mathbf{x}_{3} \}=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}0 \\ 0 \\ 1\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}-i \\ 1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix},\begin{bmatrix}i \\ 1 \\ 0\end{bmatrix} \right\}$$

`\end{proof}`

### Reflection

> [!thm] 2D Reflection Matrix
> A reflection [[2.2 - Matrices#^5bf009]] $\mathbf{Q}_{\theta}\in \mathbb{R}^{2}$ induces a [[#^f63d2d]] reflecting $\mathbb{R}^{2}$ over a line at angle $\theta$ from the $x$ axis.
> $$\mathbf{Q}_{\theta}=\begin{bmatrix}
\cos 2\theta & \sin 2\theta \\
\sin 2\theta & -\cos 2\theta
\end{bmatrix}$$

^q2d

> [!thm] 3D Reflection Matrix
> A reflection [[2.2 - Matrices#^5bf009]] $\mathbf{Q}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{n}}}\in \mathbb{R}^{3}$ induces a [[#^f63d2d]] reflecting $\mathbb{R}^{3}$ across the plane with unit normal $\hat{\boldsymbol{n}}=(n_{1},n_{2},n_{3})$.
>
> The component along $\hat{\boldsymbol{n}}$ is negated, and the components in the plane are unchanged. Using the [[#^b64e03]] $\mathbf{P}_{\hat{n}}=\hat{\boldsymbol{n}}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{n}}^{T}$,
> $$\mathbf{Q}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{n}}}=\mathbf{I}-2\,\hat{\boldsymbol{n}}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{n}}^{T}=\begin{bmatrix}
1-2n_{1}^{2} & -2n_{1}n_{2} & -2n_{1}n_{3} \\
-2n_{1}n_{2} & 1-2n_{2}^{2} & -2n_{2}n_{3} \\
-2n_{1}n_{3} & -2n_{2}n_{3} & 1-2n_{3}^{2}
\end{bmatrix}$$

^q3d

### Projection

> [!def] Projection Matrix
> An [[2.2 - Matrices#^5bf009]] $\mathbf{P}$ that gives the same result when acting twice in succession as when it acts just once.
>
> $$(\mathbf{P})^{2}\equiv\mathbf{P}$$

^207fe0

> [!thm] 1D Projection Matrix
> A projection [[2.2 - Matrices#^5bf009]] $\mathbf{P}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}}\in \mathbb{R}^{n}$ induces a [[#^f63d2d]] projecting $\mathbb{R}^{n}$ onto the line along unit vector $\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}$. The [[#^e93306]] is a [[#^207fe0]].
> $$\mathbf{P}_{\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}}=\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}^{T}=\ket{u}\bra{u}$$

^p1d

> [!thm] Projection Operator
> A projection [[2.2 - Matrices#^5bf009]] $\mathbf{P}_{U}\in \mathbb{C}^{n}$ maps $\mathbb{C}^{n}$ onto a lower dimensional [[2.2 - Subspaces#^420b21]] ($m<n$) with [[2.2 - Subspaces#^c6eb2b]] $\{u\}=\{\ket{u_{1}}, \, \dots, \, \ket{u_{m}} \}$.
>
> The [[#^e93306]] is a [[#^207fe0]] $\mathbf{P}_{U}$, found with a sum of each [[#^p1d]].
>
> $$\mathbf{P}_{U} =\sum_{i=1}^{m}\ket{u_{i}} \bra{u_{i}} $$

^b64e03

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^b64e03]]

The projection of $\mathbf{v}$ onto subspace $U$ is the sum of the [[#^p1d]] of $\mathbf{v}$ onto each orthonormal basis vector $\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{i}$.

$$
\mathbf{P}_{U}\,\mathbf{v}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\mathbf{v}\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{u}})\,\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}\otimes \hat{\boldsymbol{u}})\cdot \mathbf{v}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\ket{u_{i}}\bra{u_{i}}\ket{v} 
$$

`\end{proof}`
