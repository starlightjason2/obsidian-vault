# 3.3.4 - Coordinate Systems

> [!def] Coordinate Vector
> A [[2.1 - Vectors#^column-vector]] $\mathbf{r}\in \mathbb{R}^{3}$ representing a point in space, expressed in [[2.6 - Coordinate Sytems#^cartesian-coordinates]] by default.
>$$\mathbf{r}=\begin{bmatrix}
x \\
y \\
z
\end{bmatrix}=x\,\hat{x}+y\,\hat{y}+z\,\hat{z}$$

^367268

> [!def] Orthogonal Coordinate System
> Any [[2.2 - Subspaces#^orthonormal-basis]] $\{\,\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{1},\,\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{2},\,\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{3} \}$ spanning $\mathbb{R}^{3}$ forms an **orthogonal coordinate system** $(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})$.
>
> Any [[2.6 - Coordinate Sytems#^367268]] $\mathbf{r}\in \mathbb{R}^{3}$ can be a transformed from $(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})$ to [[#^cartesian-coordinates]] $(x,y,z)$ with [[3.1 - Scalar Functions#^function|Functions]] $x=X(\mathbf{u}),\ y=Y(\mathbf{u}), \ z=Z(\mathbf{u})$.
>
>$$\begin{align}
\mathbf{r}(\mathbf{u})&=u_{1}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{1}+u_{2}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{2}+u_{3}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{3}=X(\mathbf{u})\,\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}+Y(\mathbf{u})\,\hat{\boldsymbol{y}}+Z(\mathbf{u})\,\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}\end{align}$$
>
>
>
>
>

^9cf7bf

> [!def] Cartesian Coordinates
> The Cartesian $(x,y,z)$ coordinate system is the [[#^9cf7bf]] using the [[2.2 - Subspaces#^8f5ebd]].
> $$\mathbf{r}=x\,\,\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}+y\,\,\hat{\boldsymbol{y}}+z\,\,\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}=\mathbf{I}\begin{bmatrix}
> x \\
> y \\
> z
> \end{bmatrix}$$

^cartesian-coordinates

## Orthogonal Coordinates

> [!thm] $d\mathbf{r}$ in Orthogonal Coordinates
> Let $\mathbf{r}(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})$ be a [[3.2 - Vector Functions#^c8839e]] in an [[2.6 - Coordinate Sytems#^9cf7bf]] $(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})$.
>
> The [[3.6 - Vector Derivatives#^vector-differential]] $d\mathbf{r}$ is the sum of all $h_{k}\,du_{k}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{k}$, where $h_{k}$ is the [[#^6287a0]] for $u_{k}$ for $k\in \{1,2,3 \}$.
>
> $$d\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{J}\,d\mathbf{u}=\sum_{k=0}^{3}{h_{k}\,du_{k}}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{k}$$
>

^dmathbfr-in-orthogonal-coordinates

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^dmathbfr-in-orthogonal-coordinates|$d\mathbf{r}$]]
Let $\mathbf{r}=X(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})\,\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}+Y(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})\,\hat{\boldsymbol{y}}+Z(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})\,\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}$ with $\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{r}}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial u_{1}} & \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial u_{2}} & \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial u_{3}}\end{bmatrix}$.

By definition, $d\mathbf{r}$ is the [[3.5 - Derivatives#^24b618]] along each vector component.

$$
d\mathbf{r}=\begin{bmatrix}
dX \\
dY \\
dZ
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}
\dfrac{\partial X}{\partial u_{1}}+\dfrac{\partial X}{\partial u_{2}}+\dfrac{\partial X}{\partial u_{3}} \\
\dfrac{\partial Y}{\partial u_{1}}+\dfrac{\partial Y}{\partial u_{2}}+\dfrac{\partial Y}{\partial u_{3}} \\
\dfrac{\partial Z}{\partial u_{1}}+\dfrac{\partial Z}{\partial u_{2}}+\dfrac{\partial Z}{\partial u_{3}}
\end{bmatrix}
$$

^4d6274

Multiply out $\mathbf{J}\,d\mathbf{u}$ to see that [[#^4d6274]] is equal to [[#^7006b1]].

$$
\mathbf{J}\,d\mathbf{u}=\begin{bmatrix}
\dfrac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial u_{1}} & \dfrac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial u_{2}} & \dfrac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial u_{3}}
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}
du_{1} \\
du_{2} \\
du_{3}
\end{bmatrix}=\frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial u_{1}}du_{1} +\frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial u_{1}}du_{2} + \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial u_{1}}du_{3}
$$

^7006b1

 The [[#^orthonormal-curvilinear-basis]] is $\{\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{1},\,\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{2},\,\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{3}\}=\left\{\frac{1}{h_{u_{1}}}\frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial u_{1}}, \frac{1}{h_{u_{2}}}\frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial u_{2}}, \frac{1}{h_{u_{3}}} \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial u_{3}}\right\}$. Multiply the $k$th element by $h_{k}\,du_{k}$ and apply a sum. Notice [[#^f38a7a]] and [[#^7006b1]] are also equal.

$$\begin{align}
h_{1}\,du_{1}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{1}+h_{2}\,du_{2}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{2}+du_{3}\,h_{3}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{3}=\frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial u_{1}}\,du_{1}+\frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial u_{2}}\,du_{2}+\frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial u_{3}}\,du_{3}  
\end{align}$$

^f38a7a

The same argument applies for higher dimensional vectors by symmetry. 
`\end{proof}`
### Basis

>[!thm] Orthonormal Curvilinear Basis
>An [[2.2 - Subspaces#^orthonormal-basis]] for [[#^9cf7bf]] $(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})$ is formed by the [[2.2 - Matrices#^column-space]] of the [[3.6 - Vector Derivatives#^483301]] $\mathbf{J}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial u_{1}} & \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial u_{2}} & \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial u_{3}}\end{bmatrix}$.
>
> The basis is [[2.1 - Vectors#^unit-vector|normalized]] with the [[#^6287a0]]. 
>$$\{\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{1},\,\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{2},\,\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{3}\}=\left\{\frac{1}{h_{u_{1}}}\frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial u_{1}}, \frac{1}{h_{u_{2}}}\frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial u_{2}}, \frac{1}{h_{u_{3}}} \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial u_{3}}\right\}$$
>

^orthonormal-curvilinear-basis

>[!thm] Coordinate Scaling Factors
> Let $\mathbf{r}\in \mathbb{R}^{3}$. Let the transformation from [[#^9cf7bf]] $(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})$ to $(x,y,z)$ be described by the [[3.6 - Vector Derivatives#^483301]] $\mathbf{J}=\begin{bmatrix}\frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial u_{1}} & \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial u_{2}} &\frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial u_{3}}\end{bmatrix}$. Let $k\in \{1,2,3 \}$.
> 
> The [[2.1 - Vectors#^57eeb7]] $\left\lVert {\frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial u_{k}}} \right\rVert\ =h_{u_k}$ represents how much each $u_{k}$ component of $\mathbf{r}$ **scales** during the transformation.  
> $$
\begin{align}
h_{u_k}=\left\lVert {\frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial u_{k}}} \right\rVert\,=\sqrt{\left( \frac{\partial X}{\partial u_{k}} \right)^{2}+\left( \frac{\partial Y}{\partial u_{k}} \right)^{2}+\left( \frac{\partial Z}{\partial u_{k}} \right)^{2}}
\end{align}$$

^6287a0

>[!thm] Jacobian Determinant
>The [[2.4 - Determinants#^determinant]] of the [[3.6 - Vector Derivatives#^483301]] describes the volume differential $dV$ in a $(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})$ [[#^9cf7bf]]. It's equal to the product of each [[#^6287a0|Scaling Factor]].
>$$dV=\det(\mathbf{J})=h_{u_{1}}h_{u_{2}}h_{u_{3}}$$

^jacobian-determinant


## Cylindrical Coordinates

>[!def] Cylindrical Coordinates
>The Cylindrical $(s,\phi,z)$ coordinate system is the [[#^9cf7bf]] where $s$ is the distance from the origin to $\mathbf{r}$, the polar $\phi$ is the angle between the $x$ axis and $\mathbf{r}$, and $z$ is the standard $z$.
>
>$$\mathbf{r}=s\cos\phi \,\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}+s\sin \phi \,\hat{\boldsymbol{y}}+z \,\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}$$
>
>The [[#^73267c]] are $\{h_{s},h_{\phi},h_{z} \}=\{1,s,1 \}$ so [[#^dmathbfr-in-orthogonal-coordinates|$d\mathbf{r}$]] is
>
>$$d\mathbf{r}=ds\,\hat{\boldsymbol{s}}+s\,d\phi\,\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}+dz\,\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}$$
>
>

^cylindrical-coordinates

### Jacobian

>[!thm] Cylindrical Jacobian
>The [[3.6 - Vector Derivatives#^483301]] $\mathbf{J}$ for $\mathbf{r}$ in [[#^cylindrical-coordinates]] $(s,\phi,z)$ is 
>$$\mathbf{J}=\begin{bmatrix}
\cos \phi & -s\sin \phi & 0 \\
\sin \phi & s\cos \phi & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 1
\end{bmatrix}$$

^1691ee

>[!cor] Cylindrical Scaling Factors
>Each [[#^6287a0]] for [[#^cylindrical-coordinates]] is 
>$$\begin{align}
h_{s}&=\sqrt{\cos^{2}\phi+\sin^{2}\phi}&&=1 \\
h_{\phi}&=\sqrt{s^{2}\sin^{2}\phi+s^{2}\cos^{2}\phi}&&=s \\
h_{z}&=\sqrt{1^{2}}&&=1
\end{align}$$

^73267c

### Basis

>[!thm] Cylindrical Basis
>The [[#^orthonormal-curvilinear-basis]] for [[#^cylindrical-coordinates]] is $\{\hat{\boldsymbol{s}},\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}},\hat{\boldsymbol{z}} \}$.
>
>$$\begin{align}
\hat{\boldsymbol{s}}&=\cos \phi \,\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}+\sin \phi \,\hat{\boldsymbol{y}} \\
\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}&=-\sin \phi \,\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}+\cos \phi \,\hat{\boldsymbol{y}} \\
\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}&=\,\hat{\boldsymbol{z}} \\
\end{align}$$


^4f7563

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^4f7563]]
Using the [[#^1691ee]], we know 

$$
\begin{align}
\frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial s}&=\cos \phi \,\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}+\sin \phi \,\hat{\boldsymbol{y}} \\
\frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial \phi}&=-s\sin \phi \,\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}+s\cos \phi \,\hat{\boldsymbol{y}} \\
\frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial z}&=\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}
\end{align}
$$

Let $\{h_{s},h_{\phi},h_{z} \}=\{1,s,1 \}$ be the [[#^73267c]].  

$$
\begin{align}
\hat{\boldsymbol{s}}&=\frac{1}{h_{s}}\frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial s}=1(\cos \phi \,\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}+\sin \phi \,\hat{\boldsymbol{y}})\\
\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}&=\frac{1}{h_{\phi}}\frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial \phi}=\frac{1}{\cancel{s}}(-\cancel{s}\sin \phi\,\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}+\cancel{s}\cos \phi \,\hat{\boldsymbol{y}}) \\
\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}&=\frac{1}{h_{z}}\frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial z}=1\,\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}
\end{align}
$$

`\end{proof}`

>[!cor] $d\mathbf{r}$ in Cylinderical Coordinates
>The differential [[#^dmathbfr-in-orthogonal-coordinates|$d\mathbf{r}$]] in [[#^cylindrical-coordinates]].
>$$d\mathbf{r}=ds\,\hat{\boldsymbol{s}}+s\,d\phi\,\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}+z\,\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}$$

^dmathbfr-in-cylinderical-coordinates

## Spherical Coordinates

> [!def] Spherical Coordinates
> Let $\mathbf{r}\in \mathbb{R}^{3}$. The spherical $(r,\phi,\theta)$ coordinate system is an [[#^9cf7bf]] where $r$ is the distance from the origin to $\mathbf{r}$, the polar $\phi$ is the angle between the $x$ axis and $\mathbf{r}$, and the azimuthal $\theta$ is the angle between the $z$ axis and $\mathbf{r}$.
>
> $$\mathbf{r}=x \,\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}+y \,\hat{\boldsymbol{y}}+z \,\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}=r\cos\phi \sin\theta \,\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}+r\sin \phi \sin\theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol{y}}+r\cos \theta \,\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}$$
> 
> The [[#^080a20]] are $\{h_{r},h_{\phi},h_{\theta} \}=\{1,r\sin\theta,r \}$, and [[#^dmathbfr-in-orthogonal-coordinates|$d\mathbf{r}$]] is $d\mathbf{r}=dr\,\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}+r\sin\theta\,d\phi\,\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}+r\,d\theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}$.

^spherical-coordinates

### Jacobian

> [!thm] Spherical Jacobian
> The [[3.6 - Vector Derivatives#^483301]] $\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{r}}$ in [[#^spherical-coordinates]] $(r,\phi,\theta)$ is
>$$\mathbf{J}=\begin{bmatrix}
\cos \phi \sin\theta & -r\sin \phi \sin\theta & r\cos \phi \cos\theta \\
\sin \phi \sin\theta & r\cos \phi \sin\theta & r\sin \phi\cos \theta \\
\cos\theta& 0 & -r\sin\theta
\end{bmatrix}$$

^125da3

> [!cor] Spherical Scaling Factors  
> Each [[#^6287a0]] for [[#^spherical-coordinates]] is
>$$\begin{align}
h_{r}&=\sqrt{\sin^{2}\theta(\cos^{2}\phi+\sin^{2}\phi)+\cos^{2}\theta}&&=1 \\
h_{\phi}&=\sqrt{r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\,(\cos^{2}\phi+\sin^{2}\phi)}&&=r\sin\theta \\
h_{\theta}&=\sqrt{r^{2}\cos^{2}\theta\,(\sin^{2}\phi+\cos^{2}\phi))+r^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}&&=r
\end{align}$$

^080a20

### Basis

> [!cor] Spherical Basis
> The [[#^orthonormal-curvilinear-basis]] for [[#^spherical-coordinates]] is $\{\hat{\boldsymbol{r}},\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}},\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}\}$.
>$$\begin{align}
\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}&=x\,\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}+y\,\hat{\boldsymbol{y}}+z\,\hat{\boldsymbol{z}} \\
\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}&=-\sin \phi \,\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}+\cos \phi\,\hat{\boldsymbol{y}}\\
\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}&=\cos \phi \cos \theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}+\sin \phi \cos \theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol{y}}-\sin \theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}
\end{align}$$

^95fa4c

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^95fa4c]]
Using the [[#^125da3]], we know 

$$
\begin{align}
\frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial r}&= \cos \phi \sin\theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol{x}} +
\sin \phi \sin\theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol{y}}+\cos\theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol{z}} \\
\frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial \phi}&=-r\sin \phi \sin\theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol{x}} +r\cos \phi \sin\theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol{y}}\\
\frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial \theta}&=r\cos \phi \cos\theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}+r\sin \phi\cos \theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol{y}}-r\sin\theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}
\end{align}
$$

Let $\{h_{r},h_{\phi},h_{\theta} \}=\{1,r\sin\theta,r \}$ be the [[#^080a20]].  

$$
\begin{align}
\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}&=\frac{1}{h_{r}}\frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial r}= 1(\cos \phi \sin\theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol{x}} +
\sin \phi \sin\theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol{y}}+\cos\theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol{z}})
\\
\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}&=\frac{1}{h_{\phi}}\frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial \phi}= \cancel{\frac{1}{r\sin\theta}} (-\cancel{r\sin\theta}\sin \phi\,\hat{\boldsymbol{x}} +\cancel{r\sin\theta}\cos \phi \,\hat{\boldsymbol{y}})
\\
\hat{\boldsymbol{\theta}}&=\frac{1}{h_{\theta}}\frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial @t}=\cancel{\frac{1}{r}} (\cancel{r}\cos \phi \cos\theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}+\cancel{r}\sin \phi\cos \theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol{y}}-\cancel{r}\sin\theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol{z}})
\end{align}
$$
