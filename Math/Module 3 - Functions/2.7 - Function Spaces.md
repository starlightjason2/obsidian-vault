# 2.7 - Function Spaces

> [!def] Function Space
> A function space is a [[2.1 - Vector Spaces#^aece1f]], defined with the [[#^9dd68a]] and the induced norm $\left\lvert{f(x)}\right\rvert^{2}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\bar{f}(x)\,f(x)\,dx$.
>
> Each entity $f(x)$ is a [[3.1 - Scalar Functions#^f874ea]] behaving like a [[2.1 - Vector Spaces#^f36929]] with **infinite** [[2.2 - Subspaces#^09a800]].
>
>

## Inner Product

> [!def] Functional Inner Product
> The [[2.1 - Vectors#^efaf9e]] of each [[3.1 - Scalar Functions#^f874ea]] $f(x), \ g(x)$ is the [[3.9 - Integrals#^integral]] of $\bar{f}(x)g(x)$ over $x\in(-\infty,\infty)$, where $\bar{f}(x)$ is the [[3.6 - Complex Numbers#^29ae56]] of $f(x)$.
> $$\Braket{f | g}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\bar{f}(x)\,g(x)\,dx  $$
>

^9dd68a

> [!thm] Cauchy-Schwarz Inequality
> For any two functions $f, g$ in a [[#^549a88|Function Space]]:
> $$\braket{f|f}\braket{g|g}\geq|\braket{f|g}|^2$$

^cauchy-schwarz

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^cauchy-schwarz]]
For any $\lambda\in\mathbb{C}$, the norm $\braket{f+\lambda g|f+\lambda g}\geq 0$. Expanding:
$$\braket{f|f}+\lambda\braket{f|g}+\bar{\lambda}\braket{g|f}+|\lambda|^2\braket{g|g}\geq 0$$
Setting $\lambda=-\braket{g|f}/\braket{g|g}$ and simplifying gives $\braket{f|f}\braket{g|g}\geq|\braket{f|g}|^2$.
`\end{proof}`

## Basis

> [!def] Basis Functions
> The [[2.2 - Subspaces#^ffe859]] of a [[#^549a88]] is an [[5.1 - Infinite Sequences#^cb5e23]] of **basis functions** $\{ \hat{f}_{n}(x)\}$ for $n\in \mathbb{Z}$.
>
> A function $f(x)$ can be written in basis $\{ \hat{f}_{n}(x)\}$ with an [[5.2 - Infinite Series#^07b41c]] of the [[#^9dd68a]].
> $$f(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{n}\,\hat{f}_{n}(x) =\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left( \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\bar{\hat{f}_{n}}(x)\,f(x)\,dx \right)\hat{f}(x)$$

^858d1d

> [!def] Linearly Independent Functions
> If each [[3.1 - Scalar Functions#^f874ea]] $\{f_{1}(x), \ \dots, \ f_{n}(x) \}$ forms a [[2.2 - Subspaces#^992f04]], then the functions are linearly independent.

^964327

> [!def] Orthonormal Basis
> An [[2.2 - Subspaces#^c6eb2b]] for a [[#^549a88]] is expressed compactly with the [[3.5 - Step and Delta#^017ca2]].
> $$\braket{x_{i}|x_{j}}=\delta(x_{i}-x_{j})$$
>

^81a43f

> [!thm] Completeness Relation
> The [[2.1 - Vector Spaces#^3e433d]] for a continuous [[2.1 - Vector Spaces#^0af3a0|Complete]] [[2.7 - Function Spaces#^549a88]] becomes as [[3.9 - Integrals#^integral]] equating to the [[2.7 - Function Spaces#^66cb8c]].
> $$\int \ket{x} \bra{x} \,dx=1$$

^3e433d

> [!def] Complete Orthonormal Basis
> [[#^858d1d]] obey the [[2.1 - Vector Spaces#^3e433d]] for a complete function space. The [[3.5 - Step and Delta#^017ca2]] represents orthonormality.
> 1. $$\braket{x_{i}|x_{j}}=\delta(x_{i}-x_{j})$$
> 2. $$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\ket{x_{i}} \bra{x_{i}} \,dx=\mathbf{I}$$

## Linear Transformations

> [!def] Linear Operator
> A linear operator $\mathcal{L}$ is a [[2.5 - Linear Transformations#^f63d2d]] acting on a [[3.1 - Scalar Functions#^f874ea]].
> $$\mathcal{L}\Big(c_{1}\,f(x)+c_{2}\,g(x)\Big)=c_{1}\,\mathcal{L}f(x)+c_{2}\,\mathcal{L}g(x)$$

^3fa82b

> [!def] Hermitian Operator
> A [[#^3fa82b]] $\mathcal{L}$ equal to it's [[3.6 - Complex Numbers#^29ae56]].
> $$\mathcal{L} \text{ is Hermitian}\iff \mathcal{L}f(x)=\mathcal{L}\bar{f}(x)$$

^6146ae

> [!thm] Shift of Hermitian Operator is Hermitian
> For a [[#^6146ae|Hermitian Operator]] $\hat{A}$ with real expectation value $\langle\hat{A}\rangle\in\mathbb{R}$, the shifted operator $\delta\hat{A}=\hat{A}-\langle\hat{A}\rangle$ is also Hermitian:
> $$(\delta\hat{A})^\dagger=(\hat{A}-\langle\hat{A}\rangle)^\dagger=\hat{A}^\dagger-\langle\hat{A}\rangle=\hat{A}-\langle\hat{A}\rangle=\delta\hat{A}$$

^herm-shift

> [!def] Identity Operator
> The identity [[#^3fa82b]] $\mathbf{1}$ leaves a function unchanged.
> $$\mathbf{1}f(x)\equiv f(x)$$
>

### Commutators

> [!def] Commutator
> Let $A, \ \hat{B}$ each be a [[2.7 - Function Spaces#^3fa82b]]. The **commutator** $[\hat{A},\hat{B}]$ is another operator that measures the difference between applying each in different order.
> $$[\hat{A},\hat{B}]\equiv\hat{A}\hat{B}-\hat{B}\hat{A}$$

^cb348c

> [!def] Anti-Commutator
> The **anti-commutator** $\{\hat{A},\hat{B}\}$ of two [[#^3fa82b|Linear Operators]] is the sum of the two orderings:
> $$\{\hat{A},\hat{B}\}\equiv\hat{A}\hat{B}+\hat{B}\hat{A}$$
> If $\hat{A},\hat{B}$ are [[#^6146ae|Hermitian]], then $\{\hat{A},\hat{B}\}$ is also Hermitian: $\{\hat{A},\hat{B}\}^\dagger=\{\hat{A},\hat{B}\}$.

^anti-comm

> [!def] Commuting Operators
> **Commuting matrices** $\hat{A}, \ \hat{B}$ have a [[#^cb348c]] of $[\hat{A},\hat{B}]=0$.

### Eigenbasis

> [!def] Eigenfunction
> For a [[#^3fa82b]] $\mathcal{L}$, the eigenfunction $\phi_{n}$ is an [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^b325d4]] with an [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^888169]] $a_{n}$.
> $$\mathcal{L}\,\phi_{n}=a_{n}\phi_{n}(x)$$
