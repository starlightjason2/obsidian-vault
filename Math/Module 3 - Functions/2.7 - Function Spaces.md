# 2.7 - Function Spaces

> [!def] Function Space
> A function space is a [[2.1 - Vector Spaces#^hilbert-space]], defined with the [[#^functional-inner-product]] and the induced norm $\left\lvert{f(x)}\right\rvert^{2}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\bar{f}(x)\,f(x)\,dx$.
>
> Each entity $f(x)$ is a [[3.1 - Scalar Functions#^function]] behaving like a [[2.1 - Vector Spaces#^vector]] with **infinite** [[2.2 - Subspaces#^dimension]].

^function-space

> [!def] $L^{2}$ Space
> The "well-behaved" square [[3.9 - Integrals#^integral|Integrable]] functions live in the $L^{2}$ [[2.7 - Function Spaces#^function-space]].

^l2-space

## Inner Product

> [!def] Functional Inner Product
> The [[2.1 - Vectors#^efaf9e]] of each [[3.1 - Scalar Functions#^function]] $f(x), \ g(x)$ is the [[3.9 - Integrals#^integral]] of $\bar{f}(x)g(x)$ over $x\in(-\infty,\infty)$, where $\bar{f}(x)$ is the [[3.6 - Complex Numbers#^complex-conjugate]] of $f(x)$.
> $$\Braket{f | g}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\bar{f}(x)\,g(x)\,dx  $$

^functional-inner-product

> [!thm] Cauchy-Schwarz Inequality
> For any two functions $f, g$ in a [[#^function-space|Function Space]]:
> $$\braket{f|f}\braket{g|g}\geq|\braket{f|g}|^2$$

^cauchy-schwarz-inequality

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^cauchy-schwarz-inequality]]
For any $\lambda\in\mathbb{C}$, the norm $\braket{f+\lambda g|f+\lambda g}\geq 0$. Expanding:
$$\braket{f|f}+\lambda\braket{f|g}+\bar{\lambda}\braket{g|f}+|\lambda|^2\braket{g|g}\geq 0$$
Setting $\lambda=-\braket{g|f}/\braket{g|g}$ and simplifying gives $\braket{f|f}\braket{g|g}\geq|\braket{f|g}|^2$.
`\end{proof}`

## Basis

> [!def] Basis Functions
> The [[2.2 - Subspaces#^basis]] of a [[#^function-space]] is an [[5.1 - Infinite Sequences#^cb5e23]] of **basis functions** $\{ \hat{f}_{n}(x)\}$ for $n\in \mathbb{Z}$.
>
> A function $f(x)$ can be written in basis $\{ \hat{f}_{n}(x)\}$ with an [[5.2 - Infinite Series#^infinite-series]] of the [[#^functional-inner-product]].
> $$f(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{n}\,\hat{f}_{n}(x) =\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left( \int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\bar{\hat{f}_{n}}(x)\,f(x)\,dx \right)\hat{f}(x)$$

^basis-functions

> [!def] Linearly Independent Functions
> If each [[3.1 - Scalar Functions#^function]] $\{f_{1}(x), \ \dots, \ f_{n}(x) \}$ forms a [[2.2 - Subspaces#^linearly-independent-set]], then the functions are linearly independent.

^linearly-independent-functions

> [!def] Orthonormal Basis
> An [[2.2 - Subspaces#^orthonormal-basis]] for a [[#^function-space]] consists of [[#^basis-functions]] and is expressed compactly with the [[3.5 - Step and Delta#^017ca2]].
> $$\braket{x_{i}|x_{j}}=\delta(x_{i}-x_{j})$$

^orthonormal-function-basis

> [!thm] Completeness Relation
> The [[2.1 - Vector Spaces#^completeness-relation]] for a continuous [[2.1 - Vector Spaces#^complete-normed-space|Complete]] [[2.7 - Function Spaces#^function-space]] becomes as [[3.9 - Integrals#^integral]] equating to the [[2.8 - Linear Operators#^identity-operator]].
> $$\int \ket{x} \bra{x} \,dx=1$$

^completeness-function-relation

> [!def] Complete Orthonormal Basis
> A complete [[#^orthonormal-function-basis]] obeys the [[2.1 - Vector Spaces#^completeness-relation]].
> $$f(x)=\sum_{n}c_{n}x_{n}$$
> The [[3.5 - Step and Delta#^017ca2]] is a compact representation of orthonormality.
>
> 1. $$\braket{x_{i}|x_{j}}=\delta(x_{i}-x_{j})$$
> 2. $$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\ket{x_{i}} \bra{x_{i}} \,dx=\mathbf{I}$$

^complete-orthonormal-function-basis
