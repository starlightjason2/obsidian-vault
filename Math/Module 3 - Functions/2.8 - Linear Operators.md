# 2.8 - Linear Operators

> [!def] Linear Operator
> A linear operator $\mathcal{L}$ is a [[2.5 - Linear Transformations#^linear-transformation]] acting on a [[3.1 - Scalar Functions#^function]].
> $$\mathcal{L}\Big(c_{1}\,f(x)+c_{2}\,g(x)\Big)=c_{1}\,\mathcal{L}f(x)+c_{2}\,\mathcal{L}g(x)$$

^linear-operator

## Hermitian Operators

> [!def] Hermitian Operator
> A [[#^linear-operator]] $\mathcal{L}$ equal to it's [[3.6 - Complex Numbers#^complex-conjugate]].
> $$\mathcal{L} \text{ is Hermitian}\iff \mathcal{L}f(x)=\mathcal{L}\bar{f}(x)$$

^hermitian-operator

> [!thm] Shift of Hermitian Operator is Hermitian
> For a [[#^hermitian-operator|Hermitian Operator]] $\hat{A}$ with real expectation value $\langle\hat{A}\rangle\in\mathbb{R}$, the shifted operator $\delta\hat{A}=\hat{A}-\langle\hat{A}\rangle$ is also Hermitian:
> $$(\delta\hat{A})^\dagger=(\hat{A}-\langle\hat{A}\rangle)^\dagger=\hat{A}^\dagger-\langle\hat{A}\rangle=\hat{A}-\langle\hat{A}\rangle=\delta\hat{A}$$

^shift-of-hermitian-operator-is-hermitian

## Commutators

> [!def] Commutator
> Let $A, \ \hat{B}$ each be a [[#^linear-operator]]. The **commutator** $[\hat{A},\hat{B}]$ is another operator that measures the difference between applying each in different order.
> $$[\hat{A},\hat{B}]\equiv\hat{A}\hat{B}-\hat{B}\hat{A}$$

^commutator-2

> [!def] Anti-Commutator
> The **anti-commutator** $\{\hat{A},\hat{B}\}$ of two [[#^linear-operator|Linear Operators]] is the sum of the two orderings:
> $$\{\hat{A},\hat{B}\}\equiv\hat{A}\hat{B}+\hat{B}\hat{A}$$
> If $\hat{A},\hat{B}$ are [[#^hermitian-operator|Hermitian]], then $\{\hat{A},\hat{B}\}$ is also Hermitian: $\{\hat{A},\hat{B}\}^\dagger=\{\hat{A},\hat{B}\}$.

^anti-commutator

> [!def] Commuting Operators
> **Commuting matrices** $\hat{A}, \ \hat{B}$ have a [[#^commutator-2]] of $[\hat{A},\hat{B}]=0$.

^commuting-operators

## Eigenstructure

> [!def] Eigenfunction
> For a [[#^linear-operator]] $\mathcal{L}$, the eigenfunction $\phi_{n}$ is an [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^eiegenvector]] with an [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^eiegenvalue]] $a_{n}$.
> $$\mathcal{L}\,\phi_{n}=a_{n}\phi_{n}(x)$$

^eigenfunction

> [!thm] Matrix Representation of a Linear Operator
> Let there be a [[#^linear-operator]] $\hat{A}$ on a [[2.7 - Function Spaces#^function-space|Function Space]] $V$ with [[2.7 - Function Spaces#^complete-orthonormal-function-basis|Orthonormal Basis]] $\{\ket{e_{n}}\}$. The action of $\hat{A}$ in this basis is encoded as the [[2.2 - Matrices#^5bf009|Matrix]] $\mathbf{A}$ whose $(i,j)$ entry is $A_{ij}=\bra{e_{i}}\hat{A}\ket{e_{j}}$.
>
> $$
> \mathbf{A}\doteq\begin{bmatrix}
> \bra{e_{1}}\hat{A}\ket{e_{1}} & \dots & \bra{e_{1}}\hat{A}\ket{e_{n}} \\
> \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\
> \bra{e_{m}}\hat{A}\ket{e_{1}} & \dots & \bra{e_{m}}\hat{A}\ket{e_{n}}
> \end{bmatrix}
> $$

^matrix-representation-of-a-linear-operator

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^matrix-representation-of-a-linear-operator]]
Insert the [[2.1 - Vector Spaces#^completeness-relation|Completeness Relation]] $\hat{I}=\sum_{j}\ket{e_{j}}\bra{e_{j}}$ on the right of $\hat{A}$, then project onto $\bra{e_{i}}$ on the left:

$$\bra{e_{i}}\hat{A}\ket{\psi}=\bra{e_{i}}\hat{A}\!\left(\sum_{j}\ket{e_{j}}\bra{e_{j}}\right)\!\ket{\psi}=\sum_{j}\bra{e_{i}}\hat{A}\ket{e_{j}}\braket{e_{j}|\psi}$$

The coefficients $\braket{e_{j}|\psi}$ are the components of $\ket{\psi}$ in the basis, and $\bra{e_{i}}\hat{A}\ket{\psi}$ is the $i$th component of $\hat{A}\ket{\psi}$. The expression is therefore the matrix-vector product $(\mathbf{A}\boldsymbol{\psi})_{i}=\sum_{j}A_{ij}\psi_{j}$ with $A_{ij}=\bra{e_{i}}\hat{A}\ket{e_{j}}$.
`\end{proof}`

## Common Operators

> [!def] Identity Operator
> The identity [[#^linear-operator]] $\mathbf{1}$ leaves a function unchanged.
> $$\mathbf{1}f(x)\equiv f(x)$$

^identity-operator

> [!def] Parity Operator
> The parity operator $\hat{P}$ flips the sign of position. Applying it twice returns the [[#^identity-operator|Identity]], $\hat{P}^{2}=\mathbf{1}$, so its [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^eiegenvalue|Eigenvalues]] are $\pm 1$: an **even** [[3.1 - Scalar Functions#^function|Function]] satisfies $\hat{P}\psi=\psi$, an **odd** function satisfies $\hat{P}\psi=-\psi$.
> $$\hat{P}\psi(x)=\psi(-x)$$

^parity-operator
