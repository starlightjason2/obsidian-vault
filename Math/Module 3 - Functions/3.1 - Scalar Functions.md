# 3.1.1 - Scalar Functions

> [!def] Function
> A **scalar function** is a mapping $f(t_{1},\dots,t_{n}):\mathbb{R}^{n}\to \mathbb{R}$. Let the domain $D\subset \mathbb{R}^{n}$ be the input space, and the range $R\in \mathbb{R}$ be the output space. Functions live in a [[2.7 - Function Spaces#^function-space]].
>
> $$f(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})\equiv\{D\subset \mathbb{R}^{n}\} \to \{R\subset \mathbb{R} \}$$
>
>
>
>

^function

> [!def] Scalar Field
> A **vector field** is a [[#^function]] mapping each point in space to a scalar.
> $$f(x,y,z)=p$$

^scalar-field

> [!def] Level Curves
> To visualize a [[#^function]], we often use a contour map. For a 4D three variable function, the level "curve" will be a 3D level surface. Let $c$ be a constant.
> $$f(x,y, \dots)=c$$

^level-curves

## Parity

> [!def] Even Function
>A [[#^function]] symmetric about the $y$ axis is even:
>$$f(x)=f(-x)$$
>

^even-function

> [!def] Odd Function
>A [[#^function]] symmetric about $y=x$ is odd:
>$$f(-x)=-f(x)$$
>

## Common Functions

### Planes

> [!thm] Plane
> A [[#^function]] function of the form $z=ax+by+c$ results in a **plane.** Then $\mathbf{n}=\left<n_{x},n_{y},n_{z}\right>$ is a **normal [[2.1 - Vector Spaces#^vector]]** orthogonal to the plane.
> $$z=-\frac{n_{x}}{n_{z}}x-\frac{n_{y}}{n_{z}}y+d$$

^plane

> [!cor] Parallel Planes
> Let $P_{1},P_{2}$ be two planes in three dimensional space, and let $\mathbf{n}_{1},\mathbf{n}_{2}$ be their normal vectors.
>
> 1. If $\mathbf{n}_{2}\parallel \mathbf{n}_{2}$, then $P_{1}\parallel P_{2}$
> 2. If $\mathbf{n}_{1}\perp \mathbf{n}_{2}$, then $P_{1}\perp P_{2}$
> 3. If $\mathbf{n}_{2}$ intersects $\mathbf{n}_{2}$, then $P_{1}$ intersects $P_{2}$

> [!cor] Distance between a point and a plane
> Let there be a plane with a normal vector $\mathbf{n}$, and let $Q$ be a point on the plane. The distance between any point $P$ and the plane is the [[2.1 - Vectors#^0ab8b3]] of $\mathbf{QP}$ onto $\mathbf{n}$.
>
> $$\frac{\lVert{\mathbf{QP}\cdot \mathbf{n}}\rVert}{\lVert{\mathbf{n}}\rVert}$$

### Quadric Surfaces

A **quadric surface** is the level set of a second-order polynomial in three variables. Each quadric is classified by the signs of its squared terms, giving distinct conic-section traces along the coordinate planes.

> [!def] Ellipsoid
> Closed [[#^function|Scalar Function]] level set with three positive squared terms. All three coordinate-plane traces are ellipses, so the surface bounds a finite volume in every direction.
> $$\frac{x^{2}}{a^{2}}+\frac{y^{2}}{b^{2}}+\frac{z^{2}}{c^{2}}=1$$

^ellipsoid

> [!def] Hyperboloid of One Sheet
> Connected [[#^function|Scalar Function]] level set with two positive and one negative squared term, opening as a single hourglass-shaped surface. Constant-$x$ and constant-$y$ traces are hyperbolas, constant-$z$ traces are ellipses.
> $$\frac{x^{2}}{a^{2}}+\frac{y^{2}}{b^{2}}-\frac{z^{2}}{c^{2}}=1$$

^hyperboloid-one-sheet

> [!def] Hyperboloid of Two Sheets
> Disconnected [[#^function|Scalar Function]] level set with one positive and two negative squared terms, splitting into two opposing bowls along the dominant axis. Constant-$x$ and constant-$y$ traces are hyperbolas, constant-$z$ traces are ellipses or empty in the gap.
> $$-\frac{x^{2}}{a^{2}}-\frac{y^{2}}{b^{2}}+\frac{z^{2}}{c^{2}}=1$$

^hyperboloid-two-sheets

> [!def] Elliptic Cone
> Degenerate [[3.4 - Limits#^limit|Limit]] of the [[#^hyperboloid-one-sheet|Hyperboloid of One Sheet]] with right-hand side zero, pinching the surface to a point at the origin. Constant-$x$ and constant-$y$ traces are hyperbolas; constant-$z$ traces are ellipses that scale with $|z|$.
> $$\frac{x^{2}}{a^{2}}+\frac{y^{2}}{b^{2}}-\frac{z^{2}}{c^{2}}=0$$

^elliptic-cone

> [!def] Elliptic Paraboloid
> [[#^function|Scalar Function]] level set in two squared $x,y$ terms set linearly proportional to $z$, opening upward as a bowl. Constant-$x$ and constant-$y$ traces are parabolas, constant-$z$ traces are ellipses.
> $$\frac{x^{2}}{a^{2}}+\frac{y^{2}}{b^{2}}-z=0$$

^elliptic-paraboloid

> [!def] Hyperbolic Paraboloid
> Saddle-shaped [[#^function|Scalar Function]] level set with squared $x,y$ terms of opposite sign, set linearly proportional to $z$. Constant-$x$ and constant-$y$ traces are parabolas, constant-$z$ traces are hyperbolas.
> $$\frac{x^{2}}{a^{2}}-\frac{y^{2}}{b^{2}}-z=0$$

^hyperbolic-paraboloid
