# 3.1.2 - Vector Functions

> [!def] Vector Function
> A **vector function** is a mapping $\mathbb{R}^{n}\to \mathbb{R}^{m}$ written as a [[2.1 - Vector Spaces#^vector]] $\mathbf{F}\in \mathbb{R}^{m}$ where each component is a [[3.1 - Scalar Functions#^function]] $f_{i}(\mathbf{x})$ for $\mathbf{x}\in \mathbb{R}^{n}$.
>
> $$\mathbf{r}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\equiv\{D\subset \mathbb{R}^{n}\} \to \{R\subset \mathbb{R}^{m} \}=\begin{bmatrix}
f_{1}(\mathbf{x}) \\ \vdots \\f_{m}(\mathbf{x})
\end{bmatrix}$$

^c8839e

> [!thm] Integral of a Vector Function
> Let $\mathbf{r}(t)$ be a [[#^c8839e]]. Then $\int \mathbf{r}(\mathbf{t})$ is the component-wise integral.
> $$\int_{a}^{b} \mathbf{r}(\mathbf{t})\,dt=\begin{bmatrix}{\int_{a}^{b} r_{1}(\mathbf{t})\,dt}\\{\int_{a}^{b} r_{2}(\mathbf{t})\,d\mathbf{t}}\\{\,\dots}\end{bmatrix}$$

^integral-of-a-vector-function

> [!thm] Derivative of a Vector Function
> Let $\mathbf{r}(\mathbf{t})=\left<f_{1}(\mathbf{x}),\dots,f_{n}(\mathbf{x})\right>$ be a [[3.2 - Vector Functions#^c8839e]]. The [[3.5 - Derivatives#^partial-derivative]] with respect to $x_{i}$ is evaluated component-wise.
>
> $$\frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial x_{i}}=\begin{bmatrix}{\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial x_{i}}}\\{\dots}\\{\frac{\partial f_{n}}{\partial x_{i}}}\end{bmatrix}$$

^derivative-of-a-vector-function

## 2D Curves

> [!def] Curve
> An oriented **curve** is the path $C$ traced by a [[2.6 - Coordinate Sytems#^367268]] $\mathbf{r}$, oriented in the direction of the [[#^unit-tangent]].
> $\mathbf{r}(t)=\begin{bmatrix}{x(t)}\\{y(t)}\\{z(t)}\end{bmatrix}$
>
> Let $C$ start at point $\mathbf{r}(t_{0})$ and end at $\mathbf{r}(t_{f})$. Then for $t_{0}\leq t\leq t_{f}$, $C$ is
>
> 1. **Smooth** IFF each [[3.5 - Derivatives#^partial-derivative]] $x_{t},\ y_{t}, \ z_{t}$ exhibits [[3.4 - Limits#^continuous-function]].
> 2. **Closed** IFF $\mathbf{r}(t_{0})=\mathbf{r}(t_{f})$
> 3. **Simple** IFF $C$ does not **cross itself**: $\mathbf{r}(t) \leftrightarrow t\in(t_{0},t_{f})$.

^curve

> [!def] Curve Differential
> The differential of a [[#^curve]] $\mathbf{r}(t)$ is the [[3.6 - Vector Derivatives#^vector-differential]] $d\mathbf{r}$. With a one dimensional parameter $t$, we can use the [[3.2 - Vector Functions#^derivative-of-a-vector-function]] to find the [[3.5 - Derivatives#^exact-differential]] $d\mathbf{r}$.
> $$d\mathbf{r}=\frac{d\mathbf{r}}{dt}\,dt$$

^curve-differential

> [!exm] Line
> The [[#^curve]] $\mathbf{r}(t)=\left<x,y,z\right>$ traces out a **line** with a **starting point $\mathbf{r}_{0}=\left<x_{0},y_{0},z_{0}\right>$** and a **direction $\mathbf{v}$** (thought of as velocity).
> $$\mathbf{r}(t)=\mathbf{r}_{0}(t)+t\mathbf{v}$$
> By the definition of a line, the slope along $x,y,z$ should be linear, although not necessarily the same value.
> $$\frac{x-x_{0}}{a}=\frac{y-y_{0}}{b}=\frac{z-z_{0}}{c}$$

^line

### Arc Length

> [!thm] Arc Length
> Let $\mathbf{r}$ represent a **simple smooth** [[#^curve]] $C$. The **arc length** of $C$ is the integral of the magnitude of the [[3.2 - Vector Functions#^curve-differential]] $\lVert{d\mathbf{r}}\rVert\,$ over $a\leq t\leq b$.
> $$s=\int_{a}^{b}\left\lVert {d\mathbf{r}} \right\rVert$$
>
>

^arc-length

### Tangent

> [!def] Unit Tangent
> For a [[#^curve]] or [[#^645bcd]] $\mathbf{r}$, the **unit tangent** $\mathbf{T}$ is the [[2.1 - Vectors#^unit-vector]] of the **tangent**. The tangent is found with the [[3.5 - Derivatives#^partial-derivative]].
> $$\mathbf{T}=\frac{\mathbf{r}'}{\lVert{\mathbf{r}'}\rVert}$$

^unit-tangent

## 3D Surfaces

> [!def] Surface
> A **surface** $S$ is the shape traced by a [[3.2 - Vector Functions#^c8839e]] $\mathbf{r}(t,u)$. The direction of the [[#^unit-normal]] is the **orientation** of $S$.
>
> $$\mathbf{r}(t,u)=\begin{bmatrix}x(t,u)\\y(t,u)\\z(t,u)
\end{bmatrix}$$
>
> Let $D$ be the domain of $(t,u)$. Then $\forall\,(t,u)\in D$, $S$ is
>
> 1. **Regular** IFF $\mathbf{r}_{u}\times \mathbf{r}_{v}\neq 0$ (meaning $S$ has no holes).
> 2. **Smooth** IFF each [[3.5 - Derivatives#^partial-derivative]] exhibits [[3.4 - Limits#^continuous-function]] (meaning $S$ has no corners).
> 3. **Piecewise smooth** if it can be broken up into **smooth** faces.

^645bcd

### Differentiating Surfaces

> [!def] Surface Differential
> Let there be a [[3.2 - Vector Functions#^645bcd]] $S$ represented by $\mathbf{r}(u,v)$ in any coordinate system, with cross sectional [[#^curve]] given by [[3.5 - Derivatives#^partial-derivative]] $d\mathbf{r}_{1}=\frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial u}\\, \ d\mathbf{r}_{2}=\frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial v}$.
>
> We define $d\mathbf{A}$ with the [[2.1 - Vectors#^84cb23]] of $d\mathbf{r}_{1}\times d\mathbf{r}_{2}$.
>
> $$d\mathbf{A}=d\mathbf{r}_{1}\times d\mathbf{r}2=\left( \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial u}\times \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial v} \right)\,du\,dv$$

^surface-differential

> [!thm] Surface Area
> Let there be a surface $S$ represented by $\mathbf{r}(u,v)$ where $(u,v)\in D$. The surface area $A$ is the [[3.9 - Integrals#^double-integral]] of the [[2.1 - Vectors#^57eeb7]] of the [[#^surface-differential]].
>
> $$A=\int_{S} \lVert{d\mathbf{A}}\rVert\,=\iint_{D}\left\lVert {\frac{\partial{\mathbf{r}}}{\partial{u}}\times\frac{\partial{\mathbf{r}}}{\partial{v}}} \right\rVert\,du\,dv$$
>
>

^surface-area

> [!def] Curvature
> The **curvature** $\kappa$ of a [[#^curve]] is a measurement of how fast it turns, or the rate of change of the [[#^unit-tangent]] $\mathbf{T}(t)$ with respect to [[#^arc-length]]. Visualize it as the **radius of a tangent circle**.
>
> 1. $$\mathbf{r}\in \mathbb{R}^{2}, \ \kappa=\frac{d\mathbf{T}}{ds}=\frac{\mathbf{T}’(t)}{\mathbf{r}’(t)}$$
> 2. $$\mathbf{r}\in \mathbb{R}^{3}, \ \kappa=\frac{\mathbf{r}'(t)\times \mathbf{r}’’(t)}{\lVert{\mathbf{r}’(t)}\rVert^{3}}$$
> 
>

> [!thm] Parameterization of a Function
> A [[3.1 - Scalar Functions#^function]] of the form $z=f(x,y)$ can represent a shape in $\mathbb{R}^{3}$. That same shape be written as the [[#^645bcd]] $\mathbf{r}(t,u)$.
>
> $$\mathbf{r}(t,u)=\left<t,u,f(t,u)\right>$$
>
> With [[#^surface-differential]]:
>
>$$\begin{align}
d\mathbf{S}&=\left<-\frac{\partial f}{\partial t},-\frac{\partial f}{\partial u},\,1\right> \\
\lVert{d\mathbf{S}}\rVert\,&=\sqrt{\left( \frac{\partial f}{\partial t} \right)^{2}+\left( \frac{\partial f}{\partial u} \right)^{2}+1}\,dt\,du
\end{align}$$
>

#### Normal Vector

> [!def] Unit Normal
> A **unit normal** $\mathbf{N}$ is a [[2.1 - Vectors#^unit-vector]] always **orthogonal** to the [[#^unit-tangent]] $\mathbf{T}$ of a curve $\mathbf{r}$.
>
> 1. For a [[#^curve]] $\mathbf{r}(t)=\left<x(t)\\,y(t)\right>$, we see that $\mathbf{T}\cdot\left<-y'(t)\\,x'(t)\right>= 0$, and so
> $$\mathbf{N}=\frac{{\left<y'(t),-x'(t)\right>}}{\lVert{\left<y'(t),-x'(t)\right>}\rVert\,}$$
> 
> 2. For [[#^645bcd]] $\mathbf{r}(t,u)$, the [[2.1 - Vectors#^cross-product]] creates a vector orthogonal to the two tangent vectors $\frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial t}\\,\frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial u}$, and so
> $$\mathbf{N}=\frac{{\frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial t}\times \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial u}}}{\lVert{\frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial t}\times \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial u}}\rVert\,}$$

^unit-normal

## Common Parameterizations

> [!exm] Parameterization of a Sphere
> A sphere centered at the origin with a radius $R$ can be written as the [[#^645bcd]]
>
> $$\mathbf{r}(u,v)=R\left<\sin u \cos v ,\sin u  \sin v,\cos u\right>$$
>
> where the differential [[#^surface-area]] is
>
>$$\begin{align}
d\mathbf{S}&=R^{2}\left<\right>
\lVert{d\mathbf{S}}\rVert&=R^{2}\sin \phi\,d\theta\,d\phi
\end{align}$$
>

> [!exm] Parameterization of a Cylinder
> A cylinder with radius $R$ and height $Z$ can be written as the [[#^645bcd]]
>
> $$\mathbf{r}(u,v)=\left<R\cos u,R \sin u,v\right>, \ v\in[0,Z]$$
>
> where the differential [[#^surface-area]] is
>
>$$\begin{align}
d\mathbf{S}&=R\left<\cos u,\sin u\right>\,du\,dv \\
\lVert{d\mathbf{S}}\rVert&=R\,dt\,du
\end{align}$$

> [!exm] Parameterization of a Cone
> A cylinder with radius $R$ and height $Z$ can be written as the [[#^645bcd]] where $k=\frac{R}{Z}$
>
> $$\mathbf{r}(\theta,z)=\left<kz\cos\theta,kz\sin\theta,z\right>$$
>
> where the differential [[#^surface-area]] is
>
>$$\begin{align}
d\mathbf{S}&=k\left<z\cos\theta,z\sin\theta,-kz\right >\,d\theta\,dz \\
\lVert{d\mathbf{S}}\rVert&=kz\sqrt{1+k^{2}}\,d\theta\,dz
\end{align}$$
