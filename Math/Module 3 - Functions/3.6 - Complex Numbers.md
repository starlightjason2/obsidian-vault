# 3.6 - Complex Numbers

> [!def] Imaginary Number
> $$i\equiv\sqrt{-1}$$

^imaginary-number

> [!def] Complex Number
> A complex number $z\in \mathbb{C}$ contains a real component $a\in \mathbb{R}$ and multiple $b\in \mathbb{R}$ of the [[#^imaginary-number]] $i$.
>
> $$z=a+i\,b\tag{rectangular form}$$
>
> With [[#^eulers-formula]] where the [[#^norm]] $\left\lvert{z}\right\rvert=\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}$ and [[#^phase]] $\phi=\tan^{-=1}(b / a)$, we can express $z$ in exponential form,
>
> $$z=\left\lvert{z}\right\rvert\,e^{i\phi}\tag{polar form}$$

^complex-number

## Complex Space

> [!def] Complex Space
> The [[2.0 - Sets#^set]] $\mathbb{C}^{n}$ is a [[2.1 - Vector Spaces#^hilbert-space]] formed by every [[2.1 - Vector Spaces#^vector]] $\Ket{v}$ with [[2.2 - Subspaces#^dimension]] $n$ where each component is a [[3.6 - Complex Numbers#^complex-number]].
>
> As a Hilbert space, $\mathbb{C}^{n}$ is defined with the [[2.1 - Vectors#^efaf9e]] and the induced norm $\left\lvert\Ket{v}\right\rvert^{2}=\Braket{v | v}$.
>
>$$
>\mathbb{C}^{n}\equiv\left\{\begin{bmatrix}
x_{1} \\ \vdots \\ x_{n}
\end{bmatrix}:x_{j\in \mathbb{Z}^{+}}\in \mathbb{C}\right\}$$
>

## Properties

> [!def] Complex Conjugate
> Every [[#^complex-number]] $z=x+i\,y$ has a **conjugate** $\bar{z}=x-i\,y$.
>
> Notice $\bar{z}$ is the reflection of $z$ across the real axis.
> $$z=x+i\,y\implies \bar{z}=x-i\,y$$

^complex-conjugate

### Norm

> [!def] Norm
> The [[2.1 - Vectors#^57eeb7]] $\left\lvert{z}\right\rvert$ of a [[#^complex-number]] $z=a+i\,b$ is the Pythagorean length,
> $$\left\lvert{z}\right\rvert=\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}$$

^norm

> [!def] Norm Squared
> The **square** of the [[#^norm]].
> $$\lvert{x+i\,y}\rvert=x^{2}+y^{2}$$

^norm-squared
> [!thm] Norm from Conjugate
> Given a [[3.6 - Complex Numbers#^complex-number]] $z$, the [[#^norm]] squared $\lvert{z}\rvert^{2}$ is $z$ times it's [[#^complex-conjugate]] $\bar{z}$.
> $$z\,\bar{z}=\lvert{z}\rvert^{2}$$

^norm-from-conjugate

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^norm-from-conjugate]]
By [[#^eulers-formula]],

$$A=\left\lvert{A}\right\rvert e^{i\phi}=a+i\,b, \quad \bar{A}=\left\lvert{A}\right\rvert e^{-i\phi}=a-ib$$

Therefore, $A\bar{A}=\left\lvert{A}\right\rvert^{2}e^{i\phi}e^{-i\phi}=\left\lvert{A}\right\rvert^{2}$
`\end{proof}`

### Phase

> [!def] Phase
> The **phase** $\phi$ of a [[#^complex-number]] $z=a+i\,b$ is the angle from the real axis,
> $$\phi=\tan^{-1}\left( \frac{b}{a} \right)$$

^phase

> [!def] Division of Complex Numbers
> Let $z_{1}=x_{1}+i\,y_{1}$, $z_{2}=x_{2}+y_{2}\,i$ each be a [[#^complex-number]].
> $$\frac{z_{1}}{z_{2}}\equiv\frac{z_{1}\,\bar{z}_{2}}{z_{2}\,\bar{z_{2}}}=\frac{z_{1}\,\bar{z}_{2}}{\lvert{z_{2}}\rvert^{2}}$$

## Polar Form

> [!thm] Euler's Formula
> Let there be a [[#^complex-number]] $a+i\,b$. The complex exponential $e^{i\theta}$ is a point in polar coordinates in the complex plane where $\cos\theta=a$, $\sin\theta=b$.
>
>
> $$e^{i\theta}=\cos\theta+i\sin\theta$$

^eulers-formula

`\begin{proof}`[[#^eulers-formula]]
The [[6.2 - Taylor Series#^ef8304]] tells us that $e^x=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\frac{1}{n!}x^{n}}$. Notice the [[6.2 - Taylor Series#^taylor-series-for-cos]] and [[6.2 - Taylor Series#^taylor-series-for-sin]].

$$
\begin{align}
e^{i\theta}&=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\frac{1}{n!}(i\,\theta)^{n}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\frac{i^{n}}{n!}\theta^{n}} \\
&=1+i\,\theta+\frac{i^{2}}{2!}\theta^{2}+\frac{i^{3}}{3!}\theta^{3}+\frac{i^{4}}{4!}\theta^{4}+\frac{i^{5}}{2}\theta^{5}+\frac{i^{6}}{6!}\theta^{6}-\frac{i^{7}}{7!}\theta^{7}+\dots \\
&=1+i\,\theta-\frac{1}{2!}\theta^{2}-\frac{i}{3!}\theta^{3}+\frac{1}{4!}\theta^{4}+\frac{i}{5!}\theta^{5}-\frac{1}{6!}\theta^{6}-\frac{i}{7!}\theta^{7}+\dots \\
&=\left( 1-\frac{\theta^{2}}{2!}+\frac{\theta^{4}}{4!}-\frac{\theta^{6}}{6!}+\dots \right)+i\left( \,\theta-\frac{\theta^{3}}{3!}+\frac{\theta^{5}}{5!}-\frac{\theta^{7}}{7!}+\dots \right) \\
\end{align}
$$

Which are the [[6.2 - Taylor Series#^taylor-series-for-cos]] and [[6.2 - Taylor Series#^taylor-series-for-sin]].
`\end{proof}`

## Algebra

> [!def] Addition of Complex Numbers
> Let $z_{1}=x_{1}+i\,y_{1}$, $z_{2}=x_{2}+y_{2}\,i$ each be a [[#^complex-number]]
> $$z_{1}+z_{2}=(x_{1}+x_{2})+i\,(y_{1}+y_{2})$$

> [!def] Multiplication of Complex Numbers
> Let $z_{1}=x_{1}+i\,y_{1}$, $z_{2}=x_{2}+y_{2}\,i$ each be a [[#^complex-number]].
> $$z_{1}z_{2}\equiv(x_{1}+i\,y_{1})(x_{2}+i\,y_{2})=x_{1}x_{2}+i\,(x_{1}y_{2}+x_{2}y_{1})-y_{1}y_{2}$$
