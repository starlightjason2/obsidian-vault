# 3.6 - Laplace Transform

> [!def] Laplace Transform
> The **Laplace transform** $\mathcal{L}:f(t)\to F(s)$ is a generalized [[3.7 - Periodic Functions#^fourier-transform]] for all values of the [[3.6 - Complex Numbers#^complex-number]] $s\in \mathbb{C}$ where the following improper integral converges.
>
> $$F(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(t)e^{-st}\,f(t)\,dt$$
>
> We write $f(t)\leftrightarrow F(s)$ as a **Laplace transform pair**.
>

^laplace-transform

> [!thm] Inverse Laplace Transform
> The [[2.5 - Linear Transformations#^585247]] of the the [[#^laplace-transform]] is $\mathcal{L}^{-1}:F(s)\to f(t)$ where
>
> $$f(t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}F(s)\,e^{-st}\,dt$$
>

^inverse-laplace-transform

## Properties of Laplace Transforms

### Linearity

> [!thm] Linearity of the Laplace Transform
> The [[#^laplace-transform]] is a [[2.5 - Linear Transformations#^linear-transformation]] $\mathcal{L}\{f(t) \}:f(t)\to F(s)$.
>
> $$\mathcal{L}\{c_{1}\,f_{1}+c_{2}\,f_{2} \}=c_{1}\,\mathcal{L}\{f_{1} \}+c_{2}\,\mathcal{L}\{f_{2} \}$$
>

^linearity-of-the-laplace-transform

> [!thm] The Inverse Laplace Transform is a Linear Transformation
> The [[#^inverse-laplace-transform]] is a [[2.5 - Linear Transformations#^linear-transformation]].
>
> $$\mathcal{L}^{-1}\{c_{1}\,f_{1}+c_{2}\,f_{2} \}=c_{1}\,\mathcal{L}^{-1}\{f_{1} \}+c_{2}\,\mathcal{L}^{-1}\{f_{2} \}$$
>

### Shifting

> [!thm] First Shifting Theorem
> Consider the [[#^laplace-transform]] pair $f(t)\leftrightarrow F(s)$ defined for $s> s_{0}$. Then for $s>s_{0}+a$, multiplying $f(t)$ by $e^{at}$ will shift $F(s)$ by $a$.
>
> $$ e^{at}f(t)\leftrightarrow F(s-a)$$

^first-shifting-theorem

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^first-shifting-theorem]]
$$
\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}\big\{e^{at}f(t)\big\}&= \int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-st}\,e^{at}f(t)\,dt \\
&= \int_{0}^{\infty}\,e^{(a-s)t}f(t)\,dt \\
\end{align}
$$
Do a $u$-sub where $u=f(t), \ du=e^{(a-s)t}\,dt$ and $v=(a-s)^{-1}\,e^{(a-s)t}, \ du=f'(t)\,dt$.
$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}\big\{e^{at}f(t)\big\}&= \frac{f(t)\,e^{(a-s)t}}{a-s}-\int v\,du \\
\end{align}$$
`\end{proof}`

> [!thm] Second Shifting Theorem
> Consider the [[#^laplace-transform]] pair $f(t)\leftrightarrow F(s)$. Multiplying $F(s)$ by $e^{at}$ will shift $f(t)$ by $a$ using the [[3.5 - Step and Delta#^ad4b33]] $\Theta(t-a)$. Note that this "deletes" any values where $t<a$.
>
> $$f(t-a)\,\Theta(t-a) \leftrightarrow e^{-sa}F(s)$$
>
> Or, equivalently, $f(t)\,\Theta(t-a) \leftrightarrow e^{-sa}F(s+a)$.


## Convolutions

> [!def] Convolution
> Let $f(t), \ g(t)$ each be a [[3.1 - Scalar Functions#^function]]. The convolution $g\ast f(t)$ is
>
> $$g\ast f(t)=\int_{0}^{t}g(\tau)\,f(t-\tau)\,d\tau=\int_{0}^{t}g(t-\tau)\,f(\tau)\,d\tau$$

^convolution

> [!thm] Convolution Theorem
> Let $f(t)\leftrightarrow F(s), \ g(t)\leftrightarrow G(s)$ each be a [[3.6 - Laplace Transform#^laplace-transform]] pair. The Laplace Transform of the [[#^convolution]] $f\ast g(t)$ is $G(s)\,F(s)$.
> $$f\ast g(t)\leftrightarrow G(s)\,F(s)$$

^convolution-theorem

## Differentiating Laplace Transforms

> [!thm] Laplace Transform of the First Derivative
> Let there be a [[#^laplace-transform]] pair $f(t) \leftrightarrow F(s)$, and let $f'(t)$ be the first [[3.5 - Derivatives#^derivative]] of $f(t)$.
> $$f'(t) \leftrightarrow s\,F(s)-f(0)$$
>

^laplace-transform-of-the-first-derivative

> [!thm] Laplace Transform of the Second Derivative
> Let there be a [[#^laplace-transform]] pair $f(t) \leftrightarrow F(s)$, and let $f'(t), \ f''(t)$ be the first and second [[3.5 - Derivatives#^derivative]] of $f(t)$.
> $$f''(t) \leftrightarrow s^{2}\,F(s)-s\,f(0)-f'(0)$$

^laplace-transform-of-the-second-derivative

>[!thm] Laplace Transform of the $n$th Derivative
>Let there be a [[#^laplace-transform]] pair $f(t) \leftrightarrow F(s)$, and let $f^{(n)}(t)$ be the $n$th [[3.5 - Derivatives#^derivative]] of $f(t)$.
>$$\mathcal{L}\{f^{(n)}(t) \}=s^{n}\,F(s)-\left( \sum_{k=1}^{n}{s^{n-k}f^{(k-1)}(0)} \right)$$

^laplace-transform-of-the-nth-derivative

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^laplace-transform-of-the-first-derivative]]
By definition, given a function $f(t)$,

$$
F(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-st}f(t)\,dt
$$
^e849ac

Integration by parts where $v=f(t), \ u=e^{-st}$ and $dv=f'(t)\,dt, \ du=-s\,e^{-st}\,dt$.

$$\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}\big\{f'(t)\big\}&=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-st}f'(t)\,dt \\
&=\left( \int e^{-st}f'(t)\,dt \right)\Bigg \vert_{0}^{\infty} \\
&=\left( e^{-st}f(t)\,+s\int f(t)\,e^{-st}\,dt \right)\Bigg \vert_{0}^{\infty}  \\
&=\Big(e^{-st}f(t)\Big)\bigg\vert_{0}^{\infty}\,+s\int_{0}^{\infty}f(t)\,e^{-st}\,dt \\ \\
\mathcal{L}\big\{f'(t)\big\}&=-f(0)\,+s\,F(s) \}
\end{align}$$
`\end{proof}`


`\begin{proof}`@[[#^laplace-transform-of-the-nth-derivative]]
Let $f^{(n-1)}(t)=g(t)$. So $\mathcal{L}\big\{f^{(n)}(t)\big\}=\mathcal{L}\big\{g'(t)\big\}$. Then, by the [[#^laplace-transform-of-the-first-derivative]], $\mathcal{L}\big\{g'(t)\big\}=s\,G(s)-g(0)$. Or,

$$
\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}\big\{f'(t)\big\}&=s\,F(s)-f(0)&n=1 \\
\mathcal{L}\big\{f^{(n)}(t)\big\}&=s\,\mathcal{L}\big\{f^{(n-1)}(t)\big\}-f^{(n-1)}(0)&n>1
\end{align}
$$

Therefore,

$$
\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}\big\{f''(t)\big\}&=s\,\big(s\,F(s)-f(0)\big)-f^{(n-1)}(0)&&=s^{2}\,F(s)-s\,f(0)-f'(0) \\
\mathcal{L}\big\{f'''(t)\big\}&=s\,\big(s^{2}\,F(s)-s\,f(0)-f'(0)\big)-f''(0)&&=s^{3}\,F(s)-s^{2}f(0)-s\,f'(0)-f''(0) \\
\end{align}
$$

And, extrapolating, 

$$
\mathcal{L}\big\{f'''(t)\big\}=s^{n}\,F(s)-s^{n-1}f(0)-s^{n-2}\,f'(0)-\dots-f^{(n-1)}(0)
$$`\end{proof}`

## Common Transforms

> [!thm] Laplace Transform Table
> A table of [[#^laplace-transform]] pairs.
>$$\begin{align}
e^{at}&\leftrightarrow \frac{1}{s-a},&&s>a \\ \\
e^{at}\,t^{n}&\leftrightarrow \frac{n!}{(s-a)^{n+1}}, && s>a\\ \\
\sin(\omega\,t) &\leftrightarrow \frac{\omega}{s^{2}+\omega^{2}},&&s>0\\ \\
\cos(\omega\,t) &\leftrightarrow \frac{s}{s^{2}+\omega^{2}},&&s>0 \\ \\
\Theta(t-a)&\leftrightarrow \frac{1}{s}\,e^{-sa} \\ \\
f(t-a)\,\Theta(t-a) &\leftrightarrow e^{-sa}F(s) \\ \\
\delta(t-a) &\leftrightarrow e^{-sa}
\end{align}$$

> [!cor] Laplace Transform of Delta Function
> The [[#^laplace-transform]] of the [[3.5 - Step and Delta#^017ca2]] $\delta(t-a)$.
> $$\delta(t-a) \leftrightarrow e^{-sa}$$

^laplace-transform-of-delta-function

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^laplace-transform-of-delta-function]]
Found by [[3.5 - Step and Delta#^integrating-the-delta-function]].
$$\mathcal{L}\{\delta(t-t_{0}) \}=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-st}\,\delta(t-t_{0})\,dt=e^{-st_{0}}$$
`\end{proof}`
