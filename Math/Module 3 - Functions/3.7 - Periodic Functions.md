# 3.7 - Periodic Functions

> [!def] $L^{2}$ Space
> The "well-behaved" periodic functions live in the $L^{2}$ [[2.7 - Function Spaces#^549a88]].

^a94a61

> [!def] Periodic Function
> A periodic [[3.1 - Scalar Functions#^f874ea]] $f(t)$ lives in the [[#^a94a61]], defined with [[3.1 - Scalar Functions#^9f9160]] for period $T$ if $f(t+T)=f(t)$ for all $t$.
>
> We commonly express periodic functions in time with angular frequency $\omega$, and spacial periodic functions with wave number $k$; in both cases, $\omega T=kT=2\pi$.
>
>
> $$\exists\,T:f(x+T)=f(x)$$
>

> [!def] Sinusoidal Function
> A sinusoidal $f(t)$ is a [[#^7a99bc]] in the form of a sine wave for amplitude $A$, angular frequency $\omega=\frac{2\pi}{T}$, and phase shift $\phi$. For $B_{1}=A\cos \phi, \ B_{2}=-A\sin \phi$, we can also express $f(t)$ as a sum of $\sin$ and $\cos$.
>
> $$f(t)=A\cos(\omega t+\phi)=B_{1}\cos \omega t+B_{2}\sin \omega t$$
>
> Using [[3.6 - Complex Numbers#^88e10c]] and the [[3.6 - Complex Numbers#^29ae56]] pair $C=\frac{1}{2}Ae^{i\phi}, \ \bar{C}=\frac{1}{2}Ae^{-i\phi}$, we can also express $f(t)$ in exponential form, or as the real part of a [[3.6 - Complex Numbers#^4b0f81]] $D=Ae^{i\phi}$.
>
> $$f(t)=Ce^{i\omega t}+\bar{C}e^{-i\omega t}=\mathrm{Re}\Big[De^{i\omega t}\Big]$$
>

^4f8b8b

`\begin{proof}`[[#^4f8b8b]]
Using the even and odd properties of $\sin$ and $\cos$,

$$
\begin{align}
x(t)=c_{1}\,e^{i\omega t}+c_{2}\,e^{-i\omega t}&=c_{1}\cos( \omega t)+c_{2}\cos (-\omega t)+i(c_{1}\sin( \omega t)+c_{2}\sin(-\omega t)) \\
&=(c_{1}+c_{2})\cos \omega t+i(c_{1}-c_{2})\sin \omega t
\end{align}
$$

In order for $x(t)$ to be real, the [[3.6 - Complex Numbers#^29ae56]] of $c_{1}=C=\alpha+\beta i$ must be $c_{2}=\bar{C}=\alpha-\beta i$ so that $c_{1}+c_{2}=2\alpha\in \mathbb{R}$ and $i(c_{1}-c_{2})=-2i^{2}\beta=2\beta$.

$$
x(t)=2\alpha \cos \omega t+2\beta \sin \omega t
$$

Then we can also write $C=D\,e^{i\phi}, \ \bar{C}=D\,e^{-i\phi}$, so $x(t)=D\,(e^{i\phi }e^{\omega t}+\,e^{-i\phi }e^{-\omega t})$ and

$$
x(t)=D(e^{i(\omega t+\phi)}+e^{-i(\omega t+\phi)})
$$

And with the identity $\cos \theta=\frac{1}{2}(e^{i\theta}+e^{-i\theta})$, for $D=\frac{A}{2}$,

$$
x(t)=A\cos(\omega t+\phi)
$$

`\end{proof}`
