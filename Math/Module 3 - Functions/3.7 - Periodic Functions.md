# 3.7 - Periodic Functions

> [!def] Periodic Function
> A periodic [[3.1 - Scalar Functions#^function]] $f(t)\in L^{2}$ lives in the [[2.7 - Function Spaces#^l2-space]] with [[4.0 - ODEs#^periodic-boundary-conditions]] for period $T$: $f(t+T)=f(t)$ for all $t$.
> $$\exists\,T:f(x+T)=f(x)$$
> We commonly express temporal periodicity with angular frequency $\omega$, and spacial periodicity with wave number $k$:
>
> $$\omega T=kT=2\pi$$

^periodic-function

> [!def] Complex Exponential
> A [[#^periodic-function]] $f(x):\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}$ in [[3.6 - Complex Numbers#^8288b7]] where $kx=2\pi$ is the wavenumber. Let $A=|A|e^{i\phi}$ be the [[3.6 - Complex Numbers#^phase]] and $|A|$ be the amplitude. Real-valued periodic functions decompose into [[3.7.1 - Sinusoidals#^sinusoidal-function|Sinusoidals]] of the same wavenumber.
> $$f(x)=A\,e^{ikx}=|A|e^{ikx+\phi}$$

^complex-exponential

## Fourier Series

> [!def] Fourier Series
> The **Fourier series** $f(t)$ is an [[5.2 - Infinite Series#^infinite-series]] of the [[#^fourier-coefficients]] $\{a_{n},b_{n}\}=\{c_{n}\}$ times the basis [[3.7 - Periodic Functions#^complex-exponential]] $\{\sin n\omega t,\cos n\omega t\}=\{e^{i\omega t}\}$ for $n\in\mathbb{Z}$.
> $$\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}c_{n}e^{in\omega t}=\frac{1}{2}a_{0}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}\cos(n\omega t)+b_{n}\sin(n\omega t)=$$

^fourier-series

> [!def] Fourier Coefficients
> The coefficients $\{a_{n}\},\{b_{n}\}$ for the [[#^fourier-series]] of any function $f(t)\in L^{2}$ are:
>
> $$
> \begin{align}
> a_{0}&=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}f(t)\,dt \\[0.5em]
> a_{n}&=\frac{2}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\cos(n\omega t)f(t)\,dt \\[0.5em]
> b_{n}&=\frac{2}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\sin(n\omega t)f(t)\,dt
> \end{align}
> $$
>
> Or, with Euler's formula, expressed as singular $\{c_{n}\}$:
> $$c_{n}=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}f(t)e^{-in\omega t}\,dt$$

^fourier-coefficients

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^fourier-coefficients]]
Multiply both sides by $e^{-in'\omega t}$ and integrate from $t=0$ to $t=T$, using a [[2.7 - Function Spaces#^functional-inner-product]] to project onto the basis:

$$\int_{0}^{T}f(t)\,e^{-in'\omega t}\,dt=\int_{0}^{T}\!\left(\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}c_{n}e^{in\omega t}\right)e^{-in'\omega t}\,dt$$

By orthogonality of the Fourier basis,

$$\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{T}c_{n}\,e^{i(n-n')\omega t}\,dt=\begin{cases}c_{n}T & n=n' \\ 0 & n\neq n'\end{cases}$$

So for $n=n'$,

$$c_{n}=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}f(t)\,e^{-in\omega t}\,dt$$
`\end{proof}`

> [!thm] Fourier's Theorem
> The [[#^fourier-series]] is a [[2.7 - Function Spaces#^complete-orthonormal-function-basis]] spanning [[2.7 - Function Spaces#^l2-space]] ($f(t)\in L^{2}$).
> $$f(t)=a_{0}+\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}a_{n}\cos(n\omega t)+b_{n}\sin(n\omega t)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}c_{n}e^{in\omega t}$$

^fouriers-theorem

## Fourier Transform

> [!def] Fourier Transform
> The Fourier Transform $\mathcal{F}$ is a [[2.8 - Linear Operators#^linear-operator]] $\mathcal{F}:f(t)\to F(\omega)$ extending the [[#^fourier-series|Fourier Series]] to non-periodic functions in the [[3.4 - Limits#^limit]] $\lim_{T\to\infty}$.
> $$\mathcal{F}\{f(x)\}=F(\omega)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-i\omega t}\,f(t)\,dt$$

^fourier-transform

> [!def] Inverse Fourier Transform
> The [[2.5 - Linear Transformations#^585247]] of the [[#^fourier-transform]] is $\mathcal{F}^{-1}:F(\omega)\to f(t)$.
> $$f(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}F(\omega)\,e^{i\omega t}\,d\omega$$

^inverse-fourier-transform

> [!thm] Fourier Inversion
> Any [[3.1 - Scalar Functions#^function]] $f(t)$ can be expressed as a frequency-dependent [[#^periodic-function]] $F(\omega)$ in the limit $\lim_{T\to\infty}$, recovered through the [[#^inverse-fourier-transform]] of its [[#^fourier-transform]].

^fourier-inversion

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^fourier-inversion]]
Take the [[#^fourier-series]] for $f(t)$ in the limit $T\to\infty$. The frequency spacing $\Delta\omega=2\pi/T\to d\omega$ and $1/T\to d\omega/(2\pi)$.

$$f(t)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\!\left(\frac{1}{T}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(t)\,e^{-in\omega t}\,dt\right)e^{in\omega t}$$

As $T\to\infty$, $n\omega\to\omega$ and the [[3.9 - Integrals#^riemann-sum]] $\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\to\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\,d\omega$.

$$f(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\,d\omega\,e^{i\omega t}\!\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}f(t)\,e^{-i\omega t}\,dt\right]$$
`\end{proof}`

> [!thm] Fourier Transform of the Derivative
> Given the [[#^fourier-transform]] $F(\omega)\leftrightarrow f(x)$, the transform of the [[3.5 - Derivatives#^derivative]] $f'(x)$ is:
> $$\mathcal{F}\!\left\{\frac{df}{dx}\right\}=i\omega F(\omega)$$

^fourier-transform-of-the-derivative

### Common Transforms

> [!example] Fourier Transform of Dirac Delta
> The [[#^fourier-transform]] of the [[3.5 - Step and Delta#^017ca2]] $\delta(t-t_{0})$ is a constant. From the integral $\mathcal{F}\{\delta(t-t_{0})\}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\delta(t-t_{0})e^{-i\omega t}dt=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-i\omega t_{0}}$.
> $$f(t)=\delta(t-t_{0})\quad\leftrightarrow\quad F(\omega)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-i\omega t_{0}}$$

^fourier-transform-of-dirac-delta

> [!example] Fourier Transform of Gaussian
> The [[#^fourier-transform]] of a Gaussian is also a Gaussian with a new $\sigma'=1/\sigma$.
> $$f(t)=e^{-t^{2}/(2\sigma^{2})}\quad\leftrightarrow\quad F(\omega)=\sigma e^{-\sigma^{2}\omega^{2}/2}$$

^fourier-transform-of-gaussian

> [!example] Exponential Decay
> The [[#^fourier-transform]] of an exponential decay gives a Lorentzian.
> $$f(t)=e^{-\alpha|t|}\quad\leftrightarrow\quad F(\omega)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{\alpha}{\alpha^{2}+\omega^{2}}$$

^fourier-transform-of-exponential-decay

> [!example] Rectangular Pulse
> The [[#^fourier-transform]] of a rectangular pulse.
> $$f(t)=\begin{cases}1 & |t|<a \\ 0 & |t|>a\end{cases}\quad\leftrightarrow\quad F(\omega)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{\sin(a\omega)}{\omega}$$

^fourier-transform-of-rectangular-pulse

> [!example] Constant Function
> The [[#^fourier-transform]] of a constant $f(t)=1$ is a Dirac delta in frequency space.
> $$f(t)=1\quad\leftrightarrow\quad F(\omega)=\sqrt{2\pi}\,\delta(\omega)$$

^fourier-transform-of-constant

> [!example] Fourier Transform of $\sin(ax)$
> Using $\sin(ax)=(e^{iax}-e^{-iax})/(2i)$ and $\mathcal{F}\{e^{iax}\}=\sqrt{2\pi}\,\delta(k-a)$:
> $$\mathcal{F}\{\sin(ax)\}=\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{2i}\big[\delta(k-a)-\delta(k+a)\big]=-i\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\big[\delta(k-a)-\delta(k+a)\big]$$
>
> In quantum mechanics, $\sin(kx)$ is a superposition of two plane waves $e^{\pm ikx}$, directly visible from the two delta functions.

^ftsin

> [!example] Fourier Transform of $\cos(ax)$
> Using $\cos(ax)=(e^{iax}+e^{-iax})/2$:
> $$\mathcal{F}\{\cos(ax)\}=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}\big[\delta(k-a)+\delta(k+a)\big]$$
>
> Consistent with the [[#^fourier-transform-of-the-derivative]]: $\mathcal{F}\{(d/dx)\sin(ax)\}=ik\cdot\mathcal{F}\{\sin(ax)\}$, which recovers $a\cdot\mathcal{F}\{\cos(ax)\}$.

^fourier-transform-of-cosax

### FFT

> [!def] Fast Fourier Transform
> Given a [[#^fourier-transform]] $f(t)\leftrightarrow F(\omega)$, the discrete Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) is:
> $$F(\omega)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2T}}\!\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}f_{k}e^{-i\omega t}\right)\!\Delta t$$

^fast-fourier-transform
