# Fundamental Theorems

We now extend the fundamental theorem of calculus to higher dimensions, and examine its implications for scalar and vector fields.

> [!thm] Fundamental Theorem of Calculus
> The [[3.9 - Integrals#^integral]]

> [!thm] Generalized Stokes Theorem
> The integral of a differential form $\omega$ over the boundary $d\Omega$ of some orientable manifold is equal to it's exterior derivative over the whole of $\Omega$.
> $$\int_{\partial \Omega}\omega=\int_{\Omega}d\omega$$

## Line Integrals

> [!thm] Fundamental Theorem of Line Integrals
> The [[3.10 - Vector Integrals#^4bd53c]] of a [[3.3 - Vector Fields#^bf3ea9]] $\mathbf{F}=\nabla f$ over a **smooth curve** $C$ represented by $\mathbf{r}(t), \ t_{0}\leq t\leq t_{f}$, evaluates to the difference in $f(\mathbf{r})$ over $C$.
>
> $$\int_{C}\nabla f\cdot d\mathbf{r}=f(\mathbf{r}(t_{0}))-f(\mathbf{r}(t_{f}))$$
>

^ecde04

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^ecde04]]
We know that $d\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{r}'(t)\,dt$. So the line integral is $\int_{C}\nabla f\cdot d\mathbf{r}=\int_{a}^{b} \nabla f\cdot \mathbf{r}'(t)\,dt$.

Let $\mathbf{r}'(t)=\left<x'(t), y'(t), z'(t)\right>$. By the definition of the gradient, we know that $\nabla f=\left<f_{x},f_{y},f_{z}\right>$. So the integral is now
$$\int_{a}^{b} \nabla f\cdot \mathbf{r}'(t)\,dt=\int_{a}^{b}\left( \frac{\partial f}{\partial x} \frac{dx}{dt}+\frac{\partial f}{\partial y} \frac{dy}{dt}+\frac{\partial f}{\partial t} \frac{dz}{dt} \right)\,dt$$
By the [[3.5 - Derivatives#^f84f43]], the integrand simplifies to $\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}=\frac{d}{dt}f$. By the fundamental theorem of calculus,

$$
\int_{a}^{b} \left( \frac{d}{dt}f(\mathbf{r}(t)) \right)\,dt=f(\mathbf{r}(b))-f(\mathbf{r}(a))
$$
`\end{proof}`

## Stokes' Theorem

> [!thm] Stokes' Theorem
> The net [[3.6 - Vector Derivatives#^5b8564]] inside a closed surface $S$, given by the [[3.10 - Vector Integrals#^302bd5]], creates the [[3.10 - Vector Integrals#^11dd4a]] at its boundary $C$.
>
> $$\oint_{C}\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}=\iint_{S}\Big(\nabla\times\mathbf{F}\Big)\cdot d\mathbf{S}$$
>

^b14c63

### Green's Theorem

> [!cor] Green's Theorem, Circulation Form
> A special case of [[#^b14c63]] in two dimensions for $\mathbf{F}(x,y)=\left<P,Q\right>$. Let the boundary $C$ enclose a **simply** [[3.8 - Regions#^7c6cc9]] $D$.
>
> Then [[3.10 - Vector Integrals#^4bd53c]] of $\mathbf{F}$ around $C$ is equal to the [[3.9 - Integrals#^b7e17f]] of the [[3.6 - Vector Derivatives#^73a6e9]] of $\mathbf{F}$ through $D$.
>
> $$\int_{C}\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}=\iint_{D}\left( \frac{\partial Q}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial P}{\partial y} \right)\,dA$$

## Divergence Theorem

> [!thm] Divergence Theorem
> The total [[3.6 - Vector Derivatives#^0bec3a]] inside a closed region $E$, given by a [[3.9 - Integrals#^94174a]], creates the [[3.10 - Vector Integrals#^302bd5]] through its boundary $S$.
>
> $$\iint_{S}\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{S}=\iiint_{E}\Big(\nabla\cdot \mathbf{F}\Big)\,dV$$
>

^0efe6a

### Green's Theorem

> [!cor] Green's Theorem, Flux Form
> A special case of the [[#^0efe6a]] in two dimensions for $\mathbf{F}(x,y)=\left<P,Q\right>$. Let the boundary $C$ enclose a [[3.8 - Regions#^7c6cc9]] $D$.
>
> The [[3.10 - Vector Integrals#^5f691a]] of $\mathbf{F}$ through $\mathbf{r}(t)$ is equal to the [[3.9 - Integrals#^b7e17f]] of the [[3.6 - Vector Derivatives#^0bec3a|Divergence]] of $\mathbf{F}$ within $D$.
> $$\int_{C}\mathbf{F}\cdot\mathbf{N}\,ds=\iint_{D}\left( \frac{\partial P}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial Q}{\partial y} \right)\,dA$$
