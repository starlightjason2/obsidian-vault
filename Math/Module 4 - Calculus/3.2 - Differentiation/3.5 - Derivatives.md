# 3.5 - Derivatives

> [!def] Derivative
> Let there be a [[3.1 - Scalar Functions#^function]] $f(x)$ that exhibits [[3.4 - Limits#^continuous-function]] for $x\in \mathbb{R}$.
>
> Consider the slope of a line intersecting $f$ at two points $x, \ x+dx$. The first derivative $f'(x)$ is the [[3.4 - Limits#^limit]] as $dx\to 0$.
>
> $$\frac{df}{dx}=\lim_{dx\to 0}{\frac{f(x+dx)-f(x)}{dx}}\quad \text{for } n = 1$$
>
> The derivative with respect to time $t$ is conventionally denoted by $df / dt=\dot{f}(x)$.

^derivative

> [!def] Exact Differential
> Let the function $f(x)$ and it's [[#^derivative]] $f'(x)$ each be [[3.4 - Limits#^continuous-function |Continuous]].
>
> The **exact differential** $df$ is $f'(x)$ times $dx$ and represents a small change in $f(x)$.
>
> $$df\equiv\frac{df}{dx}\,dx$$
>

^exact-differential

## Multivariable Functions

> [!def] Partial Derivative
> The partial derivative of a multivariable function $f(\mathbf{x})$ is the [[#^derivative]] with respect to one variable while treating the others as constant. Visualize a partial derivative as the change in elevation while walking along an input axis.
>
>
> Let $x_{i}$ be the $i$th component of $\mathbf{x}$, and let $d\mathbf{x}_{i}=\left<0, \ \dots, \ x_{i}+d x_{i}, \ \dots\right>$.
>
> $$\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}\Big(\mathbf{x}\Big)=\lim_{d x_{i}\to0}{\frac{f(\mathbf{x}+d\mathbf{x}_{i})-f(\mathbf{x})}{d x_{i}}}$$
>
> 
>
>

> [!def] Gradient
> Let $\mathbf{x}\in \mathbb{R}^{n}$. The **gradient** is the scalar product of the [[3.6 - Vector Derivatives#^del-operator]] $\nabla$ with a [[3.1 - Scalar Functions#^function]] $f(\mathbf{x})$. The resulting [[3.3 - Vector Fields#^vector-field]] points along the tangent [[3.1 - Scalar Functions#^plane]] (the path of steepest ascent) at any point $\mathbf{x}$.
>
> In any [[2.6 - Coordinate Sytems#^9cf7bf]] $(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})$,
>
> $$\nabla f(\mathbf{u})=\sum_{k=1}^{n}{}\frac{1}{h_{u_{k}}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial u_{k}}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{k}$$
>
> In [[2.6 - Coordinate Sytems#^cartesian-coordinates]] this simplifies to
>$$\nabla f(\mathbf{x})=\begin{bmatrix}
\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}} \\
\vdots \\
\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial x_{n}}
\end{bmatrix}$$
>

^5bb63d

> [!thm] Gradient is Normal to Level Curve
> The [[#^5bb63d]] of $f$ is orthogonal to the [[3.1 - Scalar Functions#^level-curves]] of $f$ at any respective point. In other words, walking along a level curve will not result in a change in height.

^gradient-is-normal-to-level-curve

> [!def] Multivariable Differential
> Let there be a function $f(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})$ where each [[3.5 - Derivatives#^partial-derivative]] is a [[3.4 - Limits#^continuous-function]].
>
> The **exact differential** $df$ is the sum of each partial derivative $\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}$ times each $dx_{i}$. This is equal to the [[2.1 - Vectors#^dot-product]] of the [[3.5 - Derivatives#^5bb63d]] with $d\mathbf{x}=dx_{1}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}_{1}+\dots+dx_{n}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}_{n}$.
>
> $$df\equiv\sum_{i=0}^{n}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{n}}\,dx_{n}=\nabla f\cdot \begin{bmatrix}
dx_{1} \\
\vdots \\
dx_{n}
\end{bmatrix}$$
>

^24b618

### Directional Derivatives

> [!def] Directional Derivative
> The **directional derivative** $D_{\mathbf{u}}$ of $f(\mathbf{x})$ is the [[2.1 - Vectors#^dot-product]] of the [[#^5bb63d]] onto some [[2.1 - Vectors#^unit-vector]] $\,\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}$. Think of it as the slope of $f$ along $\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}$.
>
> $$D_{\mathbf{u}}\,f(\mathbf{a})=\nabla f(\mathbf{x})\cdot \,\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}$$

## Derivative Properties

### Linearity

> [!thm] Linearity of the Derivative
> The [[#^derivative]] is a [[2.8 - Linear Operators#^linear-operator]].
> $$\frac{d}{dx}\Big(a\,f(x)+b\,g(x)\Big)=a\,\frac{df}{dx}+b\,\frac{dg}{dx}$$

> [!cor] Linearity of the Partial Derivative
> The [[#^partial-derivative]] is a [[2.8 - Linear Operators#^linear-operator]].
> $$\frac{\partial }{\partial x}\Big(a\,f(x)+b\,g(x)\Big)=a\,\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}+b\,\frac{\partial g}{\partial x}$$

### Product Rules

> [!thm] Product Rule
> The [[#^derivative]] of the product of two functions $f(x), \ g(x)$ is
> $$\frac{d}{dx}\Big(f+g\Big)=g(x)\, \frac{df}{dx}+f(x)\, \frac{dg}{dx}$$

> [!thm] Gradient Product Rule
> The [[#^5bb63d]] of the product of two functions $f(x), \ g(x)$ is
> $$\nabla(f+g)=g\,\nabla f+f\,\nabla g$$

### Chain Rule

> [!thm] Chain Rule
> Let there be two functions $f(x), \ g(x)$. The [[#^derivative]] of the composition $(f\circ g)(x)=f(g(x))$ is the
> $$\frac{d}{dx}\Big(f\circ g\Big)=\frac{df}{dg} \frac{dg}{dx}$$

^chain-rule

> [!thm] Generalized Chain Rule
> Let there be two functions $f(x_{1}, \, \dots, \, x_{m})$. For each input $x_{i}$, let there be a composition function $g_{i}(x)$.
>
> The [[#^partial-derivative]] $\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}$ obeys the [[#^chain-rule]] applied each term.
>
> $$\frac{\partial }{\partial x_{i}}\bigg(f\Big( g_{1}(\mathbf{t}),\dots,g_{m}(\mathbf{t})\Big)\bigg)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}{\frac{\partial f}{\partial g_{i}}\frac{\partial g_{i}}{\partial t_{i}}}$$
>

^generalized-chain-rule
