# Vector Derivatives

The idea of the derivative can be extended to vector functions like $\mathbf{r}(\mathbf{u})$. We now define what a vector derivative like $\frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial \mathbf{u}}$ means, and how to find $d\mathbf{r}$.

> [!def] Vector Derivative
> Let there a [[3.2 - Vector Functions#^c8839e]] $\mathbf{r}(\mathbf{u})=\left<f_{1}(\mathbf{u}),\dots,f_{m}(\mathbf{u})\right>$ where $\mathbf{u}=\left<u_{1},\dots u_{n}\right>$.
>
> The **vector derivative** ${\partial \mathbf{r}}/{\partial \mathbf{u}}$ is called the **Jacobian** $\mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{r}}$ and is defined as the $m\times n$ [[2.2 - Matrices#^5bf009]] of each possible [[3.5 - Derivatives#^7ced7c]].
>
> $$
> \mathbf{J}_{\mathbf{r}}=\frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial \mathbf{u}}=\begin{bmatrix}
\dfrac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial u_{1}} & \dots & \dfrac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial u_{n}}
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}
\dfrac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial u_{1}} & \dots & \dfrac{\partial f_{m}}{\partial u_{n}} \\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\
\dfrac{\partial f_{m}}{\partial u_{1}} & \dots & \dfrac{\partial f_{m}}{\partial u_{n}}
\end{bmatrix}
> $$
> 
>

^483301

> [!def] Vector Differential
> 
>
> The vector differential $d\mathbf{r}=\frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial \mathbf{u}}d\mathbf{u}$ is equal to the [[2.2 - Matrices#^7f9d75]] of the [[3.6 - Vector Derivatives#^483301]] ${\partial \mathbf{r}}/{\partial \mathbf{u}}$ and $d\mathbf{u}$. Furthermore, $d\mathbf{r}$ is also the sum of all $h_{k}\,du_{k}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{k}$, where $h_{k}$ is the [[2.6 - Coordinate Sytems#^6287a0]] for $u_{k}$.
>
> $$d\mathbf{r}=\mathbf{J}\,d\mathbf{u}=\sum_{k=0}^{3}{h_{k}\,du_{k}}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{k}$$
>

^a65df2




We combine partial derivatives into the del operator, the building block needed to differentiate **scalar** and **vector fields**. The three ways to multiply a vector are **scalar multiples**, **dot products**, and **cross products**, and each produces a type of derivative when used with del.

> [!def] Del Operator
> The **del operator** $\nabla$ is a [[2.7 - Function Spaces#^3fa82b]]. In any [[2.6 - Coordinate Sytems#^9cf7bf]] $(u_{1},\dots,u_{n})$, each component of $\nabla$ contains a [[3.5 - Derivatives#^7ced7c]] $\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{k}}$ adjusted by the [[2.6 - Coordinate Sytems#^6287a0]] $h_{u_{k}}$.
>
> $$\nabla=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{\frac{1}{h_{u_{k}}}\,\frac{\partial }{\partial u_{k}}}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{k}$$
>

## Divergence

> [!def] Divergence
> Divergence, denoted by $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r})$, is the **outflow** of a [[3.3 - Vector Fields#^3ea3bd]] $\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r})$.
>
> Let $R$ be a [[3.8 - Regions#^b5157c]] containing $\mathbf{r}$. Let $\lvert{R}\rvert\,$ be the volume of $R$, and let surface $S$ be the boundary of $R$. The divergence at $\mathbf{r}$ is defined as the [[3.10 - Vector Integrals#^302bd5]] $\Phi_{S}$ of $\mathbf{F}$ through $S$ in the [[3.4 - Limits#^1a2846]] of $\lvert{R}\rvert\to 0$.
> $$div(\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r}))\equiv\lim_{\lvert{R}\rvert\,\to{\mathbf{0}}}{\left[ \frac{1}{\lvert{R}\rvert\,}\iint_{S}\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{A} \right]}$$

^0bec3a

> [!thm] Divergence in Orthogonal Coordinates
> The [[#^0bec3a]] $\nabla\cdot \mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r})$ in an orthogonal coordinate system $(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})$ with [[2.6 - Coordinate Sytems#^6287a0]] $h_{1}, \ h_{2}, \  h_{3}$ is
>
> $$\nabla\cdot\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r})=\frac{1}{h_{1}h_{2}h_{3}}\left( \frac{\partial }{\partial u_{3}}\Big[h_{1}\,h_{2}\,R\Big]+\frac{\partial }{\partial  u_{2}}\Big[h_{1}\,h_{3}\,Q\Big] + \frac{\partial }{\partial u_{1}}\Big[h_{2}\,h_{3}\,P\Big] \right)$$
>
>

^1b7f93

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^1b7f93]]
Let there be a vector field $\mathbf{F}=F_1\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{1}+F_2\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{2}+F_3\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{3}$ where $F_1, \ F_2, \ F_3$ are functions of $(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})$.

Consider a cubic region with side lengths $d\mathbf{u}_{1}=h_{1}\,du_{1}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{1}, \ d\mathbf{u}_{2}=h_{2}\,du_{2}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{2}, d\mathbf{u}_{3}=h_{3}\,du_{3}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{3}$, found with [[2.6 - Coordinate Sytems#^08069b]].

The normal vectors of the *opposing* faces will be *antiparallel* and otherwise identical. Let the [[3.2 - Vector Functions#^89d52f]] $d\mathbf{A}_{i}$ denote one such pair. Define $\Phi_{i}$ through the faces at $\mathbf{r}+d\mathbf{u}_{i}$ as *positive*.

$$
\begin{align}
d\mathbf{A}_{1}&=h_{1}\,h_{2}\,du_{1}\,du_{2}\,(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{1}\times \hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{2})&=h_{1}\,h_{2}\,du_{1}\,du_{2}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{3} \\
d\mathbf{A}_{2}&=h_{1}\,h_{3}\,du_{1}\,du_{3}\,(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{1}\times \hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{3})&=h_{1}\,h_{3}\,du_{1}\,du_{3}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{2} \\
d\mathbf{A}_{3}&=h_{2}\,h_{3}\,du_{2}\,du_{3}\,(\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{2}\times \hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{3})&=h_{2}\,h_{3}\,du_{2}\,du_{3}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{1}
\end{align}
$$

^81537a

By definition, the divergence is the net flux over the volume $dV=h_{1}h_{2}h_{3}\,du_{1}\,du_{2}\,du_{3}$.
$$
\nabla\cdot \mathbf{F}=\frac{\sum\Phi_{}}{dV}=\frac{1}{dV}\sum_{i=0}^{3}\iint \Big(\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r}+d\mathbf{u}_{i})-\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r})\Big)\cdot d\mathbf{A}_{i}
$$

^492551

Plug [[#^81537a]] into [[#^492551]].

$$
\begin{align}
\sum\Phi=&\iint h_{1}\,h_{2}\,\Big(F_3(\mathbf{r}+d\mathbf{u}_{3})-F_3(\mathbf{r})\Big)\,du_{1}\,du_{2}\\ 

+&\iint h_{1}\,h_{3}\,\Big(F_2(\mathbf{r}+d\mathbf{u}_{2})-F_2(\mathbf{r})\Big)\,du_{1}\,du_{3} \\ 

+&\iint h_{2}\,h_{3}\,\Big(F_1(\mathbf{r}+d\mathbf{u}_{1})-F_1(\mathbf{r})\Big)\,du_{2}\,du_{3}
\end{align}
$$

^e90bfb

Simplify [[#^e90bfb]] using the limit definition of the [[3.5 - Derivatives#^7ced7c]].

$$
\begin{align}
{h_{2}\,h_{3}\,F_1(\mathbf{r}+d\mathbf{u}_{1})-h_{2}\,h_{3}\,F_1(\mathbf{r})}&= \frac{\partial }{\partial u_{1}}\Big[h_{2}\,h_{3}\,F_1(\mathbf{r})\Big]\,du_{1} \\  

{h_{1}\,h_{3}\,F_2(\mathbf{r}+d\mathbf{u}_{2})-h_{1}\,h_{3}\,F_2(\mathbf{r})}&= \frac{\partial }{\partial u_{2}}\Big[h_{1}\,h_{3}\,F_2(\mathbf{r})\Big]\,du_{2}\\ 
 
{h_{1}\,h_{2}\,F_3(\mathbf{r}+d\mathbf{u}_{3})-h_{1}\,h_{2}\,F_3(\mathbf{r})}&= \frac{\partial }{\partial u_{3}}\Big[h_{1}\,h_{2}\,F_3(\mathbf{r})\Big]\,du_{3}
\end{align}
$$

^46e879

Substitute [[#^46e879]] into [[#^492551]].

$$
\begin{align}
\nabla\cdot\mathbf{F}&=\iiint \frac{\left( \,\dfrac{\partial (h_{1}\,h_{2}\,R)}{\partial u_{3}}+\dfrac{\partial (h_{1}\,h_{3}\,Q)}{\partial  u_{2}} + \dfrac{\partial (h_{2}\,h_{3}\,P)}{\partial u_{1}} \right)}{h_{1}h_{2}h_{3}\,\cancel{du_{1}du_{2}\,du_{3}}}\,\cancel{du_{1}\,du_{2}\,du_{3}}
\\ \\
&=\frac{1}{h_{1}h_{2}h_{3}}\left( \frac{\partial }{\partial u_{3}}\Big[h_{1}\,h_{2}\,R\Big]+\frac{\partial }{\partial  u_{2}}\Big[h_{1}\,h_{3}\,Q\Big] + \frac{\partial }{\partial u_{1}}\Big[h_{2}\,h_{3}\,P\Big] \right)
\end{align}
$$
`\end{proof}`

> [!cor] Divergence in Cartesian
> In [[2.6 - Coordinate Sytems#^000d2e]], the [[#^0bec3a]] of $\mathbf{F}$ evaluates to the [[2.1 - Vectors#^b212ec]] of the [[#^8fde69]] $\nabla$ with $\mathbf{F}$.
> $$\nabla\cdot\mathbf{F}=\frac{\partial \mathbf{F}}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial \mathbf{F}}{\partial y}+\frac{\partial \mathbf{F}}{\partial z}$$

## Curl

> [!def] Curl
> Curl, denoted by $\nabla\times\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r})$, is the **spin** of a [[3.3 - Vector Fields#^3ea3bd]] $\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r})$.
>
> Let $R$ be a [[3.8 - Regions#^6b61a4]] containing $\mathbf{r}$, with a [[3.2 - Vector Functions#^085769]] $\hat{\boldsymbol{n}}\in \mathbb{R}^{3}$. Let $\lvert{R}\rvert\,$ be the area of $R$, and a curve $C$ be the boundary of $R$. The curl at $\mathbf{r}$ is defined as the [[3.10 - Vector Integrals#^11dd4a]] of $\mathbf{F}$ around $C$ in the [[3.4 - Limits#^1a2846]] of $\lvert{R}\rvert\to 0$, oriented with $\hat{\boldsymbol{n}}$.
> $$\Big(\nabla \times\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r})\Big)\cdot \hat{\boldsymbol{n}}=\lim_{\lvert{R}\rvert\,\to\infty} \frac{1}{\lvert{R}\rvert\,} {\oint \mathbf{F}}\cdot d\mathbf{r}$$

^5b8564

> [!thm] Curl in Orthogonal Coordinates
> The [[#^5b8564]] of $\mathbf{F}$ in an orthogonal coordinate system $(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})$ with [[2.6 - Coordinate Sytems#^6287a0]] $h_{1}, \ h_{2}, \  h_{3}$ is
>
> $$\nabla \times \mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r})=\frac{1}{h_{1}h_{2}h_{3}}\begin{vmatrix}
h_{2}\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{1} & h_{3}\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{2} & h_{1}\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{3} \\
{\partial }/{\partial u_{1}} & {\partial }/{\partial u_{2}} & {\partial }/{\partial u_{3}} \\
h_{1}F_1 & h_{2}F_2 & h_{3}F_3
\end{vmatrix}$$
>

^066679

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^066679]]
Let there be a vector field $\mathbf{F}=F_1\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{1}+F_2\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{2}+F_3\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{3}$ where $F_1, \ F_2, \ F_3$ are functions of $(u_{1},u_{2},u_{3})$.

Recall [[2.6 - Coordinate Sytems#^08069b]] is $d\mathbf{r}=h_{1}\,du_{1}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{1}+h_{2}\,du_{2}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{2}+h_{3}\,du_{3}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{3}$. Consider square regions in the $u_{1}u_{2}, \ u_1u_{3}, u_{2}u_{3}\ u$ planes. Circulation along the opposite sides of each square will have opposing signs.

$$
\begin{align}
\sum_{u_{1}u_{2}}\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}&=\Big(F_2(\mathbf{r}+d\mathbf{u}_{1})-F_2(\mathbf{r})\Big)\,h_{2}\, du_{2}-\Big(F_1(\mathbf{r}+d\mathbf{u}_{2})-F_1(\mathbf{r})\Big)\,h_{1}\, du_{1} \\ 
\sum_{u_{1}u_{3}}\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}&=\Big(F_3(\mathbf{r}+d\mathbf{u}_{1})-F_3(\mathbf{r})\Big)\,h_{3}\, du_{3}-\Big(F_1(\mathbf{r}+d\mathbf{u}_{3})-F_1(\mathbf{r})\Big)\,h_{1}\, du_{1} \\ 
\sum_{u_{2}u_{3}}\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}&=\Big(F_3(\mathbf{r}+d\mathbf{u}_{2})-F_3(\mathbf{r})\Big)\,h_{3}\, du_{3}-\Big(F_2(\mathbf{r}+d\mathbf{u}_{3})-F_2(\mathbf{r})\Big)\,h_{2}\, du_{2}
\end{align}
$$

^3685b9

Simplify [[#^3685b9]] using the limit definition of the [[3.5 - Derivatives#^7ced7c]].

$$
\begin{align}
\sum_{u_{1}u_{2}}\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}&=\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{1}}\Big[F_2\,h_{2}\Big]\, du_{2}\,du_{1}-\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{2}}\Big[F_1\,h_{1}\Big]\, du_{1}\,du_{2}\\
\sum_{u_{1}u_{3}}\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}&=\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{1}}\Big[F_3\,h_{3}\Big]\, du_{3}\,du_{1}-\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{3}}\Big[F_1\,h_{1}\Big]\, du_{1}\,du_{3}\\
\sum_{u_{2}u_{3}}\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}&=\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{2}}\Big[F_3\,h_{3}\Big]\, du_{3}\,du_{2}-\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{3}}\Big[F_2\,h_{2}\Big]\, du_{2}\,du_{3}\\
\end{align}
$$

^fa982e

The net circulation found in [[#^fa982e]] evaluates to the following determinant.

$$
\begin{align}
\frac{1}{h_{1}h_{2}h_{3}}\begin{vmatrix}
h_{1}\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{1} & h_{2}\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{2} & h_{3}\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{3} \\
{\partial }/{\partial u_{1}} & {\partial }/{\partial u_{2}} & {\partial }/{\partial u_{3}} \\
h_{1}F_1 & h_{2}F_2 & h_{3}F_3
\end{vmatrix}&=
\left( \frac{\partial}{\partial u_{1}}\Big[F_2\,h_{2}\Big]-\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{2}}\Big[F_1\,h_{1}\Big]\right)\,du_{1}\,du_{2} \\
&+\left( \frac{\partial}{\partial u_{1}}\Big[F_3\,h_{3}\Big]-\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{3}}\Big[F_1\,h_{1}\Big] \right)\,du_{1}\,du_{3} \\
&+ \left( \frac{\partial}{\partial u_{2}}\Big[F_3\,h_{3}\Big]-\frac{\partial }{\partial u_{3} }\Big[F_3\,h_{2}\Big] \right)\,du_{2}\,du_{3}
\end{align}
$$

`\end{proof}`

> [!cor] Curl in Cartesian
> Let there be a a [[3.3 - Vector Fields#^3ea3bd]] $\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r})={F_1(\mathbf{r})\,\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{1}+F_2(\mathbf{r})\,\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{2}+F_3(\mathbf{r})\,\hat{\boldsymbol{u}}_{3}}$ and let $\mathbf{F}\in \mathbb{R}^{3},\ \mathbf{r}\in \mathbb{R}^{3}$.
>
> **Curl** is the [[2.1 - Vectors#^982dd9]] of the [[#^8fde69]] $\nabla$ with $\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r})$. The resulting **vector field** points along the **axis of rotation** of $\mathbf{F}$ around any point $\mathbf{r}$. By convention, positive curl is defined with the *right hand rule*.
>
> $$    \nabla \times \mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r})=\left( \frac{\partial F_3}{\partial y}-\frac{\partial F_2}{\partial z}\right)\,\hat{\boldsymbol{x}}+\\\left( \frac{\partial F_3}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial F_1}{\partial z} \right)\,\hat{\boldsymbol{y}}+\\{\left( \frac{\partial F_2}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial F_1}{\partial y} \right)}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{z}}$$
>

^f6fc21

> [!cor] 2D Curl in Cartesian
> [[#^f6fc21]] for a **two-dimensional field** $\mathbf{F}(x,y)=\left<P,Q\right>$ is
>
> $$\nabla \times \mathbf{F}(x,y) =\left( \dfrac{\partial F_2}{\partial x}-\dfrac{\partial F_1}{\partial y} \right)\,\hat{\boldsymbol{k}}$$

>
