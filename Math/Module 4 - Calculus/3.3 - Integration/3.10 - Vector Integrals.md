# 3.3.3 - Vector Integrals

We can either integrate
- a scalar function over a vector function _(line and surface integrals)_,
- a vector field over a vector function _(flow and flux)_.

## Scalar Functions

### Line Integral

> [!def] Scalar Line Integral
> Given a curve $\mathbf{r}$ and a [[3.1 - Scalar Functions#^function]] $f$, a **scalar line integral** is the integral of $f$ with respect to the [[2.1 - Vectors#^57eeb7]] of the [[3.2 - Vector Functions#^curve-differential]] $d\mathbf{r}$, evaluated along $\mathbf{r}$.
>
> Visualize this as the **area** of some **curtain** in $\mathbb{R}^{3}$ from $f(x,y)$ to $\mathbf{r}(t)$.
>
> $\int_{C}f(\dots)\,\lVert{d\mathbf{r}}\rVert\,=\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{f}}f(\mathbf{r}(t))\left\lVert {\frac{d\mathbf{r}}{dt}} \right\rVert\,dt$

^scalar-line-integral

### Surface Integral

> [!def] Scalar Surface Integral
> A [[3.9 - Integrals#^double-integral]] of $f(x,y,z)$ over the [[3.2 - Vector Functions#^surface-area]] of a [[3.2 - Vector Functions#^645bcd]] $S$ represented by $\mathbf{r}(t,u)$ where $(t,u)\in D$.
>
> Equivalent to a double integral in curvilinear coordinates $(u,v)$, where the [[2.6 - Coordinate Sytems#^jacobian-determinant|Jacobian]] is $\mathbf{J}=d\mathbf{A}$.
>
> $$\iint_{S}f\Big(x,y,z\Big)\,dA=\iint_{D}f\Big(\mathbf{r}(t,u)\Big)\left\lVert {\frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial t}\times \frac{\partial \mathbf{r}}{\partial s}} \right\rVert\,dt\,du$$
>

^scalar-surface-integral

## Vector Fields

### Line Integrals

> [!def] Vector Line Integral
> Let there be a curve $\mathbf{r}$ and a [[3.3 - Vector Fields#^vector-field]] $\mathbf{F}$. A **vector line integral** is the integral of the [[2.1 - Vectors#^dot-product]] of $\mathbf{F}$ with the [[3.2 - Vector Functions#^curve-differential]] $d\mathbf{r}$, evaluated along $\mathbf{r}$.
>
> Also called the **vector flow** because it quantizes how much $\mathbf{F}$ flows along $\mathbf{r}$.
>
> $\int_{C}\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}=\int_{t_{0}}^{t_{f}}\left( \mathbf{F}\Big(\mathbf{r}(t)\Big)\cdot \frac{d\mathbf{r}}{dt} \right)\,dt
>

^vector-line-integral

> [!def] Circulation
> The [[#^vector-line-integral]] along an **oriented and closed** curve $C$.
> $$\oint \mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}$$

^circulation

> [!thm] Properties of Vector Line Integrals
> The following properties hold for a [[#^vector-line-integral]] over a smooth curve $C$ described by $\mathbf{r}(t)$. Let $\mathbf{F}$ and $\mathbf{G}$ be **vector fields**.
>
> 1. $$\int_{C}(\mathbf{F}+\mathbf{G})\cdot d\mathbf{r}=\int_{C}\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}+\int_{C}\mathbf{G}\cdot d\mathbf{r}$$
> 2. $$\int_{C}k(\mathbf{F})\cdot d\mathbf{r}=k\int_{C}\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}$$
> 3. $$\int_{-C}\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}=-\int_{C}\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{r}$$

### Vector Flux

> [!def] Vector Flux
> The **flux** of a [[3.3 - Vector Fields#^vector-field]] $\mathbf{F}$ through surface $S$ is the [[#^scalar-surface-integral]] of the [[2.1 - Vectors#^dot-product]] of $\mathbf{F}$ with the [[3.2 - Vector Functions#^surface-differential]] $d\mathbf{A}$. This quantifies how much $\mathbf{F}$ flows through $S$.
>
> Use [[2.6 - Coordinate Sytems#^dmathbfr-in-orthogonal-coordinates]] to find orthogonal differentials $d\mathbf{r}_{1}$ and $d\mathbf{r}_{2}$ on $S$.
>
> $$
> \begin{align}
>  \iint_{S}\mathbf{F}\cdot d\mathbf{A}&=\iint_{S}\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{r})\cdot (d\mathbf{r}_{1}\times d\mathbf{r}_{2})
> \end{align}
> $$
>
>

^vector-flux

> [!def] 2D Flux
> [[#^vector-flux]] for a **two-dimensional field** $\mathbf{F}(x,y)=\left<P,Q\right>$.
> $$\int_{C}\mathbf{F}\cdot\mathbf{N}\,dA=\int_{a}^{b}\mathbf{F}\Big(\mathbf{r}(t)\Big)\cdot \begin{bmatrix}
 y'(t)\\-x'(t)
\end{bmatrix}\,dt$$
