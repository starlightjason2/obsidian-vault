# 4.0 - ODEs

> [!def] ODE
> An $n$th *rank* ordinary differential equation (ODE) is an equation containing derivatives up to the [[3.7 - Multiple Derivatives#^nth-derivative]].
>
> Let $y^{(i)}(x)$ be the $i$th [[3.5 - Derivatives#^derivative]] of $y(x)$.
> $$\sum_{i=0}^{n}{p_{i}(x,y)\,y^{(i)}(x)}=f(x,y)$$

^ode

## Separable ODEs

> [!def] Separable ODE
> A **separable** [[4.0 - ODEs#^ode]] has a product solution $y(x)$
>
> $$g(y)\, \frac{dy}{dx}=f(x)$$
>
>

^separable-ode

### General Solution

> [!def] Implicit Solution
> An **implicit solution** of a [[#^separable-ode]] is an equation in $x$ and $y$. The solution comes from the **differential form** of the equation, with $dx$ and $dy$ separated on either side.
>
> $$\int g(y)\,dy=\int f(x)\,dx$$
>

^implicit-solution

## Initial Value Problems

> [!def] Initial Value Problem
> Consider an [[#^ode]] of rank $n$ involving derivatives of $y(x)$.
>
> An initial value problem of rank $n$ has specified **initial values** $(x_{k},y_{k})$ for each $k$th order derivative where $k\in[0,n-1]$.
>
> $$\Big\{ \big(x_{k},f^{(k)}(x_{k})\big) \Big\}=\{(x_{0},y_{0}, \ \dots \ , \{x_{n-1},y_{n-1} \} \}$$
>
>

^initial-value-problem

> [!thm] Uniqueness and Existence Theorem
> Let there be an [[#^initial-value-problem]] $y'=f(x,y), \ y(x_{0})=y_{0}$.
>
> Consider the region $R:\{a<x<b, \ c<y<d \}$ where $(x_{0}\\,y_{0})\in R$.
>
> 1. If a function $f$ exhibits [[3.4 - Limits#^continuous-function]] on $R$, , then the IVP has at **least one solution** on some $x_{0}\in(a,b)$.
> 2. If $f$ and its [[3.5 - Derivatives#^partial-derivative]] $f_{y}$ exhibit continuity on $R$, then the IVP has a **unique solution** on some $x_{0}\in(a,b)$.

## Boundary Value Problems

> [!def] Boundary Value Problem
> An [[#^ode]] with conditions specified at the extremes of the domain. For a problem on $[a,b]$, the solution must satisfy constraints at both $x=a$ and $x=b$.

^boundary-value-problem

### Types of Boundary Conditions

> [!def] Homogeneous Boundary Conditions
> **Homogeneous boundary conditions** are the [[#^boundary-value-problem|Boundary Value Problem]] constraints on a [[3.1 - Scalar Functions#^function]] $y(x)$ at $x=a$ and $x=b$ with zero on the right-hand side. The conditions may also include $y'(x)$.
> $$\begin{align}
y(a)&=0 \\
y(b)&=0
\end{align}$$

^2f00e2

> [!def] Dirichlet Boundary Conditions
> [[#^2f00e2]] that specify the value of $y(x)$ at each endpoint:
> $$y(a)=0, \qquad y(b)=0$$

^dirichlet-boundary-conditions

> [!def] Neumann Boundary Conditions
> [[#^2f00e2]] that specify the value of $y'(x)$ at each endpoint:
> $$y'(a)=0, \qquad y'(b)=0$$

^neumann-boundary-conditions

> [!def] Robin Boundary Conditions
> [[#^2f00e2]] where a [[2.1 - Vectors#^linear-combination]] of $y(x)$ and $y'(x)$ vanishes at each endpoint:
> $$\alpha\, y(a)+\beta\, y'(a)=0, \qquad \alpha\, y(b)+\beta\, y'(b)=0$$

^robin-boundary-conditions

> [!def] Periodic Boundary Conditions
> [[#^2f00e2]] where $y(x)$ and $y'(x)$ match at both endpoints:
> $$y(a)=y(b), \qquad y'(a)=y'(b)$$

^periodic-boundary-conditions
