# 4.1.1 - First Order Linear ODEs

> [!def] 1st Order Linear ODE
> A **first order** [[4.1.0 - Linear ODEs#^linear-ode]] is of the following form, where $y(x)$ and $p(x)$ are functions of $x$.
> $$y'+p(x)\,y(x)=f(x)$$

^1st-order-linear-ode

> [!def] Homogeneous 1st Order Linear ODE
> A [[#^1st-order-linear-ode]] that is also a [[4.1.0 - Linear ODEs#^linear-homogeneous-ode]].

^homogeneous-1st-order-linear-ode

## Homogeneous Solution

> [!thm] Homogeneous Solution of a First Order Linear ODE
> Consider a [[#^homogeneous-1st-order-linear-ode]] $y'(x)+p(x)\,y(x)=0$.
>
> The general solution $y(x)$ is of the form,
> $$y=Ce^{-\int p(x)}$$
>

^homogeneous-solution-of-a-first-order-linear-ode

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^homogeneous-solution-of-a-first-order-linear-ode]]
Convert the equation into a [[4.0 - ODEs#^separable-ode]] and solve.

$$
\begin{align}
\frac{dy}{dx}+p(x)\,y&=0 \\
\int \frac{1}{y}\,dy&=-\int p(x)\,dx\\
\ln \lvert{y}\rvert+C_{1}&=-\int p(x)\,dx\,\\
e^{C_{1}}\,y&=\int p(x)\,dx\\ \\
y(x)&=C\,e^{-\int p(x)\,dx}
\end{align}
$$
`\end{proof}`

## General Solution

> [!thm] General Solution of a First Order Linear ODE
> Let there be a [[#^1st-order-linear-ode]].
>
> $$y'+p(x)\,y=f(x)$$
>
> The general solution $y(x)$ can be found by multiplying both sides by $e^{\int p(x)\,dx}$, creating a [[4.0 - ODEs#^separable-ode]] that can more easily be solved with a $u$-substitution where $u=y\,e^{\int p(x)\,dx}$.
>
> $$
> y(x)=e^{-\int p(x)\,dx}\left( \int f(x)\,e^{\int p(x)\,dx}\,dx \right)
> $$

^general-solution-of-a-first-order-linear-ode

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^general-solution-of-a-first-order-linear-ode]]
Multiply both sides of the equation by $e^{\int p(x)\,dx}$, a trick which allows us to use the product rule backwards.

$$
\begin{align}
\frac{dy}{dx}\,e^{\int p(x)\,dx}+p(x)\,e^{\int p(x)\,dx}\,y&=f(x)\,e^{\int p(x)\,dx} \\
\frac{d}{dx}(y\,e^{\int p(x)\,dx})&=f(x)\,e^{\int p(x)\,dx}
\end{align}
$$

Solve this with a $u$-substitution where $u=y\,e^{\int p(x)\,dx}$.

$$
\begin{align}
\frac{du}{dx}&=f(x)\,e^{\int p(x)\,dx} \\
\int du&=\int f(x)\,e^{\int p(x)\,dx}\,dx \\
y\,e^{\int p(x)\,dx}&=\int f(x)\,e^{\int p(x)\,dx}\,dx \\ \\
y&=e^{-\int p(x)\,dx}\,\int f(x)\,e^{\int p(x)\,dx}\,dx
\end{align}
$$
 `\end{proof}`

For systems of first order linear ODEs, see [[4.1.5 - First Order Systems]].
