# 4.1.3 - Constant Coefficient ODEs

> [!def] 2nd Order Constant Coeffecient ODE
> A constant coeffecient [[4.1.2 - Second Order Linear ODEs#^2e2986]] is of the form
>
> $$a\,y''+b\,y'+c\,y=f(t)$$

^f50998

> [!def] Homogeneous 2nd Order Constant Coefficient ODE
> A [[#^f50998]] that is also a [[4.1.0 - Linear ODEs#^a5e477]].
> $$a\,y''+b\,y'+c\,y=0$$

^ca6301

> [!def] Characteristic Polynomial
> Given a [[#^f50998]], the **characteristic polynomial** $p_{c}$ is constructed from the constant factors.
>
> $$p_{c}(r)\equiv a\,r^{2}+b\,r+c$$

^a66ce5

## Homogeneous Solution

> [!thm] Homogeneous Solution to 2nd Order Constant Coefficient ODE
> Let $r_{1},\,r_{2}$ be roots of the [[#^a66ce5]] of a [[#^ca6301]], found with the quadratic formula.
>
> $$r_{1,2}=\begin{cases}
-\frac{b\pm \sqrt{b^{2}-4ac}}{2a}, & b^{2}>4ac \\[0.5em]
-\frac{b}{2a}, & b^{2}=4ac \\[0.5em]
-\frac{b\pm i\sqrt{4ac-b^{2}}}{2a}, & b^{2}<4ac \\[0.5em]
\end{cases}$$
>
> The [[4.1.0 - Linear ODEs#^9d5987]] are $\{y_{1}=e^{r_{1}t}, \ y_{2}=e^{r_{2}t} \}$ for two roots $r_{1}\neq r_{2}$, or $\{y_{1}=e^{r_{1}t}, \ y_{2}=t\,e^{r_{1}t} \}$ for one root $r_{1}=r_{2}$. So for any $(c_{1},c_{2})\in \mathbb{R}$,
>
> $$y_{h}(t)= \begin{cases}  
c_{1}\,e^{rt}+c_{2}\,t\,e^{rt}, & (r_{1}=r_{2}=r)\in \mathbb{R} \\
 c_{1}\,e^{r_{1}t}+c_{2}\,e^{r_{2}t}, & (r_{1}\neq r_{2})\in \mathbb{C}
\end{cases}$$
>
>
>
>
>

^382f29

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^382f29]]
Start by guessing a solution of the form $y=e^{rx}$. Then $y'=r\,e^{rx}$ and $y''=r^{2}\,e^{rx}$. We can plug $y_{1}, \ y_{2}$ into the ODE to get the characteristic equation [[#^c2e4e6]].

$$
a\,r^{2}+b\,r+c=0
$$

^c2e4e6

Now use the [[4.1.0 - Linear ODEs#^9d5987]] for $\{e^{r_{1}t},e^{r_{2}t} \}$.

If there is one real root, consider $y_{1}=e^{rt}, \ y_{2}=t\,e^{rt}$.

$$
\begin{align}
\begin{vmatrix}
y_{1} & y_{2} \\
y_{1}' & y_{2}'
\end{vmatrix}&=(e^{rt})(e^{rt}+t\,re^{rt})-(t\,e^{rt})(r\,e^{rt}) \\
&=e^{2rt}+\cancel{t\,re^{2rt}-t\,r\,e^{2rt}}\\
\end{align}
$$

$e^{t}>0 \implies e^{2rt}>0$, so $\{e^{rt},t\,e^{rt} \}$ is a fundamental set for *one unique root*.

If there are two roots, consider $y_{1}=e^{r_{1}t}, \ y_{2}=e^{r_{2}t}$.

$$
\begin{vmatrix}
y_{1} & y_{2} \\
y_{1}' & y_{2}'
\end{vmatrix}=(\,e^{r_{1}t})\,(r_{2}\,e^{r_{2}t})-(\,e^{r_{2}t})(r_{1}\,e^{r_{1}t})=\,(r_{1}-r_{2})\,e^{(r_{1}+r_{2})t}
$$

Notice $r_{1}\neq r_{2}\implies W(t)\neq 0$, so $\{e^{r_{1}t},e^{r_{2}t} \}$ is a fundamental set for *two unique roots*.

`\end{proof}`

> [!thm] 2nd Order Homogeneous Constant Coefficient IVP
> Consider an [[4.0 - ODEs#^520eb9]] for a [[#^ca6301]].
>
>$$a\,y''+b\,y'+c\,y=0, \quad \begin{align}
y(0)&=k_{0}, \\ y'(0)&=k_{1}
\end{align}$$
>
> The general solution involves three cases for the roots of the [[#^a66ce5]],
>$$y_{h}(t)= \begin{cases}  
e^{rt}\left(k_{0}+\left( \dfrac{k_{1}-k_{0}\,r}{r} \right)\,t\right) & (r_{1}=r_{2}=r)\in \mathbb{R} \\[0.5em]
 \left( \dfrac{k_{1}-k_{0}\,r_{2}}{r_{1}-r_{2}} \right)\,e^{r_{1}t}+\left( \dfrac{k_{0}\,r_{1}-k_{1}}{r_{1}-r_{2}} \right)\,e^{r_{2}t} & (r_{1}\neq r_{2})\in \mathbb{C}
\end{cases}$$

^ec5b97

`\begin{proof}`[[#^ec5b97]]
For each case of the [[#^382f29|Homogenous Solution]], substitute $y(0)=k_{0}, \ y'(0)=k_{1}$,

For the case of one root, $y_{h}'(t)=c_{1}\,r\,e^{rt}+c_{2}\,e^{rt}(rt+1)$ and so,

$$
\begin{align}
k_{0}=y_{h}(0)&=c_{1}\,e^{0}+c_{2}\,(0)\,e^{0}&&=c_{1} \\
k_{1}=y_{h}'(0)&= c_{1}\,r\,e^{0}+c_{2}\,r\,e^{0}(1) &&=r\,(c_{1}+c_{2})
\end{align}
$$

$$
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 0 \\
r_{1} & r_{2}
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}
c_{1} \\
c_{2}
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}
k_{0} \\
k_{1}
\end{bmatrix}\implies\begin{bmatrix}
c_{1} \\
c_{2}
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}
k_{0} \\
\frac{k_{1}-k_{0}\,r}{r}
\end{bmatrix}
$$

For the case of two roots, $y_{h}'=c_{1}\,r_{1}\,e^{r_{1}t}+c_{2}\,r_{2}\,e^{r_{2}t}$ and so,

$$
\begin{align}
k_{0}=y_{h}(0)&=c_{1}\,e^{0}+c_{2}\,\,e^{0}&&=c_{1}+c_{2} \\
k_{1}=y_{h}'(0)&= c_{1}\,r_{1}\,e^{0}+c_{2}\,r_{2}\,e^{0} &&=c_{1}\,r_{1}+c_{2}\,r_{2}
\end{align}
$$
$$
\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 1 \\
r_{1} & r_{2}
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}
c_{1} \\
c_{2}
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}
k_{0} \\
k_{1}
\end{bmatrix}\implies\begin{bmatrix}
c_{1} \\
c_{2}
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}
\frac{k_{1}-k_{0}\,r_{2}}{r_{1}-r_{2}} \\
\frac{k_{0}\,r_{1}-k_{1}}{r_{1}-r_{2}}
\end{bmatrix}
$$

`\end{proof}`

## Non-Homogeneous

> [!thm] General Solution of a 2nd Order Constant Coefficient ODE
> Let there be a [[#^f50998]] with [[4.1.0 - Linear ODEs#^9d5987]] $\left\{y_{1}, \ y_{2} \right\}$.
>
> $$a\,y''+b\,y'+c=f(t)$$
>
> Let $g(t)\leftrightarrow G(s)$ be the [[3.6 - Laplace Transform#^ae5c64]] pair of the inverse [[#^a66ce5]] $p(s)^{-1}$. A *particular solution* is given by the [[3.6 - Laplace Transform#^8a5468]] $y_p(t)=f\ast g(t)$, which in practice is typically in the same form as $f(t)$.
>
>
> $$y(t)= f\ast g(t)+  c_{1}\,y_{1}+c_{2}\,y_{2}$$
>

^4bf007

`\begin{proof}`[[#^4bf007]]
Recall that [[4.1.0 - Linear ODEs#^02185f]] is $y(t)=y_{h}(t)+y_{p}(t)$, where $y_{h}=c_{1}\,y_{1}+c_{2}\,y_{2}$ is given by [[#^382f29]].

Taking the [[3.6 - Laplace Transform#^ae5c64]] of the ODE with zero initial conditions gives $Y(s)=G(s)\,F(s)$, so by the [[3.6 - Laplace Transform#^353f36]], $y_{p}=f\ast g(t)$.

`\end{proof}`

> [!thm] 2nd Order Constant Coefficient IVP
> Consider an [[4.0 - ODEs#^520eb9]] for a [[#^f50998]] with a [[3.4 - Limits#^44db3d]] $f(t)$ and an existing [[3.6 - Laplace Transform#^ae5c64]] $\mathcal{L}\left\{f \right\}$.
>
>$$a\,y''+b\,y'+c\,y=f(t), \quad \begin{align}
y(0)&=k_{0}, \\ y'(0)&=k_{1}
\end{align}$$
>
> The general solution involves three cases for the roots of the [[#^a66ce5]], including a [[3.6 - Laplace Transform#^8a5468]] integral.
>$$y(t)=\begin{cases} \dfrac{t\,e^{rt}\ast f(t)}{a}\,+\,k_{0}\,e^{r t}\,+\,\left(k_{1}+\frac{b}{a}\,k_{0}+k_{0}\,r\right)t\,e^{r t} & (r_{1}=r_{2}=r)\in \mathbb{R} \\[1em] 
 \dfrac{(e^{\alpha t}\sin\beta t)\ast f(t)}{a\,\beta}\,+\,e^{\alpha t}\left[k_0\cos\beta t\,+\,\dfrac{k_1+\left(\frac{b}{a}+\alpha\right)\,k_0}{\beta}\sin\beta t\right] & (r_{1,2}=\alpha\pm\beta\,i)\in \mathbb{C} \\[1em]
\dfrac{(e^{r_{1}t}-e^{r_{2}t})\ast f(t)}{a(r_{1}-r_{2})}\,+\,\dfrac{k_{1}+\left(\frac{b}{a}+r_{1}\right)k_{0}}{r_{1}-r_{2}}\,e^{r_{1}t}\,-\,\dfrac{k_{1}+\left(\frac{b}{a}+r_{2}\right)k_{0}}{r_{1}-r_{2}}\,e^{r_{2}t} & (r_{1}\neq r_{2})\in\mathbb{R} \end{cases}$$

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^ec5b97]]
Apply the [[3.6 - Laplace Transform#^ae5c64]] to both sides, where $f(t)\leftrightarrow F(s), \ y(t)\leftrightarrow Y(s)$, substituting the [[3.6 - Laplace Transform#^5b8955]] and [[3.6 - Laplace Transform#^64a4c1]]. Simplify with the [[3.6 - Laplace Transform#^3d544b]].

$$\begin{align}
F(s)&=a\,s^{2}\,Y(s)+b\,s\,Y(s)+c\,Y(s)-s(a\,k_{0})-(a\,k_{1}+b\,k_{0}) \\[0.5em]
Y(s)&=\frac{F(s)+s\,a\,k_{0}+a\,k_{1}+b\,k_{0}}{(a\,s^{2}\,+b\,s\,+c)}
\end{align}$$

^dd8295

Substitute $G(s)=(a\,s^{2}\,+b\,s\,+c)^{-1}$ and $H(s)=s\,(a\,s^{2}\,+b\,s\,+c)^{-1}$ into [[#^dd8295]]

$$
\begin{align}
Y(s)&=G(s)\,F(s)+a\,k_{0}\,H(s)+(a\,k_{1}+b\,k_{0})\,G(s) \\
\end{align}
$$

^5c3dc9

 To find the [[3.6 - Laplace Transform#^8e6040]] $g(t) \leftrightarrow G(s), \ h(t)\leftrightarrow H(s)$, evaluate the cases where the [[#^a66ce5]] $a\,s^{2}\,+b\,s\,+c$ has *one* root or *two* roots.

For *degenerate roots* $(r_{1}=r_{2}=r)\in \mathbb{R}$, 

$$G(s)=\frac{1}{a(s-r)^{2}}, \quad H(s)= \frac{s}{a(s-r)^{2}},$$

$$
\begin{align}
g(t)&=\,\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{ G(s)\right\}&&=\frac{{t e^{rt}}}{a} \\
h(t)&=\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{H(s) \right\}=a^{-1}\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\frac{r}{\left(s - r \right)^{2}} + \frac{1}{s - r}\right\}&&=\frac{{r\,t\, e^{rt} + e^{rt}}}{a}
\end{align}
$$


For *unique roots* $(r_{1}\neq\ r_{2})\in \mathbb{R}$, 

$$G(s)=\frac{1}{a(s-r_{1})(s-r_{2})}, \quad H(s)= \frac{s}{a(s-r_{1})(s-r_{2})},$$

$$
\begin{align}
g(t)&=\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{G(s) \right\}=\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\frac{1}{a(r_{1}-r_{2})}\left( \frac{1}{s-r_{1}}-\frac{1}{s-r_{2}} \right)\right\}&&=\frac{e^{r_{1}t}-e^{r_{2}t}}{a(r_{1}-r_{2})} \\
h(t)&=\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{H(s) \right\}=\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{ \frac{1}{a(r_{1}-r_{2})}\left( \frac{r_{1}}{s-r_{1}}-\frac{r_{2}}{s-r_{2}} \right) \right\}&&=\frac{r_{1}\,e^{r_{1}t}-r_{2}e^{r_{2}t}}{a(r_{1}-r_{2})}
\end{align}
$$

For *complex roots* $(\alpha+i\beta, \ \alpha-i\beta)\in \mathbb{C}$ (using [[3.6 - Complex Numbers#^88e10c]]),

$$
\begin{align}
G(s)&=\frac{1}{a(s-R\,e^{i\phi})(s-R\,e^{-i\phi})},& \quad H(s)&=\frac{s}{a(s-R\,e^{i\phi})(s-R\,e^{-i\phi})}, \\
G(s)&=\frac{1}{a\left((s-\alpha)^{2}+\beta^{2}\right),}& \quad H(s)&=\frac{s}{a\left((s-\alpha)^{2}+\beta^{2}\right)},
\end{align}
$$

$$
\begin{align}
g(t)&=\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{G(s)\right\}&&=\frac{e^{\alpha t}\sin\beta t}{a\,\beta}\\[0.5em]
h(t)&=\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{H(s)\right\}=\frac{1}{a}\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\frac{s-\alpha}{(s-\alpha)^{2}+\beta^{2}}+\frac{\alpha}{(s-\alpha)^{2}+\beta^{2}}\right\}&&=\frac{e^{\alpha t}(\beta\cos\beta t+\alpha\sin\beta t)}{a\,\beta}
\end{align}
$$

Take the inverse of [[#^5c3dc9]] for the solution $y(t)$, where by the [[3.6 - Laplace Transform#^353f36]], we know $\mathcal{L}^{-1}\{\\G(s)\,F(s)\}=f\ast g(t)$.

$$
y(t)=f\ast g(t)+a\,k_{0}\,h(t) + (a\,k_{1}+b\,k_{0})\,g(t)
$$

^59f3ac

Substitute the cases for $g(t), \ h(t)$ to find $y(t)$ in terms of the initial constants.

$$
\begin{align}
\begin{cases}
\dfrac{te^{r\,t}\ast f(t)}{a} + \dfrac{a\,k_{1}+b\,k_{0}}{a}\,t e^{rt}+\dfrac{a\,k_{0}}{a}\,(e^{rt}+r\,t\, e^{r t}) & r_{1} = r_{2}=r \\[0.75em]
\dfrac{(e^{\alpha t}\sin\beta t)\ast f(t)}{a\,\beta}+\dfrac{a\,k_{0}\,e^{\alpha t}(\beta\cos\beta t+\alpha\sin\beta t)}{a\,\beta}+\dfrac{e^{\alpha t}\sin\beta t\,(a\,k_{1}+b\,k_{0})}{a\,\beta} & (r_{1,2}=\alpha\pm\beta\,i)\in \mathbb{C}
\\[1em]
\dfrac{(e^{r_{1}t}-e^{r_{2}t})\ast f(t)}{a(r_{1}-r_{2})}+\dfrac{a\,k_{1}+b\,k_{0}}{a(r_{1}-r_{2})}(e^{r_{1}t}-e^{r_{2}t})+\dfrac{a\,k_{0}}{a(r_{1}-r_{2})}(r_{1}\,e^{r_{1}t}-r_{2}\,e^{r_{2}t}) & (r_{1} \neq r_{2})\in \mathbb{R}
\end{cases}
\end{align}
$$
`\end{proof}`
