# 4.3.0 - Linear PDEs

> [!def] Linear PDE
> A **linear** [[4.3 - PDEs#^f66176]] of order $n$ can be expressed as a [[2.7 - Function Spaces#^3fa82b]] $\mathcal{L}$, and is of the form
> $\sum_{|\alpha| \le k} a_{\alpha}(\mathbf{x}) \,D^{\alpha}u(\mathbf{x}) = f(\mathbf{x})$

^a64c8c

> [!def] Linear Homogeneous PDE
> A **homogeneous** [[#^a64c8c]] has $f(x)=0$ on the right. The homogeneous counterpart of any non-homogenous PDE is often called it's *complementary* equation.
>
>
> $$\sum_{|\alpha| \le k} a_{\alpha}(\mathbf{x}) \,D^{\alpha}u(\mathbf{x}) = 0$$

## Separable Linear PDEs

> [!thm] Separation of Variables
> A [[4.3 - PDEs#^695543|Separable]] [[4.3.0 - Linear PDEs#^a64c8c]] yields two decoupled eigenvalue problems, assuming [[4.0 - ODEs#^2f00e2]] conditions on Ansatz $u(x,t)=X(x)\,T(t)\in [a,b]$.
>
> Substitute the Ansatz and divide both sides by $X(t)\,T(t)$ to get an equation with singular $x$-dependence on the right and $t$-dependence on the left. The only way for them to equate is to be a constant $C$ not dependent on *any* variable, creating two ODEs.
>
> 1. A [[4.1.2 - Second Order Linear ODEs#^562d67]] for $X(x)$ with eigenvalue $\lambda_n$ and eigenfunction $X_n(x)$.
> $\mathcal{L}_x[X] = \lambda_n\,w(x)\,X$
> 2. A [[4.1.1 - First Order Linear ODEs#^655517]] or [[4.1.2 - Second Order Linear ODEs#^2e2986]] for $T(t)$ driven by each $\lambda_n$.
> $\mathcal{L}_t[T] = -\lambda_n\,T$
> 
> By the [[4.1.2 - Second Order Linear ODEs#^nwo555]], the general solution is:
>
> $$\begin{align}
u(x,t) &= \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} c_n\,X_n(x)\,T_n(t) \\[0.5em]
c_n = \frac{\langle X_n,\, f\rangle_w}{\langle X_n,\, X_n\rangle_w} &= \frac{\int_a^b \bar{X}_n(x)\,f(x)\,w(x)\,dx}{\int_a^b \bar{X}_n(x)\,X_n(x)\,w(x)\,dx}
\end{align}$$
>
