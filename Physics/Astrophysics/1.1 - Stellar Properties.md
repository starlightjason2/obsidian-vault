# 1.1 - Stellar Properties

## Properties of Light

Astrophysics combines nearly all sub-fields of physics to study stars, planets,
galaxies…the universe.
- Different types of information received on Earth include matter, particles
(e.g.neutrinos, cosmic rays), gravitational waves, and photons
- Multi-wavelength astronomy using ground and space-based observatories
- Telescopes = focusing device (mirror/lens)+detector
- Magnification, resolution, light-gathering power, seeing, integration time
- Photometry, spectroscopy, interferometry, polarimetry

> [!def] Angular Resolution
> The angular resolution $\theta$ $[\pu{rad}]$ of a telescope is the smallest angle in the sky where two light sources can be distinguished from another. For a wave of wavelength $\lambda$ and an aperture diameter $D$,
> $$\theta=1.22 \frac{\lambda}{D}$$
>

## Solid Angles

> [!def] Solid Angle
> A solid angle $\Omega$ $[\pu{sr}]$ is the 3D angular volume subtending an area $A=R^{2}$. If In [[2.6 - Coordinate Sytems#^spherical-coordinates]],
> $$d\Omega=\frac{dA}{r^{2}}=\sin\theta \,d\theta \,d\phi$$

^solid-angle

## Properties of Stars

### Distance

> [!thm]
> Movement of nearby stars against the background of distant stars as the Earth orbits the Sun allows us to calculate distances. Let $\theta$ be the angle, $d$ be the distance to the star, and $R$ be the distance from the earth to the sun.
> $$d=\frac{R}{\tan\theta}$$
>

## Blackbody Radiation

> [!def] Blackbody Spectrum
> The energy density $u(\nu)$ $[\pu{erg cm^{-3} Hz^{-1}}]$ of a perfect blackbody for a frequency interval $\nu$ is
$$u(\nu)=\frac{8\pi h \,\nu^{3}}{c^{3}(e^{h\nu/kT}-1)} $$
$$I_{\nu}=\frac{c}{4\pi}u_{\nu}={\frac{2h\nu^{3}}{c^{2}}}\frac{1}{{e^{h\nu/kT}-1}}\equiv B_{\nu}={\frac{2h c^2}{\lambda^5}}\frac{1}{{e^{h\nu/kT}-1}}$$

^be78ba

> [!def] Specific Intensity
> The specific intensity $I(\mathbf{x},t;\mathbf{n},\nu)$ $[\pu{erg cm^{-2} s^{-1} sr^{-1} Hz^{-1}}]$ of radiation at position $\mathbf{x}$ and time $t$, travelling in direction $\mathbf{n}$ with frequency $\nu$, is a measurement of *brightness*. The amount of energy $dE_{\nu}$ transported by radiation frequencies $(\nu,\nu+d\nu)$ across $dA$ in $dT$ into [[#^solid-angle]] $d\Omega$ around $\mathbf{n}$ is given by:
>
> $$I(\mathbf{x},t;\mathbf{n},\nu)=\frac{dE_{\nu}}{\cos\theta\,dA\,d\Omega\,d\nu\,dt}$$
>

^specific-intensity

> [!def] Photon Number Density
> Photon number density $\psi$ $[\pu{cm^{-3} sr^{-1} Hz^{-1}}]$ is the number of photons per unit volume at time $t$, with frequencies $(\nu,\nu+d\nu)$ into [[#^solid-angle]] $d\Omega$ with velocity $c$. If the [[#^specific-intensity]] is $I$,
> $$\psi(\mathbf{x},t;\mathbf{n},v)=\frac{I(\mathbf{x},t;\mathbf{n},v)}{ch\nu}$$

> [!thm] Stefan-Boltzmann Law
> The total energy density $u$ $[\pu{erg cm^{-3}}]$ of a [[#^be78ba]] is related to it's temperature $T$. If $f$ is the [[#^ca85c6]],
> $$u=a\,T^{4}=\frac{4f}{c}$$
> $$f=\sigma T^{4}$$
>
>

> [!thm] Wien's Law
> $$\lambda_{max}\,T=\pu{0.29 cm K}$$
> $$h\nu_{max}=\pu{2.8}\,kT$$

> [!thm] Rayleigh-Jeans
> For $h\nu\ll kT$,
> $$B_{\nu}\approx \frac{2v^{2}kT}{c^{2}}=\frac{2ckT}{\lambda^{4}}$$

### Flux

> [!def] Specific Flux
> The specific flux is the amount of energy flowing through unit area $dA$ in unit time $dt$ per frequency interval $d\nu$, integrated over [[#^solid-angle]] $\Omega$. Let $I$ be the [[#^specific-intensity]].
> $$f_{\nu}=\int_{\Omega} \frac{dE_{\nu}}{dA\,dt\,d\nu}=\int_{\Omega}I_{\nu}\,\cos\theta\,d\Omega$$

^specific-flux

> [!def] Flux
> Flux is [[#^specific-flux]] $f_{\nu}$ integrated over frequency.
>
> $$F=\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{\nu}\,d\nu \propto \frac{1}{r^{2}}$$

$$
\frac{F_{1}}{F_{2}}=\frac{R_{2}}{R_{1}}
$$

^ca85c6

### Luminosity

> [!def] Luminosity
> Frequency dependent luminosity is the [[1.5 - Work and Energy#^power]] output of the star, and is equal to [[#^specific-flux]] at stellar surface times the surface area. Let the radius of the star be $R$.
> $$L_{\nu}=f_{\nu}(R)\,4\pi R^{2}$$

^luminosity

> [!def] Total Luminosity
> Total luminosity is [[#^luminosity]] integrated across frequency $\nu$.
>
> $$L=\int_{0}^{\infty}L_{\nu}\,d\nu$$
>

> [!def] Intensity
> The intensity $I$ is the [[#^ca85c6]] per [[#^solid-angle]].

## Orbits

> [!thm] Two body problem
> Consider two masses $M_{1}$, $M_{2}$ orbiting a common [[1.7 - Fixed Axis Rotation#^center-of-mass]] with radii $r_{1}$, $r_{2}$. Then,
>
>$$\begin{align}
r_{1}&=\frac{M_{2}\,a}{M_{1}+M_{2}} \\
r_{2}&=\frac{M_{1}\,a}{M_{1}+M_{2}}
\end{align}$$
$$\frac{\left\lvert{v_{1,obs}}\right\rvert}{\left\lvert{v_{2,obs}}\right\rvert}=\frac{r_{1}}{r_{2}}=\frac{M_{2}}{M_{1}}$$
> If $i$ is the inclination to the observer's line of sight of the perpendicular to the orbital plane, and $\tau$ is the orbital period,
$$(M_{1}+M_{2})\sin^{3}i=\frac{\tau(\left\lvert{v_{1,obs}}\right\rvert+\left\lvert{v_{2},obs}\right\rvert)^{3}}{2\pi\,G}$$

> [!thm] Kepler's Third Law
> $$\omega^{2}=\frac{G(M_{1}+M_{2})}{a^{3}}$$

## Pressure

> [!thm] Gas Pressure
> Let the mean particle mass be $\bar{m}$ $[\pu{g}]$. Using the [[Kinetics#^ideal-gas-law]],
> $$P_{g}=nkT=\frac{\rho}{\bar{m}}kT$$

^gas-pressure

> [!thm] Radiation Pressure
> For [[1.1 - Stellar Properties#^be78ba]] intensity $B$,
> $$P_{\text{rad}}=\frac{4\pi}{3c}B=\frac{1}{3}u=\frac{1}{3}aT^{4}$$

^radiation-pressure

> [!def] Total Pressure
> The full equation of state for a normal star is given to a first approximation by the sum of the [[#^radiation-pressure]] and the [[#^gas-pressure]].
> $$P=\frac{\rho kT}{\bar{m}}+\frac{1}{3}aT^{4}$$
