# 1.2 - Stellar Structure

> [!thm] Vogt-Russel Conjecture
> For an isolated star in hydrostatic and thermal equilibrium, the structure and evolution of the star are fully determined by its initial mass and chemical abundances.

## Mass Conservation

> [!thm] Equation of Mass Conservation
> Let mass be $M$ $[\pu{g}]$, the density at radius $r$ be $\rho(r,t)$, velocity be $v$, and time be $t$. The Equation of Mass Conservation is the [[4.0 - ODEs#^f66176]]:
> $$dM(r,t)=4\pi r^{2}\,\rho(r,t)\Big(\,dr-v \,dt\Big)$$
>
> $$
> \begin{align}
> \frac{\partial M}{\partial r}&=4\pi r^{2}\rho \\
> \frac{\partial M}{\partial t}&=-4\pi r^{2}\rho v
> \end{align}
> $$
>
> Under static conditions,
> $$v=0\implies \frac{dM}{dr}=4\pi r^{2}\rho(r)$$

> [!cor]
> The mass $M(r)$ is given by the [[3.9 - Integrals#^94174a]] in [[2.6 - Coordinate Sytems#^3f8956]].
> $$M(R)=\int_{0}^{\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\,\int_{0}^{R}\rho\,r^{2}\sin\theta\,dr\,d\phi\,d\theta=\int 4\pi r^{2}\,\rho\,dr$$
> $$\frac{dr}{dM}=\frac{1}{4\pi r^{2}\rho}$$

## Hydrostatic Equilibrium

> [!thm] Equation of Hydrostatic Equilibrium
> The radial acceleration $\ddot{r}$ $[\pu{cm s^{-2}}]$ of a star with radius $r$, mass $M$, and at pressure $P(r)$, is given by the [[4.0 - ODEs#^f66176]] of Hydrostatic Equilibrium.
> $$\frac{\partial P}{\partial r}=-\frac{G\,M(r)\,\rho(r)}{r^{2}}$$

^9a1c8e

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^9a1c8e]]
Consider acceleration $\ddot{r}$ of a cylinderical gas element with mass $dM=\rho\,dr\,dA$. We use a cylinderical element because we only care about radial pressure. ^56a574

$$
\begin{align}
\ddot{r}\,dM&=-g\,dM+P(r)\,dA-P(r+dr)\,dA \\
P(r+dr)&=P(r)+\frac{\partial P}{\partial r}dr
\end{align}
$$

$$\ddot{r}=-\frac{GM}{r^{2}}-\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial P}{\partial r}$$

`\end{proof}`

## Energy Conservation

> [!thm] Equation of Energy Conservation
> A star's energy comes from nuclear reactions. The reaction rate varies with radius. Let $\epsilon(r)$ $[\pu{erg g^{-1} s^{-1}}]$ be the [[1.5 - Work and Energy#^b11e2f]] per unit mass in the stellar material, and let $\rho(r)$ be the density. In a shell of mass $dm$ at radius $r$, the [[1.1 - Stellar Properties#^cb7987]] $dL$ is proportional to $\epsilon$.
>
> $$\frac{dL}{dr}=\epsilon\,dm=4\pi r^{2} \rho(r)\,\epsilon(r)$$

^13a246

`\begin{proof}`[[#^13a246]]
By energy conservation,

$$dL=\epsilon\,dm=4\pi r^{2} \rho(r)\,\epsilon(r)$$
`\end{proof}`

### Pressure

> [!thm] Virial Theorem
> $$E_{gr}=-\frac{3}{5} \frac{GM^{2}}{r}$$
> $$E_{th}\propto T$$
> $$\bar{P}=-\frac{1}{3}\frac{E_{gr}}{V}=-\frac{1}{5}\frac{GM^{2}}{rV}$$
> $$E_{total}=E_{th}+E_{gr}=-E_{th}=\frac{E_{gr}}{2}$$

Multiply both sides of the [[#^9a1c8e]] by $4\pi r^{3}$ and integrate from $0<r<r_{\star}$.

$$
\begin{align}
\int_{0}^{r^\star} 4\pi r^{3}\,\frac{\partial P}{\partial r}\,dr=-\int_{0}^{r^\star} 4\pi r\,G\,M(r)\,\rho(r)\,dr
\end{align}
$$

Integration by parts. Let the gravitational potential energy be $E_{gr}$.

$$
\begin{align}
E_{gr}&=\big(P(r)\,4\pi r^{3}\big)\Big|_{0}^{r^\star}-3\int_{0}^{r^\star}P(r)\,4\pi r^{2}\,dr \\
&=\big(P(r)\,4\pi r^{3}\big)\Big|_{0}^{r^\star}-\,\big(P(r)\,4\pi r^{3}\big)\Big|_{0}^{r^\star}
\end{align}
$$

$$
\bar{P}=-\frac{1}{3}\frac{E_{gr}}{V}
$$

$$
\begin{align}
 PV&=NkT \\
E_{th}&=\frac{2}{3}\frac{E_{th}}{V} \\
P&=\frac{2}{3} \frac{E_{th}}{V}
\end{align}
$$

Multiply both sides by $4\pi r^{2}$ and integrate over stellar volume $V$.

When a star contracts, its thermal energy rises.

$$
E_{total}=E_{th}+E_{gr}=-E_{th}=\frac{E_{gr}}{2}
$$

## Radiative Energy Transport

Conduction is irrelevant in stars. Temperature gradient determines which mode of energy transport is dominant. Steep gradient leads to significant convection. Disregard convection for now.

### Scattering

> [!def] Cross-Section
> The cross-section $\sigma$ $[\pu{cm^{2}}]$ quantifies the probability of a particle interaction in a unit area.

^aa4fd4

> [!def] Thompson Scattering
> Thompson scattering is photon scattering on free electrons. The [[#^aa4fd4]] $\sigma_{T}$ of photo scattering on free electrons is
> $$\sigma_{T}=\frac{8\pi}{3}\left( \frac{e^{2}}{m_{e}c^{2}} \right)^{2}=\pu{6.7E-25 cm^{2}}$$

^83e4a7

> [!thm] Radiative Energy Transport
> Consider a model where radiation is the primary vehicle of energy transfer. Photons can be absorbed and scattered with atomic interaction, creating opacity. The [[1.1 - Stellar Properties#^cb7987]] is then,
>
> $$L(r)\approx-4\pi r^{2}lc \frac{\Delta u}{\Delta r}$$
> And more exactly,
> $$\frac{dT(r)}{dr}=\frac{3L(r)\kappa(r)\rho(r)}{16\pi\,ac\, r^{2}\,T^{3}(r)}$$

^283f6c

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^283f6c]]
Assume [[#^83e4a7]] in hydrogen gas, with one electron per atom. The number of electrons the photon encounters is $n_{e}\,dx$ with [[#^aa4fd4]] $\sigma_{T}$.

$$n_{e}\approx\frac{\rho}{m_{H}}$$

If the photon travels distance $r_{\odot}$ (radius of the sun) to reach the surface, then we can model collisions as a random walk. Consider $\mathbf{D}$ change in position after $N$ steps, with the $i$th step described by vector $\mathbf{l}_{i}$ where the magnitude is the [[#^bfabf6]] $\left\lvert{\mathbf{l}_{i}}\right\rvert=l$.

$$
\begin{align}
\mathbf{D}&=\sum_{i=0}^{N}{\mathbf{l}_{i}} \\
\mathbf{D}^{2}&=2\prod_{i=0}^{N}\mathbf{l}_{i}+\sum_{i=}^{N}{\left\lvert{\mathbf{l}_{_{i}}}\right\rvert}^{2}
\end{align}
$$

To cover distance $D=l\sqrt{N}=r_{\odot}$, the number of steps would be $N$ and each would take time $t$.

$$
\begin{align}
N&=\frac{r_{\odot}^{2}}{l^{2}} \\
\tau&=\frac{l}{c}
\end{align}\implies \tau=\frac{r_{\odot}^{2}}{lc}
$$

The power is given by the [[#^13a246]] and time $\tau$, which then gives [[1.1 - Stellar Properties#^cb7987]].

$$L(r)\approx \frac{4\pi r^{2}\Delta r\Delta u}{\tau}=\frac{4\pi r^{2}\Delta r\Delta u\,lc}{(\Delta r)^{2}}=-4\pi r^{2}lc \frac{\Delta u}{\Delta r}$$
`\end{proof}`

> [!def] Mean Free Path
> The mean free path $l$ $[\pu{cm}]$ is the distance covered by the photon _before experiencing an interaction_ with any particle in any material.
> $$l=\frac{1}{\sum_{i=0}^{N} n_{i}\sigma_{i}}=\frac{1}{\rho\kappa}$$
> `\end{proof}`

^bfabf6

> [!exm] Time for photon to leave the Sun
> For the sun,
>
> $$t=\frac{r_{\odot}^{2}}{lc}=\frac{(\pu{7e10cm})^{2}}{(\pu{10E-1cm})(\pu{3E10cm s^{-1}})}=\pu{50000 years}$$

### Opacity

- Bound-bound: excitation
- ionization
- Free-free: inverse bremsstrahlung where an electron in the presence of an ion transitions between ionized states. The electron and the ion share the photon's energy and momentum, respectively.

> [!def] Opacity
> A photon travels a length $dx$ through $i$ materials, filled with $n_{i}$ particles per volume. The number of targets the photon encounters is $n_{i}\,dx$ with [[#^aa4fd4]] $\sigma_{i}$. Let $N_{i}=n_{i}\sigma_{i}\,dx$ be the total number of interactions.
>
> The opacity $\kappa$ $[\pu{cm^{2} g^{-1}}]$ is defined in terms of the [[#^bfabf6]].
>
> $$l=\frac{1}{\sum_{i=0}^{N} n_{i}\sigma_{i}}=\frac{1}{\rho\kappa}$$

> [!def] Rosseland Mean Opacity
> The Rosseland mean opacity $\kappa$ $[\pu{cm^{2} g^{-1}}]$ is the average opacity over wavelength, and characterizes how radiation is affected by scattering and absorption.
> Generally, we create opacity tables where $\kappa$ is precomputed and saved as a function of temperature $T$.
>
> $$\bar{\kappa}\approx \frac{\rho}{T^{3.5}}$$
