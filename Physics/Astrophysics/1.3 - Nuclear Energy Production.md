# 1.3 - Nuclear Energy Production

## Abundance

> [!def] Mass Abundance
> Let $A$ be the atomic mass number of element $Z$.
> $$X\equiv \frac{\rho_{\text{H}}}{\rho}, \ Y\equiv \frac{\rho_{\text{He}}}{\rho}, \ Z_{\text{A}}\equiv \frac{\rho_{\text{A}}}{\rho}$$

^8095e7

> [!def] Number Density
> For hydrogen gas, average mass for a one electron, one proton system is $\bar{m}=\frac{1}{2}m_{h}$ since the electron mass is negligible.
> $$n_{\text{H}}\equiv \frac{X\rho}{m_{\text{H}}}, \ n_{\text{He}}\equiv \frac{Y\rho}{4m_{\text{H}}}, \ n_{\text{A}}\equiv \frac{Z_{\text{A}}\rho}{A m_{\text{H}}}$$

^faa383

> [!thm]
> For $X+Y+Z=1$
> $$n=2n_{\text{H}}+3n_{\text{He}}+\sum \frac{A}{2}n_{A}=\frac{\rho}{m_{\text{H}}}\left( 2X+\frac{3}{4}Y+\frac{1}{2}Z \right)$$

## Nuclear Properties

> [!def] Atomic Number
> The atomic number $Z$ is the number of protons in the nucleus of an atom. This defines the element and determines its chemical properties.

^679156

> [!def] Mass Number
> The Mass Number $A$ is the The total number of protons and neutrons (nucleons) in the nucleus of an atom. Also called the nucleon number.

^24f43f

> [!def] Nuclear Binding Energy
> The binding energy of a nucleus is the energy required to completely separate all nucleons in the nucleus. Calculated from the difference between mass of component protons and neutrons, the measured nuclear mass, and the [[5.5 - Mass and Energy#^82cd2b]] $E=mc^{2}$.
>
> Let $Z$ be [[#^679156]], $A$ be [[#^24f43f]], $m_p$ be proton mass, $m_n$ be neutron mass, and $m_{nucleus}$ be actual nuclear mass.
> $$BE = (Z \cdot m_p + (A-Z) \cdot m_n - m_{nucleus}) \cdot c^2$$

> [!def] Binding Energy per Nucleon
> The binding energy per nucleon is the [[#^679156|nuclear binding energy]] divided by the [[#^24f43f|mass number]] $A$.
> $$\frac{BE}{A} = \frac{(Z \cdot m_p + (A-Z) \cdot m_n - m_{nucleus}) \cdot c^2}{A}$$

> [!def] Gamow Energy
> The Gamow energy $E_G$ $[\pu{erg}]$ is the characteristic energy scale for quantum tunneling through the Coulomb barrier in nuclear fusion reactions.
> $$E_G=(\pi \alpha Z_A Z_B)^{2} 2\mu c^{2}$$
> where $\alpha$ is the fine structure constant, $Z_A$ and $Z_B$ are the [[#^679156|atomic numbers]], and $\mu$ is the reduced mass.

^a9d394

> [!def] Gamow Factor
> Let $E_{G}$ be the [[#^a9d394]].
> $$g(E)=e^{-\sqrt{E_{G}/E}}$$

## Nuclear Reactions

Nuclear reactions can be 1-body, 2-body, or 3-body (rare). For particles $i$, $j$, $m$, $o$, a 2-body reaction is denoted as $i+j\to m+o$ or $i(j,m)o$.

> [!thm] Reaction Cross-Section
> The [[1.2 - Stellar Structure#^aa4fd4]], or effective surface area, is a measure of the probability that a nuclear reaction occurs. The particles see each other smeared out over the de Broglie wavelength $\lambda$ associated with their relative momentum $p$.
> $$\lambda = \frac{h}{p}$$
> where $h$ is Planck's constant.

^7c2b2c

> [!def] Reduced Mass
> The reduced mass $\mu$ $[\pu{g}]$ of a two-body system with masses $m_A$ and $m_B$ is
> $$\mu = \frac{m_A m_B}{m_A + m_B}$$

Let the [[#^7c2b2c]] be $\sigma_{AB}$ and [[#^faa383]] of $A$ and $B$ be $n_{A}, \ n_{B}$. If every reaction releases energy $Q$, the power per unit mass is

$$\epsilon=n_{A}n_{B}\sigma_{AB},v_{AB} \frac{Q}{\rho}$$

Substitute [[#^8095e7]] $X_{A}, \ X_{B}$

$$\epsilon=\frac{\rho X_{A}X_{B}}{m_{H}^{2}A_{A}A_{B}}\sigma_{AB}v_{AB}Q$$

However, in reality, nuclei in a gas have a distribution of velocities given by the Boltzmann distribution.

$$\langle \sigma_{AB}v_{AB} \rangle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\sigma_{AB}v_{AB}P(v_{AB})\,dv_{AB}$$
Where the probability $P(v_{AB})$ comes from the [[Boltzmann Distribution#^a57092]]. Switch variables from relative velocity to kinetic energy in the [[1.7 - Fixed Axis Rotation#^70740f]] frame.

$$
\begin{align}
E=\frac{1}{2}\mu v^{2}, \ dE=\mu v\,dv \\
\end{align}
$$

> [!def] Reaction Expectation Value
> The [[6.2 - Uncertainty#^6591bb|Expectation Value]] of a reaction is $\langle \sigma_{AB}v_{AB} \rangle$ $[\pu{cm^{3} s^{-1}}]$, where $\sigma_{AB}$ is the [[#^7c2b2c]].
> $$\langle \sigma v \rangle =\sqrt{\frac{8}{\pi \mu}} \frac{S_{0}}{(kT)^{3/2}}\int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-E/kT} e^{-\sqrt{E_G/E}} \,dE$$

> [!def] Energy Generation Rate
> The energy generation rate per unit mass $\epsilon$ $[\pu{erg g^{-1} s^{-1}}]$ for a nuclear reaction between species $A$ and $B$ releasing energy $Q$ is
> $$\epsilon=\frac{\rho X_{A}X_{B}}{m_{H}^{2}A_{A}A_{B}}\langle \sigma_{AB}v_{AB} \rangle \,Q$$
> where $X_A$ and $X_B$ are [[#^8095e7|mass abundances]].

> [!def] $S$-Factor
> The $S$-Factor $S(E)$ $[\pu{erg cm^{2}}]$ times the [[1.2 - Stellar Structure#^aa4fd4]] gives the reaction cross-section with the [[#^a9d394|Gamow factor]] removed:
> $$\sigma_{AB}(E) = \frac{S(E)}{E} e^{-\sqrt{E_G/E}}$$

> [!exm] $p$-$p$ chain
> The sun's energy comes from hydrogen fusion. The 3-step process is called the $p$-$p$ chain.
>
> $$
> \begin{align}
> \ce{p + p &-> d + e+ + v_{e}} \\
> \ce{p + d &-> ^{3}He + \gamma} \\
> \ce{^{3}He + ^{3}He &-> ^{4}He + \gamma}
> \end{align}
> $$
>
> The rest-mass-to-energy conversion efficiency is $0.7\%$. Therefore, it gives us a lifetime of
> $$\tau_{\text{nuc}}=\frac{0.1 \times 0.0007 \times M_{\odot} c^2}{L_{\odot}}$$
> where $M_{\odot}$ is the solar mass and $L_{\odot}$ is the solar luminosity.
