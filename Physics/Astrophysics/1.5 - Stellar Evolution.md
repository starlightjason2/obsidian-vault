# 1.5 - Stellar Evolution

> [!def] Supernova
> A star with an iron core will eventually stop fusing and undergo collapse. The time for a star's core to collapse to a neutron star is $\tau_{ff}\left[\pu{s}\right]$.
> $$\tau_{ff}=\sqrt{\frac{3\pi}{32G\bar{p}}}\approx \pu{0.1s}$$
> The binding energy released during collapse is,
> $$E_b\approx E_{g}\approx \frac{3}{5}\frac{GM_{ns}^{2}}{r}$$
> Stellar material bounces off the stellar core, repulsed by the strong force. If around 1% of the energy is reabsorbed by supernova shock, the shock is revived and blows up the star in a **supernova**. The following nuclear reaction coverts the stellar remnant into a **neutron star**.
> $$
> \begin{align}
\ce{\nu_{e} + n &-> p + e-} \\
\ce{\bar{\nu}_{e} + p &-> n + e+}
\end{align}
> $$
> As shock goes through the rest of the star, it triggers nucleosynthesis. The most important product is the unstable isotope $\ce{^{56}Ni}$.

Neutron decay and neutronization starts at densities $>\pu{1e11 g/cm^{3}}$, where nuclei dissolve. The resulting material has a balance of neutrons to protons to electrons of $8:1:1$.

$$
\begin{align}
 \ce{p + e- &-> n + \nu_{e}} \\
\ce{^{56}Fe + e- &-> ^{56}Co + \nu_{e}}
\end{align}
$$

> [!thm] EM Luminosity of Spinning Magnetic Dipole
> $$L=\frac{1}{6c^{3}}B^{2}r^{6}\omega^{4}\sin^{2}\theta$$

The luminosity of a pulsar is powered by the loss of [[1.7 - Fixed Axis Rotation#^262e33]] of the star.

$$L=-\frac{dE_{rot}}{dt}=-\frac{1}{2}I\omega^{2}$$

So

$$
L=\frac{1}{6c^{3}}B^{2}r^{6}\omega^{6}\sin^{2}\theta\implies I\omega\frac{d\omega}{dt}\propto \omega^{4}\implies \frac{d\omega}{dt}=C\omega^{3}
$$

Where $C=\omega_{0} / \omega_{0}^{3}$.
