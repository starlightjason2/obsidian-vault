# 1.11 - Drag Forces

## Friction

Friction constants are determined experimentally.

### Static Friction

> [!def] Static Friction
> Consider an object at rest on a surface. Let $\mu_s$ be the coefficient of static friction between the object and the surface, $\mathbf{F}_N$ be the normal force, and $\mathbf{F}_{\text{app}}$ the applied force. Then:
>
> $$
> \lVert \mathbf{F}_s \rVert \leq \mu_s \lVert \mathbf{F}_N \rVert
> $$

### Kinetic Friction

> [!def] Kinetic Friction
> Consider an object in motion on a surface. Let $\mu_k$ be the coefficient of kinetic friction and $\mathbf{F}_N$ the normal force. Then:
>
> $$
> \lVert \mathbf{F}_k \rVert = \mu_k \lVert \mathbf{F}_N \rVert
> $$

## Drag

### Linear Drag

Air resistance (drag) is a form of friction that acts opposite to the direction of motion.

> [!def] Linear Drag Force
> Consider an object with cross-sectional area $A\, [\pu{m^2}]$, speed $v\, [\pu{m/s}]$, in a fluid of density $\rho\, [\pu{kg/m^3}]$. Let $C$ be the (dimensionless) drag coefficient. Then the drag force is
>
> $$
> \mathbf{F}_{D}=-C\,\hat{\boldsymbol{v}}
> $$

^ec8e89

> [!thm] Velocity under Drag Force
> The velocity $\mathbf{v}(t)$ of the object moving with initial velocity $\mathbf{v}_{0}=v_{0x}\,\mathbf{\hat{x}}+v_{0y}\,\mathbf{\hat{y}}$ and experiencing a [[#^ec8e89]] with $c_{1}=b$ is
>
>$$
> \mathbf{v}=\begin{bmatrix}
v_{0x}\,e^{-bt/m} \\ \left( v_{0y}+\dfrac{mg}{b} \right)\,e^{-bt/m}-\dfrac{mg}{b}
\end{bmatrix}
>$$

^0d1398

An object is moving with initial velocity $\mathbf{v}_{0}=\left<v_{0x},v_{0y}\right>$ and experiencing drag $\mathbf{F}_{d}=-b\,\mathbf{v}$. With [[1.3 - Newton's Laws#^aee106]], the net force in each dimension is a [[4.1.1 - First Order Linear ODEs#^655517]].

 $$
\begin{bmatrix}
-b\,v_{x} \\
-b\,v_{y}-mg
\end{bmatrix}=m\begin{bmatrix}\dot{v}_{x}\\\dot{v}_{y}
\end{bmatrix}
 $$

$$
\begin{align}
\,\dot{v}_{x}+\frac{b}{m}\,v_{x}&=0 \\[0.5em]
\,\dot{v}_{y}+\frac{b}{m}\,v_{y}+g&=0
\end{align}
$$

For the $v_{x}$, use the [[4.1.1 - First Order Linear ODEs#^00894f]] where $p(t)=\frac{b}{m}$.

$$
C\,e^{-bt/m}=v_{x}
$$
The constant is $C=v_{0x}$ if $v_{x}(0)=v_{0x}$.

 For $v_{y}$ use the [[4.1.1 - First Order Linear ODEs#^ff34cc]], with the same $p(t)$ as above. Multiply both sides by $e^{\int p(t)\,dt}=e^{bt/m}$.

  $$
\begin{align}
\dot{v}_{y}+\frac{b}{m}v&=-g \\[0.5em]
\frac{d}{dt}(e^{bt/m}\,v_{y})&=-g\,e^{bt/m} \\[0.5em]
e^{bt/m}\,v_{y}&=-g\int e^{bt/m}\,dt \\[0.5em]
e^{bt/m}\,v_{y}&=-\frac{g\,m}{b} \,e^{bt/m}+C \\[0.5em]
v_{y}&=-\frac{g\,m}{b}+C\,e^{-bt/m}
\end{align}
$$
The constant is $C=v_{0y}+\frac{g\,m}{m}$.

> [!def] Terminal Velocity
> An object in free fall reaches terminal velocity in the [[3.4 - Limits#^1a2846]] of the [[#^0d1398]] as time $t$ approaches infinity.
>
> $$
> \lim_{t\to\infty}{\mathbf{v}}=\begin{bmatrix}
0 \\
\frac{m\,g}{b}
\end{bmatrix}
> $$

### Quadratic Drag

> [!def] Quadratic Drag Force
> Consider an object with cross-sectional area $A\, [\pu{m^2}]$, speed $v\, [\pu{m/s}]$, in a fluid of density $\rho\, [\pu{kg/m^3}]$. Let $C$ be the (dimensionless) drag coefficient. Then the drag force is
>
> $$
> \lVert \mathbf{F}_D \rVert = -c_{2}\,\mathbf{v}^{2}
> $$

^631802

> [!thm] Velocity under Quadratic Drag
> Let there be an object moving with initial velocity $\mathbf{v}_{0}=v_{0}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{\imath}}$ and experiencing a [[#^631802]] $\mathbf{F}_{d}=-b\,v^{2}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{\imath}}$.
>
> The velocity $\mathbf{v}(t)$ of the object is
> $$v(t)=\frac{v_{0}\,m}{m+v_{0}\,c\,t}$$
>
