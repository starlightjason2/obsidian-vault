## Angular Momentum

> [!def] Angular Momentum
> Angular momentum $\mathbf{L}\left[\pu{kg\cdot m^{2}/s}\right]$ is the rotational equivalent of [[1.6 - Linear Momentum and Collisions#^57c92e]]. Let $\mathbf{r}$ be the distance vector from the axis of rotation and $\mathbf{p}$ be the momentum.
>
> $$
> \mathbf{L} = \mathbf{r} \times \mathbf{p} = I\mathbf{\omega}
> $$

^f050a1

### Newton's Second Law with Angular Momentum

> [!thm] Newton's Second Law with Angular Momentum
> If $\mathbf{L}$ is the [[#^f050a1]] and $\mathbf{\tau}$ is the [[1.7 - Fixed Axis Rotation#^cbafb0]],
>
> $$
> \frac{d\mathbf{L}}{dt} = \mathbf{\tau}
> $$

We can find a rotational equivalent to the familiar translational equations for momentum.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d\mathbf{L}}{dt} &= \frac{d\mathbf{r}}{dt} \times \mathbf{p} + \frac{d\mathbf{p}}{dt} \times \mathbf{r}
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $\mathbf{r}$ is the distance vector, we can say that velocity is $\mathbf{v} = \frac{d\mathbf{r}}{dt}$. We can also substitute [[1.6 - Linear Momentum and Collisions#^6257fb]] for $\frac{d\mathbf{p}}{dt}$.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d\mathbf{L}}{dt} &= \mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{p} + \frac{d\mathbf{p}}{dt} \times \mathbf{r} \\
&= \mathbf{v} \times (m\mathbf{v}) + \sum \mathbf{F} \times \mathbf{r} \\
&= m\cancel{(\mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{v})} + \sum \mathbf{F} \times \mathbf{r} \\
&= \sum \mathbf{F} \times \mathbf{r} = \sum \mathbf{\tau}
\end{aligned}
$$

### Conservation of Angular Momentum

> [!thm] Conservation of Angular Momentum
> The total [[#^f050a1]] of a system around a fixed point is conserved without any net external [[1.7 - Fixed Axis Rotation#^cbafb0]].

^833af3

> [!exm] Death of a Star
> A neutron star rotates once every $\pu{100}$ days. When it dies, its radius reduces from $R = \pu{700000km}$ to $r = \pu{10km}$. Assume its mass remains unchanged. What's its new frequency?
>
> The moment of inertia for a sphere is $I = \frac{2}{5}mr^{2}$. Then, by the [[#^833af3]],
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> I_1 \omega_1 &= I_2 \omega_2 \\
> \frac{2}{5} m r_1^2 \omega_1 &= \frac{2}{5} m r_2^2 \omega_2 \\
> \omega_2 &= \frac{r_1^2}{r_2^2} \omega_1
> \end{aligned}
> $$
>
> Then the frequency is
>
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> f_2 &= \frac{\omega_2}{2\pi} \\
> &= \frac{r_1^2}{r_2^2} \cdot \frac{f_1}{1} \\
> &= f_1 \cdot \frac{(r_1)^2}{(r_2)^2} \\
> &= \left( \frac{\pu{7.0e5km}}{\pu{10km}} \right)^2 \cdot \left( \frac{\pu{1rev}}{(\pu{100days})(\pu{24hr/day})(\pu{3600s/hr})} \right)
> \end{aligned}
> $$

Think of an ice skater speeding up as she draws her arms in. The radius $\mathbf{r}$ shrinks, so the linear momentum $\mathbf{p}$ of the gymnast increases.

## Rolling Motion

The total kinetic energy of the rolling object is the sum of translational and rotational kinetic energy:

$$
E_{\text{total}} = PE_g + KE_{\text{trans}} + KE_{\text{rot}}
$$
