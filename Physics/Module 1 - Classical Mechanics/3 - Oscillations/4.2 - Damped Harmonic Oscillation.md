# 4.2 - Damped Harmonic Oscillation

> [!def] Damped Harmonic Oscillator Equation
> The Damped Harmonic Oscillator Equation is the [[4.1 - Simple Harmonic Oscillation#^6f684f]] with an additional [[1.11 - Drag Forces#^ec8e89]] $F_{d}=-2\beta \,\dot{x}$ where $\beta \left[\pu{Hz}\right]$ is the *damping coefficient*.
>
> $$\ddot{x}+2\beta \,\dot{x}+\omega_{0}^{2}\,x=0$$

^a1b2c3

> [!def] Damped Harmonic Oscillator
> A damped harmonic oscillator is an object obeying the [[#^a1b2c3]].
>
> The solution has three scenarios dependent on the damping constant $\beta$ and resonant frequency $\omega_{0}$: [[#^57d8dc]], [[#^739494]], and [[#^4511ae]].
>
> $$x(t) = \begin{cases}  
A\,e^{-\beta t}\cos(\omega_1 t + \phi) & \beta < \omega_0 \\
e^{-\beta t}(c_1 + c_2\,t) & \beta = \omega_0 \\
c_1\,e^{-\gamma_+ t} + c_2\,e^{-\gamma_t} & \beta > \omega_{0} \end{cases}$$

^e649e7

## Underdamped Case

> [!thm] Underdamped Oscillator
> A [[#^e649e7]] where the damping coefficient $\beta$ is smaller than the resonant frequency $\omega_{0}$. The resulting motion is a *damped* [[4.1 - Simple Harmonic Oscillation#^d9cde8]] with frequency $\omega_{1}\left[\pu{Hz}\right]=\sqrt{\omega_{0}^{2}-\beta^{2}}$.
>
> $$x(t)=A\,e^{-\beta t}\cos(\omega_{1}t+\phi) \quad;\quad \beta^{2}<\omega_{0}^{2}$$
>

^57d8dc

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^57d8dc]]
The characteristic equation $r^{2}+2\beta\,r+\omega_{0}^{2}=0$ has solutions found with the quadratic formula: $r_{1,2}=\frac{1}{2}(-2\beta\pm \sqrt{4\beta^{2}-4\omega_{0}^{2}})=-\beta\pm \sqrt{\beta^{2}-\omega_{0}^{2}}$.

If $\beta^{2}<\omega_{0}^{2}$, then $r_{1,2}=-\beta\pm i\sqrt{\omega_{0}^{2}-\beta^{2}}$. Substitute $\omega_{1} = \sqrt{\pm(\omega_{0}^{2}-\beta^{2})}$ into the [[4.1.3 - Constant Coefficient ODEs#^382f29]] (two root case).

$$\begin{align}
x(t) &= c_{1}\,e^{-\beta t+i\omega_{1}t}+c_{2}\,e^{-\beta t-i\omega_{1}t} \\ 
&=e^{-\beta t}(c_{1}\,e^{i\omega_{1}t}+c_{2}\,e^{-i\omega_{1}t})
\end{align}$$

Substitute a [[4.1 - Simple Harmonic Oscillation#^d9cde8]] for $\omega=\omega_{1}$ in the case $\beta^{2}<\omega_{0}^{2}$.
`\end{proof}`

## Critically Damped Case

> [!thm] Critically Damped Oscillator
> A [[#^e649e7]] where the damping coefficient $\beta$ is equal to the resonant frequency $\omega_{0}$. 
>
> $$x(t)=e^{-\beta t}(c_{1}+c_{2}\,t) \quad;\quad \beta=\omega_{0}$$

^739494

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^57d8dc]]
The characteristic equation $r^{2}+2\beta\,r+\omega_{0}^{2}=0$ has solutions found with the quadratic formula: $r_{1,2}=\frac{1}{2}(-2\beta\pm \sqrt{4\beta^{2}-4\omega_{0}^{2}})=-\beta\pm \sqrt{\beta^{2}-\omega_{0}^{2}}$.

If $\beta^{2}=\omega_{0}^{2}$, then $r=-\beta$. Substitute into the [[4.1.3 - Constant Coefficient ODEs#^382f29]] (one root case).

$$\begin{align}
x(t) &= c_{1}\,e^{-\beta t}+c_{2}\,t\,e^{-\beta t} \\ 
&=e^{-\beta t}(c_{1}+c_{2}\,t)
\end{align}$$

`\end{proof}`
## Overdamped Case

> [!thm] Overdamped Oscillator
> A [[#^e649e7]] where the damping coefficient $\beta$ is greater than the resonant frequency $\omega_{0}$. Let $\gamma_{\pm}=\beta\pm\sqrt{\beta^{2}-\omega_{0}^{2}}$. The resulting motion is
>
> $$x(t) = c_{1}\,e^{-\gamma_{+}t}+c_{2}\,e^{-\gamma_{-}t}\quad;\quad \beta>\omega_{0}$$

^4511ae

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^57d8dc]]
The characteristic equation $r^{2}+2\beta\,r+\omega_{0}^{2}=0$ has solutions found with the quadratic formula: $r_{1,2}=\frac{1}{2}(-2\beta\pm \sqrt{4\beta^{2}-4\omega_{0}^{2}})=-\beta\pm \sqrt{\beta^{2}-\omega_{0}^{2}}$.

If $\beta^{2}>\omega_{0}^{2}$, then $r_{1,2}=-\beta\pm\sqrt{\beta^{2}-\omega_{0}^{2}}$, where $\beta<\sqrt{\beta^{2}-\omega_{0}^{2}}$, so $r_{1,2}<0$. Let $r_{1,2}=-\gamma_{\pm}$ and substitute into the [[4.1.3 - Constant Coefficient ODEs#^382f29]] (two root case)
`\end{proof}`
