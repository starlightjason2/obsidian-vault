# 4.3 - Driven Harmonic Oscillation

> [!def] Driven Harmonic Oscillator Equation
> The Driven Harmonic Oscillator Equation is a [[4.2 - Damped Harmonic Oscillation#^a1b2c3]] with an additional driving force $f(t)$, making it a inhomogeneous [[4.1.2 - Second Order Linear ODEs#^2e2986]].
> $$\ddot{x}+2\beta \,\dot{x}+\omega_{0}^{2}\,x=f(t)$$

^d4e5f6

> [!def] Driven Harmonic Oscillator
> A driven harmonic oscillator is an object obeying the [[#^d4e5f6]].
>
> The transient (homogeneous) solution is equivalent to a [[4.2 - Damped Harmonic Oscillation#^e649e7]] with damped frequency $\omega_{1}$. The steady state (particular) solution $x_{p}(t)$ oscillates at the driving frequency $\omega$.
>
> $$x(t) = x_{h}(t)+x_{p}(t)$$

## Sinusoidal Driving Force

> [!def] Sinusoidal DHO
> A [[#^5cc7c5]] with a driving [[3.1 - Scalar Functions#^4f8b8b]] $f(t)=f_{0}\cos (\omega t+\delta)$ where $\omega  \left[\pu{Hz}\right]$ is the *driving frequency*. The steady state (particular) solution is $x_{p}(t)=D\,e^{i\omega t}$ for [[3.6 - Complex Numbers#^4b0f81]] $D=\left\lvert{D}\right\rvert\,e^{i\delta}$ where
> $$\begin{align}  \\
\left\lvert{D}\right\rvert &=\frac{f_{0} / m}{\sqrt{(\omega_{0}^{2}-\omega ^{2})^{2}+4\beta^{2}\omega ^{2}}} , &
 \tan\delta=\frac{-2\beta \omega }{\omega_{0}^{2}-\omega ^{2}} \\
\end{align}$$
>
> $$x(t)=\left\lvert{D}\right\rvert\cos(\omega t+\delta)$$
>

^744bdf

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^744bdf]]
See [[4.1.3 - Constant Coefficient ODEs#^4bf007]]. Use the Ansatz $De^{i\omega t} = q_p$. Therefore,

$$\dot{q}_p = Di\omega \,e^{i\omega t}, \qquad \ddot{q}_p = -D\omega^2\, e^{i\omega t}$$

Substitute into $\ddot{x}+2\beta \,\dot{x}+\omega_{0}^{2}\,x=\frac{f_{0}}{m}\,e^{i\omega t}$.

$$
\begin{align}
D\!\left(-\omega^2 + 2i\beta\omega + \omega_0^2\right)e^{i\omega t} &= \frac{f_0}{m}e^{i\omega t} \implies D= \frac{f_0}{m}\frac{1}{(\omega_0^2 - \omega^2) + i\,(2\beta\omega)}
\end{align}
$$

Rationalize with $(a - ib)(a+ib) = a^2 + b^2$ where $a=\omega_{0}^{2}-\omega_{d}^{2}$, $b=2\beta \omega_{d}$.

$$D=\frac{1}{(\omega_0^2-\omega^2)+2i\beta\omega} \left( \frac{(\omega_0^2-\omega^2)-2i\beta\omega}{(\omega_0^2-\omega^2)-2i\beta\omega} \right) = \frac{(\omega_0^2-\omega^2) - i(2\beta\omega)}{(\omega_0^2-\omega^2)^2 + 4\beta^2\omega^2}$$

Factor out the denominator and find $\left\lvert{D}\right\rvert, \ \delta$ such that $D=\left\lvert{D}\right\rvert\,e^{i\delta}$.

$$
\begin{align}
|D| &= \frac{\sqrt{(\omega_0^2-\omega^2)^2 + 4\beta^2\omega^2}}{(\omega_0^2-\omega^2)^2+4\beta^2\omega^2} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{(\omega_0^2-\omega^2)^2+4\beta^2\omega^2}} \\[]
\tan\delta &= \frac{-2\beta\omega}{\omega_0^2 - \omega^2}
\end{align}
$$

`\end{proof}`

## Periodic Driving Force

> [!def] Periodic DHO
> A [[#^5cc7c5]] where the driving force is any [[3.1 - Scalar Functions#^7a99bc]] $f(t)$.
>
> The physical steady state solution is an [[5.2 - Infinite Series#^07b41c]] of harmonic solutions for the $n$th [[#^744bdf]] with $n$th norm and phase:
> $$\begin{align}  \\
\left\lvert{D_{n}}\right\rvert &=\frac{f_{0} / m}{\sqrt{(\omega_{0}^{2}-\omega_{n} ^{2})^{2}+4\beta^{2}\omega_{n} ^{2}}} , &
 \tan\delta_{n}=\frac{-2\beta \omega_{n} }{\omega_{0}^{2}-\omega _{n}^{2}} \\
\end{align}$$
> $$x(t)=\left\lvert{D}\right\rvert\cos(\omega t+\delta)$$

^b3938c

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^b3938c]]
Using [[6.3 - Fourier Series#^9aeaaa]], we can write an equation of motion for $n\in \mathbb{Z}$ harmonic frequencies $\omega_{n}$.

 $$\ddot{x}+2\beta \,\dot{x}+\omega_{0}^{2}\,x=f(t)=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{c_{n\,}e^{i\omega_{n}t}}$$
 Proceed with the proof of [[#^744bdf]].
 `\end{proof}`

### Resonance

> [!def] Q-Factor
> The **quality factor** $Q$ measures the window of energy transfer $\Delta \omega=2\beta$ of a [[4.2 - Damped Harmonic Oscillation#^e649e7]] with resonant frequency $\omega_{0}$. At each $\omega_{0}\pm\beta$, the energy response $E_{\pm}$ will be *half* the [[4.1 - Simple Harmonic Oscillation#^fe0773]] $E_{max}$.
> $$Q\equiv \frac{\omega_{0}}{\Delta \omega}=\frac{\omega_{0}}{2\beta}$$
