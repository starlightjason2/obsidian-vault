# 4.4 - Wave Equation

> [!def] Dispersion Relation
> A relation between wave number $k$ and angular frequency $\omega$.

^dispersion-relation

> [!def] 1D Wave Equation
> Let $\psi(x,t)$ describe the amplitude of a wave at position $x$ at time $t$ with *wave speed* $v$. The wave equation in one dimension is a [[4.3.2 - Second Order Linear PDEs#^homogeneous-2nd-order-linear-pde]].
> $$\frac{\partial^2 \psi}{\partial t^2}-v^{2}\,\frac{\partial^2 \psi}{\partial x^2}=0$$
>
> The general solution is spanned by two [[#^plane-wave]](s).
> $$\begin{align}
\psi(x,t)&=Ae^{i(kx-\omega t)}+Be^{-i(kx-\omega t)}
\end{align}$$

^aeb7c2

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^aeb7c2]]
Consider a string under tension, with *small vertical displacement* $u(x,t)$ at point $x$ at time $t$ and $u(x+\Delta x,t)$ at point $x+\Delta x$. Let $T_{1}$, $T_{2}$ be the opposing tension at the respective points, forming angle $\theta_{1}$, $\theta_{2}$ from the horizontal. If there is no longitudinal motion, the forces in the $x$ direction are balanced. By [[1.3 - Newton's Laws#^newtons-second-law]],

$$
\begin{align}
T_{2}\cos\theta_{2}-T_{1}\cos\theta_{1} =0, \quad
T_{2}\sin \theta_{2}-T_{1}\sin\theta_{1}=m\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2}
\end{align}
$$

Using the first order [[6.2 - Taylor Series#^taylor-series-for-cos]] $\cos\theta\approx1$, we get a single tension $T_{2}=T_{1}=T$. Using the first order [[6.2 - Taylor Series#^taylor-series-for-sin]] $\sin\theta\approx\theta$, we get $\theta_{1}\approx\Delta u / \Delta x$, $\theta_{2}\approx\Delta u(x+\Delta x) / \Delta x$.

$$
\begin{align}
T(\theta_{2}-\theta_{1})&=m\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2} \\[0.5em]
T\left( \frac{\Delta u(x+\Delta x)-\Delta u(x)}{\Delta x} \right)&=m\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2}
\end{align}
$$

^aebf6a

The diagonal length $L$ is $\sqrt{\Delta x^{2}+\Delta u(x)^{2}}=\Delta x\,\sqrt{1+(\Delta u / \Delta x)^{2}}$ and $(\Delta u / \Delta x)^{2}\ll1$ is negligible because $\Delta u / \Delta x < 1$. So $L\approx\Delta x$. Mass can be approximated as $m=\mu\, L\approx \mu\,\Delta x$ where $\mu$ is *linear mass density*. Substitute in [[#^aebf6a]].

$$
\begin{align}
T\left( \frac{\Delta u(x+\Delta x)-\Delta u(x)}{\Delta x^{2}} \right)=T\,\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2}&=\frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2}
\end{align}
$$

^945957

By the limit definition of the [[3.7 - Multiple Derivatives#^second-partial-derivative]], the left side of [[#^945957]] is $u_{xx}$, and $T$ is any constant.
`\end{proof}`

## Travelling Waves

> [!def] Plane Wave
> A 1D travelling plane wave is the [[3.7 - Periodic Functions#^complex-exponential]] solution of the [[#^aeb7c2]], moving in one direction with amplitude $A$, wave number $k$, angular frequency $\omega$, and phase $\phi$, bound by a [[#^dispersion-relation]] for its medium.
> $$\psi(x,t)=\mathrm{Re}\Big[Ae^{i(kx-\omega t)}\Big]=A\cos(kx-\omega t+\phi)$$

^plane-wave

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^plane-wave]]
Try the Ansatz $u(x,t)=X(x)\,T(t)$ to $v^{2}\,u_{xx}=u_{tt}$. Then $u_{x}=T(t)\,X'(x)$, $u_{xx}=T(t)\,X''(x)$ and $u_{t}=X(x)\,\dot{T}(t)$, $u_{tt}=X(x)\,\ddot{T}(t)$.

$$
c^{2}\, T(t)\frac{d^{2}X}{dx^{2}}=X(x)\frac{d^{2}T}{dt^{2}}
$$

^4ac11d

Divide both sides of [[#^4ac11d]] by $u(x,t)=X(x)\,T(t)$.

$$
\frac{c^{2}}{X(x)}\frac{d^{2}X}{dx^{2}}=\frac{1}{T(t)}\frac{d^{2}T}{dt^{2}}=C
$$

^b5bb37

Because $X, \ T$ are independent by definition, second derivatives in [[#^b5bb37]] must equal a constant $C$. Choose $C=-\omega^{2}$. Then [[#^b5bb37]] reduces to two ODEs, where each is a [[4.1 - Simple Harmonic Oscillation#^d9cde8]] in space and time respectively.

$$
\begin{align}
\frac{d^{2}X}{dx^{2}}+\frac{\omega^{2}}{c^{2}}\,X(x)&=0, \quad \frac{d^{2}T}{dt^{2}}+\omega^{2}\,T(t)=0
\end{align}
$$

Therefore, $X(x)=A_{1}\cos\frac{\omega}{c} x+A_{2}\sin \frac{\omega}{c}x$ and $T(t)=B_{1}\cos\omega t+ B_{2}\sin \omega t$. Define wave number $k\equiv \omega / c$. Then the solution $u$ is

$$
\begin{align}
u(x,t)=X(x)&=(A_{1}\cos kx+A_{2}\cos kx)(B_{2}\cos \omega t+B_{2}\sin \omega t \\
&=A\cos kx\cos \omega t+B\cos kx\sin \omega t+C\sin kx\cos \omega t t+D\sin kx\sin \omega t
\end{align}
$$
Which can be reduced to the standard form.
`\end{proof}`

> [!def] Phase Velocity
> The [[3.6 - Complex Numbers#^phase]] velocity $v$ of a [[#^plane-wave]] with wavenumber $k$ and angular velocity $\omega$ is defined as
> $$v\equiv \frac{\omega}{k}$$

> [!def] Material Velocity
> The **material velocity** of a [[#^plane-wave]] $\psi(x,t)$ is the [[3.5 - Derivatives#^partial-derivative]] with respect to time.
> $$\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial t}=A\omega \sin(kx-\omega t+\phi)$$

^545dbf

### Reflection at a Boundary

> [!thm] Plane Wave at a Boundary
> Consider an incident [[#^plane-wave]] $u_{I}$ hitting a boundary at $x=0$ and breaking into a *transmitted* wave $u_{T}$ and *reflected* wave $u_{R}$.
>
> $$u_{I}=A\cos kx\cos \omega t+B\cos kx\sin \omega t+C\sin kx\cos \omega t+D\sin kx\sin \omega t=u_{T}+u_{R}$$
>
> Let the medium at $x<0$ have $k_{1}$, and medium at $x>0$ have $k_{2}$. The two boundary conditions are expressed in terms of the [[#^reflection-coefficient]] $R$ and [[#^transmission-coefficient]] $T$.
> 1. $$A+B=C\implies1 + R =T$$
> 2. $$k_{1}A-k_{1}B=k_{2}C \implies k_{1}-k_{1}\,R=k_{2}\,T$$

^plane-wave-at-a-boundary

> [!def] Reflection Coefficient
> Given a [[#^plane-wave-at-a-boundary]] where the first medium has wave number $k_{1}$ and the second medium has wave number $k_{2}$, the **reflection coefficient** $R$ measures the fraction of the incident amplitude reflecting back into the first medium.
> $$R\equiv \frac{B}{A}=\frac{k_{1}-k_{2}}{k_{1}+k_{2}}$$

^reflection-coefficient

> [!def] Transmission Coefficient
> Given a [[#^plane-wave-at-a-boundary]] where the first medium has wave number $k_{1}$ and the second medium has wave number $k_{2}$, the **transmission coefficient** $T$ measures the fraction of the incident amplitude transmitting into the second medium.
> $$T\equiv \frac{C}{A}=\frac{2k_{1}}{k_{1}+k_{2}}$$

^transmission-coefficient
