# 4.6 - Standing Waves

### Closed at Both Ends

> [!def] Closed Standing Wave
> Consider a medium of length $L$ closed at both ends. A [[4.4 - Wave Equation#^a3c7d1]] $u_{1}$ hits the boundary and reflects back as equal and opposite $u_{2}$.
>
>$$\begin{align}
\psi(x,t)&= A\cos(\omega t-kx-\phi)+A\cos(\omega t+kx+\phi) \\
&=A\cos kx\cos \omega t+B\cos kx\sin \omega t+C\sin kx\cos \omega t+D\sin kx\sin \omega t
\end{align}$$
>
> Continuity implies the wavefunction and [[#^545dbf]] are both zero at $x=0, \ x=L$.
> 1. $$\psi(0,t)=\psi(L,t)=0$$
> 2. $$\dot{\psi}(0,t)=\dot{\psi}(L,t)=0$$
> 
> These boundary conditions impose the quantization $n\pi=kL$.
> $$\begin{align}
\psi(x,t)&= A\sin \left( \frac{n\pi x}{L} \right)\cos \left( \frac{n\pi vt}{L} -\phi\right)
\end{align}$$

^287dbf

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^287dbf]]
The fixed end imply boundary conditions for the wave and it's [[#^545dbf]]; both must be nil at the ends: $u(0,t)=\dot{u}(0,t)=0$.

$$
\begin{align}
u(0,0)&=A_{1}\cos\phi_{1}+A_{2}\cos\phi_{2}&=0 \\
\dot{u}(0,0)&=\omega A_{1}\sin \phi_{1} -\omega A_{2}\sin \phi_{2}&=0
\end{align}
$$

^af9aeb

Manipulating the trig ratios, we get

$$
\begin{align}
\frac{A_{2}}{A_{1}}=-\frac{{\cos \phi_{1}}}{\cos \phi_{2}}&= \frac{\sin\phi_{1}}{\sin \phi_{2}} \implies \\[0.5em]
-\frac{\sin \phi_{1}}{\cos \phi_{2}}&=\frac{\sin \phi_{2}}{\cos \phi_{2}}\implies  \\[0.5em]-\tan \phi_{1}&=\tan \phi_{2}\implies -\phi_{1}=\phi_{2}
\end{align}
$$

Plugging into [[#^af9aeb]] and using the even properties of cosine,

$$
\frac{A_{2}}{A_{1}}=-\frac{\cos (\phi_{1})}{\cos(-\phi_{1})}=-\frac{\cos \phi_{1}}{\cos \phi_{1}}=-1\implies A_{2}=-A_{1}
$$

Therefore, we can write $u(x,t)$ as the sum of two identical traveling waves moving in opposite directions. Using the sum identity $\cos(a\pm b)=\cos a\cos b\mp \sin a\sin b$,

$$
\begin{align}
u(x,t) &= A\Big(\cos\big(kx - (\omega t+\phi)\big) - \cos\big(kx + (\omega t+\phi)\big)\Big) \\[0.5em]
        &= A\Big(
            \cos(kx)\cos(\omega t+\phi) + \sin(kx)\sin(\omega t+\phi) \\
        &\phantom{{}=A\Big(} - \cos(kx)\cos(\omega t+\phi) + \sin(kx)\sin(\omega t+\phi)
           \Big) \\[0.5em]
        &= A\Big(2\sin(kx)\sin(\omega t+\phi)\Big) \\[0.5em]
        &= 2A\sin(kx)\sin(\omega t+\phi)
\end{align}
$$

Therefore, $u(x,t)=2A\sin kx\sin(\omega t+\phi)$. With the other end fixed, $u(L,t)=0$, we get

$$
u(L,t)=2A\sin kL\sin (\omega t+\phi)=0
$$

^d25e09

In order for [[#^d25e09]] to be true for any t, $\sin kL=0$, which means $kL=n\pi$. Given a wave velocity $v$, we also know $\omega= n\pi v / L$.

`\end{proof}`

### Closed at One End

> [!thm] Open Standing Wave
> A [[4.4 - Wave Equation#^aeb7c2]] $\psi(x,t)$ fixed at only point $x=0$ ($u(0,t)=\dot{u}(0,t)=0$) requires the condition $kL=(2n-1)\, \pi / 2$.
$$k=\frac{\pi\,(2n-1 )}{2L}$$
