# 4.8 - Coupled Oscillators

> [!def] Coupled Oscillators
> A system of $n$ coupled [[4.1 - Simple Harmonic Oscillation#^d9cde8|Simple Harmonic Oscillators]] with displacements $q_{i}$. The equations of motion form a [[4.1.6 - Second Order Systems#^sysdef2|Second Order Linear System]], often represented by a [[#^stiffness-matrix]].
>
> $$\ddot{\mathbf{x}}=-\mathbf{K}\,\mathbf{x}$$
>
>

^coupled-oscillators

> [!def] Stiffness Matrix
> The stiffness [[2.2 - Matrices#^5bf009]] $\mathbf{K}$ is a symmetric positive-definite matrix encoding frequencies for [[#^coupled-oscillators]].
>

^stiffness-matrix

## Normal Modes

> [!def] Normal Mode
> Normal modes are an [[2.1 - Vectors#^orthonormal-vectors|Orthonormal Set]] of [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^eiegenvector|Eigenvectors]] $\left\{\mathbf{x}_{i} \right\}$ of the [[#^stiffness-matrix]] $\mathbf{K}$ of a system of [[#^coupled-oscillators]].
>
> In a normal mode, every mass oscillates at the *same* characteristic frequency given by [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^eiegenvalue|Eigenvalue]] $\omega_{i}=\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}$, with relative amplitudes given by the components of the respective eigenvector $\mathbf{x}_{i}$.

^normal-mode

### General Solution

> [!thm] Normal Mode Solutions to Coupled Oscillators
> The [[#^normal-mode|Normal Modes]] form an [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^eigenbasis]] for $n$ [[#^coupled-oscillators]] modeled by $\ddot{\mathbf{x}}+\mathbf{K}\,\mathbf{x}=0$. In the basis, the system decouples into $n$ independent oscillator in $q_{i}=\mathbf{v}_{i}^{T}\,\mathbf{x}$:
> $$\ddot{q}_{i}+\omega_{i}^{2}\,q_{i}=0;\quad q_{i}=\mathbf{v}_{i}^{T}\,\mathbf{x}$$
>
> Therefore, the general solution is the span of the normal modes.
> $$\mathbf{x}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{n}\big(e^{i\omega_{i}t}+e^{-i\omega_{i}t}\big)\,\mathbf{v}_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(a_{i}\cos\omega_{i}t+b_{i}\sin\omega_{i}t\big)\,\mathbf{v}_{i}$$
>
>

^normal-mode-decomposition

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^normal-mode-decomposition]]
Symmetry of $\mathbf{K}$ guarantees a real eigenbasis. Using the [[4.1.6 - Second Order Systems#^eigenvalue-solution-to-second-order-systems|Second Order Eigenvalue Ansatz]], substituting $\mathbf{x}=e^{\mu t}\,\mathbf{v}$ into $\ddot{\mathbf{x}}=-\mathbf{K}\,\mathbf{x}$ gives $\mu^{2}\,\mathbf{v}=-\mathbf{K}\,\mathbf{v}$, so $\mu^{2}=-\omega_{i}^{2}$ and $\mu=\pm i\omega_{i}$.

Taking real combinations of $e^{\pm i\omega_{i}t}\,\mathbf{v}_{i}$ gives $\cos\omega_{i}t\,\mathbf{v}_{i}$ and $\sin\omega_{i}t\,\mathbf{v}_{i}$.

`\end{proof}`

## Lattices

> [!thm] Masses on a Lattice
> A periodic chain of $N$ equally-spaced positions $x_{n}=na$ for $n=1,\ldots,N$ is a **Lattice** with **lattice spacing** $a$, and each $x_{n}$ is a **lattice site**. On a closed ring the indices have [[4.0 - ODEs#^periodic-boundary-conditions]] and wrap as $n+N\equiv n$.
>
> Placing $N$ identical masses $m$ at the lattice sites and coupling nearest neighbors with springs of constant $k$ produces [[#^coupled-oscillators]] $m\ddot{x}_{j}=-k(2x_{j}-x_{j+1}-x_{j-1})$ for every $j$, represented by matrix $\mathbf{K}$ built from [[2.2 - Special Matrices#^shift-up-matrix|Cyclic Up-Shift]] $\mathbf{S}^{\uparrow}$ and [[2.2 - Special Matrices#^shift-down-matrix|Cyclic Down-Shift]] $\mathbf{S}^{\downarrow}$:
> $$\mathbf{K}=\frac{k}{m}\begin{bmatrix}
2 & -1 & 0 & \cdots & 0 & -1 \\
-1 & 2 & -1 & \cdots & 0 & 0 \\
0 & -1 & 2 & \cdots & 0 & 0 \\
\vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots & \vdots \\
0 & 0 & 0 & \cdots & 2 & -1 \\
-1 & 0 & 0 & \cdots & -1 & 2
\end{bmatrix}=\frac{k}{m}\big(2\mathbf{I}-\mathbf{S}^{\uparrow}-\mathbf{S}^{\downarrow}\big)$$
>
> The shift matrices [[2.2 - Special Matrices#^f5881e|share an eigenbasis]] with $\mathbf{K}$. Their [[2.2 - Special Matrices#^spectrum-of-cyclic-shifts|eigenvalues]] $\lambda_{l}=e^{2\pi i l/N}$ for $l=0,1,\ldots,N-1$ give the [[#^normal-mode|Normal Modes]] indexed by wavenumber $q_{l}=2\pi l/(Na)$ over the [[#^brillouin-zone|Brillouin Zone]]:
> $$\mathbf{v}_{l}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\begin{pmatrix}1 \\ \lambda_{l} \\ \lambda_{l}^{2} \\ \vdots \\ \lambda_{l}^{N-1}\end{pmatrix},\qquad \omega_{l}^{2}=\frac{2k}{m}\big(1-\cos q_{l}a\big)$$

^lattice

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^lattice]]
Because $[\mathbf{S}^{\uparrow},\mathbf{S}^{\downarrow}]=0$ and $\mathbf{S}^{\downarrow}=(\mathbf{S}^{\uparrow})^{-1}$, any eigenvector $\mathbf{v}_{l}$ of $\mathbf{S}^{\uparrow}$ with eigenvalue $\lambda_{l}=e^{2\pi i l/N}$ satisfies $\mathbf{S}^{\downarrow}\mathbf{v}_{l}=\lambda_{l}^{-1}\mathbf{v}_{l}$. Acting $\mathbf{K}$ on $\mathbf{v}_{l}$:

$$\mathbf{K}\mathbf{v}_{l}=\frac{k}{m}\big(2-\lambda_{l}-\lambda_{l}^{-1}\big)\mathbf{v}_{l}$$

Using $\lambda_{l}+\lambda_{l}^{-1}=2\cos(2\pi l/N)$:

$$\omega_{l}^{2}=\frac{2k}{m}\left(1-\cos\!\left(\frac{2\pi l}{N}\right)\right)=\frac{2k}{m}\big(1-\cos q_{l}a\big)$$

The mass $j$ in mode $l$ has displacement:

$$x_{j}^{(l)}(t)=\frac{e^{i(q_{l}\,ja-\omega_{l}t)}}{\sqrt{N}}$$
`\end{proof}`

### Brillouin Zone

> [!def] Brillouin Zone
> The interval of distinct wavenumbers $q\in(-\pi/a,\pi/a]$ for a [[#^lattice]] with spacing $a$.
>
> Wavenumbers outside this interval differ from one inside by a multiple of $2\pi/a$ and produce the same physical [[#^normal-mode|Normal Mode]], so the Brillouin zone is the smallest set of $q$ that already contains every distinct mode.
> $$q\in(-\pi/a,\pi/a]$$

^brillouin-zone

### Continuum Limit

> [!example] Infinite Masses on a Ring
> In the limit $N\to\infty$ of [[#^lattice|Masses on a Lattice]], the discrete wavenumber $q_{l}=2\pi l/(Na)$ becomes continuous over the [[#^brillouin-zone|Brillouin Zone]], and the discrete frequency becomes a [[4.4 - Plane Waves#^dispersion-relation|Dispersion Relation]].
>
> $$\omega(q)=\sqrt{\frac{4k}{m}}\sin\left( \frac{qa}{2} \right)$$

^infinite-masses-on-a-ring
