# 4.8 - Coupled Oscillators

> [!def] Coupled Oscillators
> A system of $n$ coupled [[4.1 - Simple Harmonic Oscillation#^d9cde8|Simple Harmonic Oscillators]] with positions $x_{i}$ linked by springs. The equations of motion form a [[4.1.6 - Second Order Systems#^sysdef2|Second Order Linear System]] represented by a 
>
> $$\ddot{\mathbf{x}}=-\mathbf{K}\,\mathbf{x}$$
>
>

^c460db

> [!def] Stiffness Matrix
> The **stiffness matrix** $\mathbf{K}$ is of $n$ [[#^c460db]] is a symmetric positive-definite matrix encoding the spring couplings between adjacent masses.
>

^576221

## Normal Modes

> [!def] Normal Mode
> A normal mode is an [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^b325d4|Eigenvector]] $\mathbf{v}_{i}$ of the [[#^576221]] $\mathbf{K}$. In a normal mode, every mass oscillates at the *same* characteristic frequency $\omega_{i}=\sqrt{\lambda_{i}}$, with relative amplitudes given by the components of $\mathbf{v}_{i}$.

^normalmode

> [!thm] Normal Mode Decomposition
> Since $\mathbf{K}$ is symmetric positive-definite, every [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^888169|Eigenvalue]] is positive, $\lambda_{i}=\omega_{i}^{2}>0$. The general solution to $\ddot{\mathbf{x}}=-\mathbf{K}\,\mathbf{x}$ is a superposition of [[#^normalmode|Normal Modes]]:
> $$\mathbf{x}(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(a_{i}\cos\omega_{i}t+b_{i}\sin\omega_{i}t\big)\,\mathbf{v}_{i}$$
> In the [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^1a2381|Eigenbasis]] of $\mathbf{K}$, the system decouples into $n$ independent [[4.1 - Simple Harmonic Oscillation#^d9cde8|Simple Harmonic Oscillators]] in **normal coordinates** $q_{i}=\mathbf{v}_{i}^{T}\,\mathbf{x}$:
> $$\ddot{q}_{i}+\omega_{i}^{2}\,q_{i}=0$$

^modedecomp

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^modedecomp]]
Symmetry of $\mathbf{K}$ guarantees a real [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^1a2381|Orthonormal Eigenbasis]]; positive-definiteness gives $\lambda_{i}>0$. By the [[4.1.6 - Second Order Systems#^eigans2|Second Order Eigenvalue Ansatz]], substituting $\mathbf{x}=e^{\mu t}\,\mathbf{v}$ into $\ddot{\mathbf{x}}=-\mathbf{K}\,\mathbf{x}$ gives $\mu^{2}\,\mathbf{v}=-\mathbf{K}\,\mathbf{v}$, so $\mu^{2}=-\omega_{i}^{2}$ and $\mu=\pm i\omega_{i}$. Taking real combinations of $e^{\pm i\omega_{i}t}\,\mathbf{v}_{i}$ gives $\cos\omega_{i}t\,\mathbf{v}_{i}$ and $\sin\omega_{i}t\,\mathbf{v}_{i}$.

Equivalently, with the [[2.2 - Matrix Decomposition#^4fddab|Diagonalizing Matrix]] $\mathbf{P}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{v}_{1} & \dots & \mathbf{v}_{n}\end{bmatrix}$, change variables to $\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{P}^{-1}\,\mathbf{x}$. Then $\ddot{\mathbf{q}}=-\mathbf{D}\,\mathbf{q}$ where $\mathbf{D}=\operatorname{diag}(\omega_{1}^{2},\dots,\omega_{n}^{2})$, so each component obeys $\ddot{q}_{i}+\omega_{i}^{2}\,q_{i}=0$.
`\end{proof}`
