# 2.1.3 - Electric Dipoles

## Dipoles

> [!def] Dipole Moment
> An electric **dipole** is a pair of equal and opposite charges $\pm q$ at a close distance $\mathbf{d}$ from each other. The **dipole moment** $\mathbf{p}\left[\pu{C.m}\right]$ of a dipole is a vector pointing from the negative to the positive charge.
>
> $$\mathbf{p}=q\mathbf{d}$$

^21e3cd

### Constant Electric Field

The two charges of a dipole experience opposite forces in an electric field, creating a **[[1.7 - Fixed Axis Rotation#^cbafb0]]** at distance $\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{d}$ from the negative charge (the center of the dipole). The vector from the positive charge to the center will be $-\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{d}$.

$$
\begin{align}
\mathbf{\tau}&=\left( \frac{1}{2}\mathbf{d}\times \mathbf{F}_{+} \right)+\left( -\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{d}\times \mathbf{F}_{-} \right) \\
&=\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{d}\times \mathbf{F})-\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{d}\times-\mathbf{F}) \\
&=\mathbf{F}\times \mathbf{d}
\end{align}
$$

Or, by the definition of the electric field,

$$
\mathbf{\tau}=q\mathbf{E}\times \mathbf{d}
$$

We can then substitute the dipole moment.

$$\mathbf{\tau}=\mathbf{p}\times \mathbf{d}$$

### Potential of a Dipole

> [!thm] Potential of a Dipole
> The [[2.1.2 - Electric Potential#^4995c8]] of a [[#^21e3cd]] $\mathbf{p}$ at a point $\mathbf{r}$ is
>
> $$V(\mathbf{r})=k_{e}\frac{\mathbf{p}\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}}{\lVert{\mathbf{r}}\rVert ^{2}}$$

^e26dd1

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^e26dd1]]
Consider a dipole centered at the origin.

![[Pasted image 20250721010138.png]]

Let the charges $\pm q$ be separated by $d\left[\pu{m}\right]$, so $\mathbf{r}_{q^{_+}}=\frac{d}{2}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{\jmath}}$ and $\mathbf{r}_{q^{-}}=-\frac{d}{2}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{\jmath}}$. The distance from each charge to a point $(x,y,z)$ is then

$$r_{q^{\pm}}=\sqrt{x^{2}+\left(y\pm \frac{d}{2} \right)^{2}}$$

Or in polar coordinates,

$$
\begin{align}
r_{q^{\pm}}&=\sqrt{r^{2}\sin^{2}(\theta)+\left(r\cos(\theta)\pm \frac{d}{2} \right)^{2}}  \\
&=\sqrt{r^{2}\sin^{2}(\theta)+r^{2}\cos^{2}(\theta)\pm r\,d\cos(\theta)+\cancel{\frac{d}{4}^{2}}} \\
\end{align}
$$

Let $\frac{d^{2}}{4}\to 0$ since $d\ll \lVert{\mathbf{r}}\rVert$.

$$
\begin{align}
r_{q\pm}&=\sqrt{r^{2}\left( \sin^{2}(\theta)+\cos^{2}(\theta)\pm {\frac{d}{r}}\cos(\theta) \right)}  \\
&=r\sqrt{1\pm {\frac{d}{r}}\cos(\theta)}
\end{align}
$$

By the [[2.1.2 - Electric Potential#^1d5081]] and the superposition principle,

$$
\begin{align}
V&=\frac{k_{e}q}{r\sqrt{1+ {\frac{d}{r}}\cos(\theta)}}+\frac{-k_{e}q}{r\sqrt{1-\frac{d}{r}\cos(\theta)}} \\
&=\frac{k_{e}\,q}{r}\left( \frac{1}{\sqrt{1+ {\frac{d}{r}}\cos(\theta)}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\frac{d}{r}\cos(\theta)}} \right)
\end{align}
$$

Using the first order [[6.2 - Taylor Series#^95d5fe]] approximation $\frac{1}{\sqrt{1\pm x}}\approx 1\pm\frac{x}{2}$

$$
\begin{align}
V(\mathbf{r})&=\frac{k_{e}\,q}{r} \left( \left( 1+\frac{\frac{d}{r}\cos(\theta)}{2} \right)-\left( 1-\frac{\frac{d}{r}\cos(\theta)}{2} \right)\right) \\
&=\frac{k_{e}\,q}{r} \left(\cancel{1}+\frac{d\cos(\theta)}{2r}\cancel{-1}+\frac{d\cos(\theta)}{2r} \right) \\
&=\frac{k_{e}\,q}{r} \left( \frac{d}{r}\cos(\theta) \right) \\&=\frac{k_{e}q}{r^{2}}\,d\cos(\theta)
\end{align}
$$

Which simplifies further with the dot product identity,

$$
V(\mathbf{r})=\frac{k_{e}q}{r^{2}}\mathbf{d}\cdot \,\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}
$$

Substitute the dipole moment for the final formula.
`\end{proof}`
