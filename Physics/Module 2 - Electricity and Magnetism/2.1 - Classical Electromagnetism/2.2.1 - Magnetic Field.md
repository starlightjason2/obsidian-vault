# 2.2.1 - Magnetic Field

> [!def] Magnetic Field
> The **magnetic field** is a [[3.3 - Vector Fields#^3ea3bd]], and is the consequence of a **changing electric field** via [[#^adbf11]].

^5b0b4d

> [!def] Magnetic Potential
> For a 2.1.4 - Current distribution described by $\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{r}')$, the magnetic vector potential $\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{r})\left[\pu{}\right]$ is the [[3.9 - Integrals#^94174a]] of the [[2.1.4 - Current#^17d99f]] over the separation $\lvert{\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'}\rvert\,$.
> $\frac{\mu_{0}}{4\pi}\iiint \frac{\mathbf{J}(\mathbf{r}')}{\lvert{\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'}\rvert\,}dV'$

## Ampere's Law

> [!thm] Ampere's Law
> The [[2.2.1 - Magnetic Field#^5b0b4d]] $\mathbf{B}$ obeys [[3.11 - Fundamental Theorems#^b14c63]]. Consider a wire that has a *cross-section $S$* with *boundary $C$*.
>
> The circulation of $\mathbf{B}$ around $C$ is proportional to the [[2.1.4 - Current#^45defa|Current]] through $C$.
>
>
> $$\oint \mathbf{B} \cdot d\mathbf{r}=\mu_{0}\,I_{enc}$$
>
> Or, the curl of $\mathbf{B}$ is the [[2.1.4 - Current#^17d99f]] $\mathbf{J}$ through $S$.
>
> $$\nabla \times \mathbf{B}(\mathbf{r})=\mu_{0}\,\mathbf{J}$$

^349c25

`\begin{proof}` [[#^349c25]]
By definition, the current is the flux of the current density through a surface $S$, like the surface of a wire.

$$\mathbf{I}=\iint_{S}\mathbf{J}\cdot d\mathbf{A}$$

By [[3.11 - Fundamental Theorems#^b14c63]] for the magnetic vector field $\mathbf{B}$, the circulation around the boundary of $S$ is the flux of the curl through $S$.

$$
\oint \mathbf{B}\cdot d\mathbf{r}=\iint_{S}(\nabla\times\mathbf{B})\cdot d\mathbf{A}
$$

`\end{proof}`

> [!example] Ampere's Law with Cylindrical Symmetry
> With the right hand rule, determine that $\mathbf{B}=B\,\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}$. Choose an Amperian loop where $d\mathbf{r}$ points parallel to $\mathbf{B}$. Use [[2.6 - Coordinate Sytems#^f22626]] where $d\phi=0, \ dz=0$, we get $d\mathbf{r}=s\,d\phi\,\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}$. Substitute into [[#^349c25]].
>
>$$\begin{align}
\mu_{0}\,I_{enc}&=\oint B\,\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}\cdot s\,d\phi\,\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}} \\
&=\int_{0}^{2\pi} B\,s\,d\phi =2\pi\,s\,B
\end{align}$$
> $$\mathbf{B}=\frac{\mu_{0}\,I}{2\pi\,s}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{\phi}}$$

## Force from a Magnetic Field

> [!thm] Lorentz's Law
> A charge $q$ with velocity $\mathbf{v}$ in a [[#^5b0b4d]] $\mathbf{B}$ experiences a Lorentz Force $\mathbf{F}$.
> $$\mathbf{F}=q\mathbf{v}\times \mathbf{B}$$

^adbf11

> [!exm] Magnetic Force on a Straight Current
> Since current is a movement of charge, a wire carrying it will experience a force within a [[#^5b0b4d]].
>
> Substitute [[2.1.4 - Current#^0ad91f]] into [[#^adbf11]]. The charge will the elementary charge $q=e$. Define $d\mathbf{l}$ along the length of the wire.$$\begin{align}
v_{d}&=\frac{I}{neA}\\
d\mathbf{F_{d}}&=e(\mathbf{v}_{d})\times\mathbf{B}
\end{align}$$
> This gives us the differential magnetic force on a **single charge carrier**. We can multiply by the **number of charge carriers** to get the **net differential force** $d\mathbf{F}=\sum \mathbf{F}_{d}$. For a volume with **unit length $l=1$**, the number of charge carriers is $nV=nAl=nA$. We can define $\mathbf{v}_{d}$ along the direction of the wire $d\mathbf{l}$.
>
> $$
> \begin{align}
> d\mathbf{F}&=(e)(nA\,d{l})(\mathbf{v}_{d})\times \mathbf{B} \\
> &=(\cancel{neA}) \frac{I}{\cancel{neA}}\mathbf{v}_{d}\,dl\times \mathbf{B} \\
> \end{align}
> $$
>
> $$\mathbf{F}=I\mathbf{l}\times\mathbf{B}$$

## Magnetic Field from Current (Biot-Savart)

> [!thm] Biot Savart Law
> The [[#^5b0b4d]] $d\mathbf{B}$ at a point mdue to a wire with [[2.1.4 - Current#^45defa|Current]] $I$ with a differential length $d\mathbf{l}$ is
> $$\mathbf{B}=\frac{\mu_{0}}{4\pi}\int \frac{Id\mathbf{l}\times \,\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}}{\lVert{\mathbf{r}}\rVert^{2}}$$

^9be085

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^9be085]]
Given a [[#^6840ed]] $\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{r})$, we know the [[#^5b0b4d]] is .

 $$
\begin{align} \\
\mathbf{B}&=\nabla \times \mathbf{A} \\
&=\nabla \times\left( k\iiint \frac{\mathbf{J'}\,dV'}{\lvert{\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'}\rvert\,} \right) \\
&=k\left(\iiint -\frac{\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}'}{(\mathbf{r}-\mathbf{r}')^{3}} \times(\mathbf{J}'\,dV')\right)
\end{align}
$$
`\end{proof}`

> [!exm] Magnetic Field of Wire
> Consider an infinite straight wire $\mathbf{l}=x\,\hat{\boldsymbol{\imath}}$ with a current $I$ ($d\mathbf{l}=dx\,\hat{\boldsymbol{\imath}}$). Evaluate the magnetic field at a point $\mathbf{r}=R\,\hat{\boldsymbol{\jmath}}$. Then by the [[#^9be085]],
>
> $$
> \mathbf{B}=\frac{\mu_{0}}{4\pi}\int \frac{Idx}{r^{2}} \,\hat{\boldsymbol{\imath}}\times \,\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}
> $$
