# 2.2 - Faraday's Law

> [!def] Magnetic Flux
> The **magnetic flux** is the amount of magnetic field going through a surface.
> $$\phi_m= \int_{S}\mathbf{B}\cdot \,\hat{\boldsymbol{n}}dA  $$

> [!thm] Faraday's Law
> A changing magnetic field creates an electric field. More formally, a **changing magnetic flux** induces an **emf** $\Delta V$, also called a [[2.3.1 - Ohm's Law#^23952c]]. The voltage, or difference in **potential**, is just the [[1.5 - Work and Energy#^e395c7]] done by an [[2.1.1 - Electric Field#^bdfb98]] in a circuit over a length $d\mathbf{l}$.
> $$\vec\varepsilon=-\frac{d\phi _{m}}{dt}=\oint \mathbf{E}\cdot d\mathbf{l}$$

^262ce8

> [!cor] Lenz's Law
> The direction of induced emf from [[#^262ce8]] always **opposes** the change in flux causing it.

## Motional Emf

A magnetic flux can change due either the field or the **surface** changing.

> [!exm] Rod through Circuit
> A conducting rod is pulled through a circuit. The circuit is a square with length $l$ and width $x$, and the bar with length $l$ is pulled across $x$, orthogonal to $\mathbf{B}$. So the flux is $\vec{\phi}_{m}=\mathbf{B}lx$. The emf, by Faraday's Law, is $$\vec{\varepsilon}=-\frac{d\phi}{dt}=\frac{d}{dt}\mathbf{B}lx=\mathbf{B}lv$$
> So with Ohm's Law, $$I=\frac{\lVert{\mathbf{B}}\rVert\,lv}{R}$$
