^voltage-phasor

> [!thm] Complex Ohm's Law
> The AC equivalent of [[2.3.1 - Ohm's Law#^ohms-law]] relating [[#^current-phasor]] $I$, [[#^voltage-phasor]] $V$, [[#^impedance]] $Z$.
> $$V=IZ$$

^complex-ohms-law

## Impedance

> [!thm] Impedance
> Impedance $Z$ is a [[3.6 - Complex Numbers#^complex-number]] characterizing generalized AC resistance, with real part being [[2.3.1 - Ohm's Law#^resistance]] $R$ and imaginary part being [[#^reactance]] $X$.
> $$Z=R+i\,X$$

^impedance

> [!def] Reactance
> The imaginary component of complex impedance.

^reactance

### Inductors

> [!def] Inductor Impedance
> The [[2.3.4 - AC Circuits#^impedance]] for an inductor with [[2.3.4 - Inductors#^inductance]] $L$ is
> $$Z_{L}=i\omega L$$

^inductor-impedance

`\begin{proof}`[[#^inductor-impedance]]
Assume $I(t)=I_{0}e^{i\omega t}$. Then, with [[2.3.4 - AC Circuits#^complex-ohms-law]] $V=IZ_{L}$,

 $$Z_{L}=\frac{V_{L}}{I}=\frac{L\dot{I}}{I}=\frac{L(i\omega I_{0}e^{i\omega t})}{I}=i\omega L$$
`\end{proof}`

## Current

> [!def] Current Phasor
> A [[3.6 - Complex Numbers#^complex-number]] representing [[2.1.4 - Current#^current]] as a [[3.7.1 - Sinusoidals#^sinusoidal-function]].

^current-phasor

> [!thm] Admittance
> Admittance $Y$ is a [[3.6 - Complex Numbers#^complex-number]] characterizing how easily AC current will flow. It's the inverse of [[#^impedance]] $Z$.
> $$Y\equiv \frac{1}{Z}$$

^admittance

> [!thm] Bode Plot
> Using [[2.1.2 - Electric Potential#^electric-power]] $P = V^2/R$ and letting $A(\omega)$ be the [[2.3.3 - RLC Circuits#^29c120]]:
> $$\text{dB} = 10\log_{10}\left(\frac{P_{\text{out}}}{P_{\text{in}}}\right) = 20\log_{10}|A(\omega)|$$
