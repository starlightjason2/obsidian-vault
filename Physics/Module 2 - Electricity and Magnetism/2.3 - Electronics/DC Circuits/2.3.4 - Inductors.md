# 2.3.4 - Inductors

> [!def] Inductance
> An inductor is a coil of wire that generates a back [[2.3.1 - Ohm's Law#^voltage]] $V$ opposing an increase in current. Let $L\left[\pu{H}\right]$ be the *inductance*, $I$ be the [[2.1.4 - Current#^current]]. By [[2.2.2 - Faraday’s Law#^262ce8]],
> $$V=-\frac{d\phi_{m}}{dt}\equiv-L\, \frac{dI}{dt}$$

^inductance

## Charge and Current

> [!def] Time Constant of Inductor
> The time constant for an inductor given [[#^inductance]] $L$ and [[2.3.1 - Ohm's Law#^resistance]] $R$ is
> $$\tau=LR$$

^time-constant-of-inductor

> [!def] Inductor Current
> Consider an LR circuit with [[#^inductance]] $L$ and [[2.3.1 - Ohm's Law#^resistance]] $R$ in series. Let $LC=\tau$ be the [[#^time-constant-of-inductor]], $VC=Q_{0}$ be the initial charge of the capacitor , and $I_{0}=\frac{V}{R}$ initial current.
>
> $$
> \begin{align}
I(t)&=\frac{V}{R}(1-e^{-t/\tau}) \\ \\
V_{L}(t)&=Ve^{-t/\tau}
\end{align}
> $$
> 

With [[2.3.1 - Ohm's Law#^kirchhoffs-laws]],

$$
\begin{align}
IR+L \frac{dI}{dt}&=V \\
\frac{dI}{dt}+\frac{R}{L}I&=\frac{V}{L}
\end{align}
$$

Which is solved using the [[4.1.1 - First Order Linear ODEs#^general-solution-of-a-first-order-linear-ode]],

$$
I(t)=Ae^{-tR/L}+B
$$

Substitute $\lim_{t\to\infty}{I}=\frac{V}{R}=A$, $I(0)=0=B$.
