# 4.5 - Optics

> [!thm] The Law of Reflection
> If light hits a reflector at an **incident angle** $\theta_{i}$ and reflects at $\theta_{r}$, then
> $$\theta_{r}=\theta_{i}$$

> [!thm] Snell's Law of Refraction
> If light enters a medium at $\theta_{1}$ and crosses into another medium at $\theta_{2}$, and the **index of refraction** for each medium respectively is $n_{1},n_{2}$, then
> $$n_{1}\sin\theta_{1}=n_{2}\sin\theta_{2}$$

^6ff875

> [!cor] Critical Angle
> The critical angle is the incident angle $\theta_{1}$ that produces a $\theta_{2}=\pu{90deg}$ refraction. By [[#^6ff875]],
> $$\theta_{c}=\sin^{-1}\left( \frac{n_{r}}{n_{i}} \right)$$

## Lenses and Mirrors

A **lens** allows transmission of light through its medium, and a **mirror** reflects all light off its surface.

> [!thm] Mirror Equation
> The **focal length** $f$ is the distance from a lens where light rays **perpendicular** to the lens focus. Consider an object $d_{o}$ away and a projected image $d_{i}$ away.
> If $f>0$, it's a concave mirror, and if $f<0$, it's a **convex** mirrors.
> $$\frac{1}{d_{o}}+\frac{1}{d_{i}}=\frac{1}{f}$$
> For a spherical mirror with **radius of curvature** $R$,
> $$f=\frac{R}{2}$$

> [!cor] Sign Rules for Lenses
> - For object:
> 	1. If $d_{o}>0$, the object is on the same side as the incoming light
> 	2. If $d_{o}<0$, the object is on the opposite side as the incoming light.
> - For image:
> 	1. If $d_{i}>0$, the image is on the same side as the outgoing light (real object, can be seen outside lens).
> 	2. If $d_{i}<0$, the image is on the opposite side as the outgoing light (virtual object, can be seen through lens).

> [!cor] The Center of Curvature
> The center of curvature for a concave lens is the point where the image is the same height as the object.
> $$d_{0}=2f$$

> [!def] Magnification
> For a spherical mirror, the **magnification** $m$ of a projected image through a lens is the ratio of the heights of the image $h_{i}$ and the object $h_{o}$. If $m<0$ the image is **upside down**.
> $$m=\frac{h_{i}}{h_{o}}=-\frac{d_{i}}{d_{o}}$$

> [!def] Far Point
> The far point $f_{far}$ is the farthest distance from your eyes when the object is in focus. The power is
> $P\left[\pu{Diopeters}\right]=\frac{1}{f_{far}}$.
