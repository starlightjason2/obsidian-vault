# 6.0 - Postulates

> [!axm] Quantum States Postulate
> The quantum state of a system includes all the information one can know about it, and can be written as a [[2.1 - Vector Spaces#^f36929]] $\ket{\psi}$ in a [[2.1 - Vector Spaces#^aece1f]] $\mathcal{H}$. The space must be a [[3.6 - Complex Numbers#^8288b7]] expressed in an [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^1a2381]].
>
> All quantum states are normalized and have a [[2.1 - Vectors#^57eeb7]] of $1$.
>
>

^70e0b0

> [!axm] Observable Postulate
> An observable quantity is described by a [[2.7 - Function Spaces#^6146ae]] $\hat{A}\in\mathcal{H}$, written in it's own [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^1a2381]].
>
>

^473d03

> [!axm] Eigenstate Postulate
> When measuring a physical quantity described by $\hat{A}$, the only possible measurement value is an [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^888169]] $a_{n}$ of $\hat{A}$; the corresponding [[2.6 - Eigenvectors and Eigenvalues#^b325d4]] $\Ket{a_{n}}$ is the eigenstate.
> $$\hat{A}\Ket{a_{n}} =a_{n}\Ket{a_{n}} $$
>

^6cee8a

> [!axm] Probability Postulate
> The probability amplitude of measuring a general quantum state $\Ket{\psi}$ in $\Ket{a_{n}}$ is the [[3.6 - Complex Numbers#^c29a3c]] of the [[2.1 - Vectors#^efaf9e]] $\Braket{a_{n} | \psi}$.
>
>
> $$\mathcal{P}(a_{n})=\left\lvert{\Braket{ a_{n}|\psi }}\right\rvert^{2}$$

^a2ed15

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^a2ed15]]
Let $\ket{\psi}=\sum_i c_i\ket{u_i}$ where $c_i=\braket{u_i|\psi}$. Expand the [[6.2 - Uncertainty#^6591bb]] using the completeness of eigenstates:

$$\langle\hat{F}\rangle=\left(\sum_i c_i^*\bra{u_i}\right)\hat{F}\left(\sum_j c_j\ket{u_j}\right)=\sum_{i,j}c_i^* c_j f_j\underbrace{\braket{u_i|u_j}}_{\delta_{ij}}=\sum_i|c_i|^2 f_i$$

Since $\langle\hat{F}\rangle$ is a probability-weighted average of eigenvalues, $|c_i|^2=|\braket{u_i|\psi}|^2$ must be the probability of measuring $f_i$.
`\end{proof}`

> [!axm] Projection Postulate
> A mixed quantum state $\Ket{\psi}$ will randomly collapse into an eigenstate $\Ket{a_{m}}$ when measured. The collapse is modeled with the [[2.5 - Linear Transformations#^b64e03]] of $\Ket{\psi}$ onto $\Ket{a_{m}}$.
> $$\Ket{a_{m}} =\frac{1 }{N}\,{\hat{P}}_{\Ket{a_{m}} }\ket{\psi}$$
>
> A state $\ket{\psi}=c_1\ket{u_1}+c_2\ket{u_2}$ is **coherent** when the relative phase $\varphi=\phi_2-\phi_1$ between components $c_i=A_i e^{i\phi_i}$ is well-defined.

> [!axm] Schrödinger Equation
> The time evolution of a state $\ket{\psi(t)}$ is determined by Schrödinger equation, a [[4.3.1 - First Order Linear PDEs#^c8cc81]]. Here $i$ is the [[3.6 - Complex Numbers#^309c47]] and the $\hat{H}$ is the [[6.3 - Energy#^362b2b]].
>
> $\hat{H}(t)\ket{\psi(t)}= i\hbar\, \frac{\partial}{\partial t}\ket{\psi(t)} $

## Formulations

### Wave Mechanics

> [!def] Wavefunction
> A continuous quantum state $\Ket{\psi}$ is a normalized [[3.1 - Scalar Functions#^f874ea]] $\psi(x)\in \mathcal{H}$ satisfying the [[6.0 - Postulates#^70e0b0]]. The state represents some [[#^7e5b0a]] $\hat{A}$ with infinite possible measurement values $x\in \mathbb{R}$.
>
> $$\Ket{\psi} \doteq \braket{x|\psi}=\psi(x)$$
>
> By the [[6.0 - Postulates#^a2ed15]], the chance of measuring $a\leq x\leq b$ is found with the [[2.7 - Function Spaces#^9dd68a]] $\int_{a}^{b}\left\lvert{\Braket{x | \psi}}\right\rvert^{2}\,dx$.
> $$\mathcal{P}(a\leq x\leq b)=\int_{a}^{b}\left\lvert{\psi(x)}\right\rvert^{2}\,dx$$
>
>
>
>

^bab050

> [!def] Continuous Observable
> An observable **continuous quantity** is represented with a [[2.7 - Function Spaces#^3fa82b]] ${\hat{A}}\in \mathbb{C}^{n}$ satisfying the [[6.0 - Postulates#^473d03]].
>
> Following the [[6.0 - Postulates#^6cee8a]], ${\hat{A}}$ is commonly written in it's eigenbasis, which is a [[2.7 - Function Spaces#^81a43f]].
>
>
> $$\hat{A}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\ket{a} \bra{a} \,da, \quad \Braket{a |a' }=\delta(a-a')$$

^7e5b0a

### Matrix Mechanics

> [!def] Discrete Quantum State
> A discrete quantum state $\Ket{\psi}$ is a [[2.1 - Vectors#^066f80]] $\Ket{\psi}\in \mathbb{C}^{n}$ satisfying the [[6.0 - Postulates#^70e0b0]]. The state represents some [[#^48d0a6]] with finite possible measurement values $a_{n}\in\left\{a_{n} \right\}$.
>
> By the [[6.0 - Postulates#^a2ed15]], each coefficient $c_{n}$ represents probability amplitude of finding eigenstate $\Ket{a_{n}}$, found with [[2.1 - Vectors#^efaf9e]] $c_{n}=\Braket{a_{n} |\psi }$.
> $$\Ket{\psi} \doteq\sum_{n}^{}{}c_{n}\Ket{a_{n}} =\begin{bmatrix}
c_{1} \\
\vdots \\
c_{n}
\end{bmatrix}$$
>
>
>

> [!def] Discrete Observable
> An observable **discrete quantity** is represented with a [[2.2 - Special Matrices#^6146ae]] $\mathbf{\hat{A}}$ satisfying the [[6.0 - Postulates#^473d03]].
>
> Following the [[6.0 - Postulates#^6cee8a]], $\mathbf{\hat{A}}$ is commonly written as a [[2.2 - Matrix Decomposition#^4fddab]] where the [[3.5 - Step and Delta#^7d7ba6]] represents orthonormality.
> $$\mathbf{\hat{A}}=\sum_{n}a_{n}\ket{a_{n}} \bra{a_{n}},\quad \Braket{a_{n} | a_{m}}=\delta_{nm}$$
>
>

## Commutator

> [!thm] Commuting Operators share Eigenstates
> If two observables are represented by [[2.7 - Function Spaces#^17b8a7]] ${\hat{A}}, \ {\hat{B}}$, they have the same eigenstates. See [[2.2 - Special Matrices#^f5881e]].
>
> This means the second measurement does not change the state, so measurement of ${\hat{A}}$ following measurement of ${\hat{B}}$ yields the *same result* as the initial measurement of ${\hat{A}}$. We say that ${\hat{A}}, \ {\hat{B}}$ can be measured *simultaneously*.
