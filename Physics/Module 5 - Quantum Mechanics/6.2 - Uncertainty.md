# 6.2 - Uncertainty Principle

> [!thm] Uncertainty Principle
> Let there be two observables represented by ${\hat{A}}$ and ${\hat{B}}$. The [[#^uncertainty]] product $\Delta \hat{A} \Delta \hat{B}$ is always greater than half the [[#^expectation-value]] of the [[2.8 - Linear Operators#^commutator-2]] $[{\hat{A}},{\hat{B}}]$.
> $$\Delta {\hat{A}}\,\Delta {\hat{B}}\geq \frac{1}{2}\left\lvert{\langle [{\hat{A}},{\hat{B}}] \rangle }\right\rvert$$

^uncertainty-principle

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^uncertainty-principle]]
By the [[2.7 - Function Spaces#^cauchy-schwarz-inequality|Cauchy-Schwarz Inequality]], $\braket{\phi|\phi}\braket{\psi|\psi}\geq|\braket{\phi|\psi}|^2$ for any two states. For any [[2.8 - Linear Operators#^hermitian-operator|Hermitian Operator]] $\hat{A}$, define $\delta\hat{A}=\hat{A}-\langle\hat{A}\rangle$; since $\langle\hat{A}\rangle\in\mathbb{R}$, the [[2.8 - Linear Operators#^shift-of-hermitian-operator-is-hermitian|shifted operator is also Hermitian]].

Setting $\ket{\phi}=\delta\hat{A}\ket{\psi}$ and $\ket{\psi'}=\delta\hat{B}\ket{\psi}$ and applying Cauchy-Schwarz:
$$(\Delta A)^2(\Delta B)^2=\braket{\phi|\phi}\braket{\psi'|\psi'}\geq|\braket{\phi|\psi'}|^2=|\braket{\psi|\delta\hat{A}\,\delta\hat{B}|\psi}|^2$$

Decomposing $\delta\hat{A}\,\delta\hat{B}$ into its [[2.8 - Linear Operators#^commutator-2|Commutator]] and [[2.8 - Linear Operators#^anti-commutator|Anti-Commutator]] parts:
$$\delta\hat{A}\,\delta\hat{B}=\frac{1}{2}[\delta\hat{A},\delta\hat{B}]+\frac{1}{2}\{\delta\hat{A},\delta\hat{B}\}=\frac{1}{2}[\hat{A},\hat{B}]+\frac{1}{2}\{\delta\hat{A},\delta\hat{B}\}$$

Since $[\hat{A},\hat{B}]$ is anti-Hermitian, $i\langle[\hat{A},\hat{B}]\rangle$ is real, and since $\{\delta\hat{A},\delta\hat{B}\}$ is Hermitian its expectation is also real. The squared modulus therefore splits into two non-negative terms:
$$|\braket{\psi|\delta\hat{A}\,\delta\hat{B}|\psi}|^2=\frac{1}{4}|\langle[\hat{A},\hat{B}]\rangle|^2+\frac{1}{4}|\langle\{\delta\hat{A},\delta\hat{B}\}\rangle|^2\geq\frac{1}{4}|\langle[\hat{A},\hat{B}]\rangle|^2$$

Taking square roots gives $\Delta A\,\Delta B\geq\frac{1}{2}|\langle[\hat{A},\hat{B}]\rangle|$.
`\end{proof}`

## Expectation Value

> [!def] Expectation Value
> Let there be an observable represented by $\hat{A}$. The **expectation value** of a measurement is the *mean* of all possible measurements $\{a_{i}\}$ given initial state $\ket{\psi}$.
> $$\langle {\hat{A}} \rangle\equiv\bra{\psi} {\hat{A}} \ket{\psi} =\sum_{n=1}^{N}{a_{n}\mathcal{P}_{a_{n}}}$$

^expectation-value

### Expectation Value Dynamics

> [!thm] Expectation Value Equation of Motion
> For any observable $\hat{A}$ and a state evolving under the [[6.0 - Postulates#^597a72|Schrödinger Equation]]:
> $$\frac{d}{dt}\langle\hat{A}\rangle=\frac{i}{\hbar}\langle[\hat{H},\hat{A}]\rangle+\left\langle\frac{\partial\hat{A}}{\partial t}\right\rangle$$
> For time-independent $\hat{A}$: if $[\hat{H},\hat{A}]=0$, then $\langle\hat{A}\rangle$ is conserved.

^expectation-value-equation-of-motion

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^expectation-value-equation-of-motion]]
Apply the product rule to $\frac{d}{dt}\braket{\psi(t)|\hat{A}|\psi(t)}$, using $i\hbar\partial_t\ket{\psi}=\hat{H}\ket{\psi}$:

$$\frac{d}{dt}\langle\hat{A}\rangle=\frac{-i}{\hbar}\braket{\psi|\hat{H}\hat{A}|\psi}+\frac{i}{\hbar}\braket{\psi|\hat{A}\hat{H}|\psi}+\left\langle\frac{\partial\hat{A}}{\partial t}\right\rangle=\frac{i}{\hbar}\langle[\hat{H},\hat{A}]\rangle+\left\langle\frac{\partial\hat{A}}{\partial t}\right\rangle$$
`\end{proof}`

> [!thm] Ehrenfest Theorem
> The [[6.2 - Uncertainty#^expectation-value|Expectation Values]] of the [[6.4 - Position and Momentum#^f3f4cf]] and the [[6.4 - Position and Momentum#^aace22]] obey [[1.3 - Newton's Laws#^newtons-second-law]]:
> $$m\frac{d^2\langle\hat{x}\rangle}{dt^2}=\frac{d\langle\hat{p}\rangle}{dt}=-\left\langle\frac{\partial V}{\partial x}\right\rangle\approx-\frac{\partial V}{\partial\langle x\rangle}$$
> Classical Newton's laws emerge from quantum averages.

^ehrenfest-theorem

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^ehrenfest-theorem]]
Apply [[#^expectation-value-equation-of-motion]] to $\hat{x}$ and $\hat{p}$ respectively.

For $\hat{x}$: $[\hat{H},\hat{x}]=[-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\partial_x^2,\hat{x}]$. Use $\partial_x^2(x\psi)=2\partial_x\psi+x\partial_x^2\psi$, so $[\partial_x^2,x]=2\partial_x$, giving

$$[\hat{H},\hat{x}]=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\cdot 2\partial_x=\frac{i\hbar}{m}\hat{p}$$
Therefore:

$$\frac{d\langle\hat{x}\rangle}{dt}=\frac{i}{\hbar}\cdot\frac{i\hbar}{m}\langle\hat{p}\rangle=\frac{\langle\hat{p}\rangle}{m}$$

For $\hat{p}$: $[\hat{H},\hat{p}]=[V(x),-i\hbar\partial_x]$ (kinetic term commutes with $\hat{p}$). Applying to $\psi$:

$$V(x)(-i\hbar\partial_x\psi)-(-i\hbar\partial_x)(V\psi)=-i\hbar V\psi'+i\hbar(V'\psi+V\psi')=i\hbar V'$$

So $[\hat{H},\hat{p}]=i\hbar\frac{\partial V}{\partial x}$. Therefore:
$$\frac{d\langle\hat{p}\rangle}{dt}=\frac{i}{\hbar}\cdot i\hbar\left\langle\frac{\partial V}{\partial x}\right\rangle=-\left\langle\frac{\partial V}{\partial x}\right\rangle$$
`\end{proof}`

## Uncertainty

> [!def] Uncertainty
> Let there be an observable represented by $\hat{A}$. The measurement uncertainty $\Delta {\hat{A}}$ the **root-mean-square deviation** in the [[#^expectation-value]] $\langle {\hat{A}} \rangle$.
>
> $$\Delta {\hat{A}}\equiv \sqrt{\langle {\hat{A}}^{2} \rangle -\langle {\hat{A}} \rangle ^{2}}$$
>

^uncertainty

### Position-Momentum

> [!cor] Position-Momentum Uncertainty Principle
> Applying the [[6.2 - Uncertainty|Uncertainty Principle]] with the [[#^canonical-commutation-relation]] $[\hat{x},\hat{p}]=i\hbar$ gives a lower bound coupling [[6.4 - Position and Momentum#^xuncertainty|Position Uncertainty]] $\Delta\hat{x}$ and [[6.4 - Position and Momentum#^puncertainty|Momentum Uncertainty]] $\Delta\hat{p}$:
> $$\Delta\hat{x}\,\Delta\hat{p}\geq\frac{\hbar}{2}$$

^position-momentum-uncertainty-principle

#### Canonical Commutator

> [!thm] Canonical Commutation Relation
> The [[6.4 - Position and Momentum#^f3f4cf|Position]] and [[6.4 - Position and Momentum#^aace22|Momentum]] observables satisfy:
> $$[\hat{x},\hat{p}]=i\hbar$$

^canonical-commutation-relation

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^canonical-commutation-relation]]
Apply the commutator to a test function $\psi$:

$$[\hat{x},\hat{p}]\psi=x\!\left(-i\hbar\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x}\right)-\left(-i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\right)(x\psi)=-i\hbar x\psi'+i\hbar(\psi+x\psi')=i\hbar\psi$$
`\end{proof}`

### Energy-Momentum

> [!thm] Momentum-Energy Commutator
> For a particle in potential $V(x)$, the [[2.8 - Linear Operators#^commutator-2|Commutator]] of the [[6.3 - Energy#^hamiltonian|Hamiltonian]] and [[6.4 - Position and Momentum#^aace22|Momentum]] is:
> $$[\hat{H},\hat{p}]=i\hbar\frac{\partial V}{\partial x}$$

^momentum-hamiltonian-commutator

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^momentum-hamiltonian-commutator]]
The kinetic term $\hat{p}^2/2m$ commutes with $\hat{p}$ since $[\hat{p}^2,\hat{p}]=0$, so only the potential contributes:
$$[\hat{H},\hat{p}]=[V(x),\hat{p}]$$
Applying to a test function $\psi$:
$$[V(x),-i\hbar\partial_x]\psi=V(-i\hbar\psi')-(-i\hbar)(V\psi)'=-i\hbar V\psi'+i\hbar(V'\psi+V\psi')=i\hbar V'\psi$$
So $[\hat{H},\hat{p}]=i\hbar\frac{\partial V}{\partial x}$.
`\end{proof}`

> [!cor] Energy and Momentum Share Eigenfunctions
> For a [[6.5 - Free Particles#^free-matter-wave|Free Matter Wave]], the [[#^momentum-hamiltonian-commutator]] is $\frac{\partial V}{\partial x}=0$. Therefore $\hat{H}$ and $\hat{p}$ [[6.0 - Postulates#^commuting-operators-share-eigenstates|share Eigenstates]].
>
> The [[6.4 - Position and Momentum#^momentum-basis|Momentum Eigenfunctions]] $e^{ipx/\hbar}$ are simultaneously [[6.3 - Energy#^energy-basis|Energy Eigenfunctions]] with $E=p^{2}/2m$.

^energy-and-momentum-share-eigenfunctions
