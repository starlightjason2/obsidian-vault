# 6.3 - Energy

> [!def] Hamiltonian
> An operator $\hat{H}$ representing the energy observable of a quantum system, satisfying the [[6.0 - Postulates#^473d03]].

^362b2b

> [!def] Kinetic Energy Observable
> The [[1.5 - Work and Energy#^95e31f]] of a quantum particle is a [[6.0 - Postulates#^7e5b0a]] defined with the [[6.4 - Position and Momentum#^aace22]] $\hat{p}$.
> $$\hat{T}=\frac{\hat{p}^{2}}{2m}=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m} \frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}$$

^602718

## Time-independent Hamiltonian

> [!def] Time Invariant Hamiltonian
> The time-invariant [[#^362b2b|Hamiltonian]] combines the [[#^602718|Kinetic Energy Observable]] and a potential $V(x)$.
>
> $$\hat{H}=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m} \frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}} +V(x)$$
>

^4c053c

> [!thm] Time-Invariant Schrödinger Equation
> Let the [[#^4c053c]] ${\hat{H}}$ have $n$th energy eigenvalue $E_{n}$ and eigenstate $\Ket{E_{n}}$. The following steps give the complete time evolution of any state $\psi(x,t)$ in the [[6.4 - Position and Momentum#^764904]].
>
> 1. Write the initial state in the energy eigenbasis, where $c_{n}= \braket{E_{n} | \psi(t=0)}$. $$\ket{\psi (x, t=0)}=\sum c_{n}\ket{E_{n}}$$
> 2. Solve the energy eigenvalue equation $\hat{H}\Ket{E_{n}}=E_{n}\Ket{E_{n}}$ for the eigenstates $\Ket{E_{n}}$ and eigenvalues $E_{n}$. In the position basis: $$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{d^{2}\phi_{n}}{dx^{2}}+V(x)\,\phi_n(x)=E_{n}\,\phi_n(x)$$
> 3. The final time dependent wavefunction is:
> $$\ket{\psi(t)} =\sum_{n}{c_{n}\,e^{-iE_{n}t/\hbar}}\,\ket{E_{n}} $$

^ecd6eb

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^ecd6eb]]
Apply [[4.3.0 - Linear PDEs#^fb39dc]] to the [[#^597a72]]. Assume Ansatz $\psi(x,t) = X(x)\,T(t)$.

$$
-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{\partial^2\psi}{\partial x^2}+V(x)\,\psi(x,t)= i\hbar\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}
$$

^d5b508

So $\frac{\partial^2\psi}{\partial x^2} = T(t)\,X''(x)$ and $\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t} = \dot{T}(t)\,X(x)$. Substituting into [[#^d5b508]],

$$
-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}X''(x)\,{T(t)} +V(x)\,X(x)\,T(t)= i\hbar\,{\dot{T}(t)}\,{X(x)}
$$
^a74864

Dividing both sides of [[#^a74864]] by $X(x)\,T(t)$ produces two decoupled ODEs with constant energy eigenvalue $E$ ([[#^586f4f]]).

$$
-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m} \frac{X''(x)}{X(x)} +V(x)= i\hbar\,\frac{\dot{T}(t)}{T(t)}=E
$$ 
^586f4f

The first equation is: $(-\hbar^{2} /2m)\,X''(x)+V(x)\,X(x)=E\,X(x)$, which is just the energy eigenvalue equation where $X(x)$ is the energy eigenfunction.

The second equation is: $i\hbar\,{\dot{T}(t)}=E_{n}\,T(t)$. Note $1/i=-i$, so:

$$ \begin{align}

 \frac{d\psi_{n}}{dt}+\left( \frac{iE_{n}}{\hbar} \right)\,\psi_{n}(t)&=0
\end{align}$$

^76f296

See [[4.1.1 - First Order Linear ODEs#^00894f]] to solve [[#^76f296]] where $p(t)=iE_{n} / \hbar$.

$$
\psi_{n}(t)=c_{n}\,e^{-iE_{n}t/\hbar}
$$
`\end{proof}`

> [!def] Energy Basis
> The eigenbasis of the [[#^362b2b|Hamiltonian]] consists of energy eigenstates $\ket{E_n}$ satisfying $\hat{H}\ket{E_n}=E_n\ket{E_n}$. The basis is complete $\sum_n\ket{E_n}\bra{E_n}=\hat{I}$ and orthonormal $\braket{E_m|E_n}=\delta_{mn}$.
> 
> In the [[6.4 - Position and Momentum#^764904|Position Basis]], the eigenfunction is $\phi_n(x)=\braket{x|E_n}$.
>
