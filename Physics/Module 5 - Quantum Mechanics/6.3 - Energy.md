# 6.3 - Energy

> [!def] Hamiltonian
> The **Hamiltonian** $\hat{H}$ is the [[6.0 - Postulates#^observable-postulate|Observable]] representing total energy of a quantum system.

^hamiltonian

> [!def] Kinetic Energy Observable
> The [[1.5 - Work and Energy#^kinetic-energy]] of a quantum particle is a [[6.0 - Postulates#^continuous-observable]] defined with the [[6.4 - Position and Momentum#^aace22]] $\hat{p}$.
> $$\hat{T}=\frac{\hat{p}^{2}}{2m}=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m} \frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}$$

^kinetic-energy-observable

## Time-independent Hamiltonian

> [!def] Time Invariant Hamiltonian
> The time-invariant [[#^hamiltonian|Hamiltonian]] combines the [[#^kinetic-energy-observable|Kinetic Energy Observable]] and a potential $V(x)$.
>
> $$\hat{H}=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m} \frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x^{2}} +V(x)$$
>

^time-invariant-hamiltonian

> [!thm] Time-Invariant Schrödinger Equation
> Let the [[#^time-invariant-hamiltonian]] ${\hat{H}}$ have $n$th energy eigenvalue $E_{n}$ and eigenstate $\Ket{E_{n}}$. The following steps give the complete time evolution of any state $\psi(x,t)$ in the [[6.4 - Position and Momentum#^position-basis]].
>
> 1. Write the initial state in the energy eigenbasis, where $c_{n}= \braket{E_{n} | \psi(t=0)}$.
> $$\ket{\psi (x, t=0)}=\sum c_{n}\ket{E_{n}}$$
> 2. Solve the energy eigenvalue equation $\hat{H}\Ket{E_{n}}=E_{n}\Ket{E_{n}}$ for the eigenstates $\Ket{E_{n}}$ and eigenvalues $E_{n}$. Use
> $$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{d^{2}\phi_{n}}{dx^{2}}+V(x)\,\phi_n(x)=E_{n}\,\phi_n(x)$$
> 3. The final time dependent wavefunction is:
> $$\ket{\psi(t)} =\sum_{n}{c_{n}\,e^{-iE_{n}t/\hbar}}\,\ket{E_{n}} $$

^time-invariant-schrdinger-equation

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^time-invariant-schrdinger-equation]]
Apply [[4.3.0 - Linear PDEs#^fb39dc]] to the [[6.0 - Postulates#^597a72]]. Assume Ansatz $\psi(x,t) = X(x)\,T(t)$.

$$
-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{\partial^2\psi}{\partial x^2}+V(x)\,\psi(x,t)= i\hbar\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}
$$

^d5b508

So $\frac{\partial^2\psi}{\partial x^2} = T(t)\,X''(x)$ and $\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t} = \dot{T}(t)\,X(x)$. Substituting into [[#^d5b508]],

$$
-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}X''(x)\,{T(t)} +V(x)\,X(x)\,T(t)= i\hbar\,{\dot{T}(t)}\,{X(x)}
$$
^a74864

Dividing both sides of [[#^a74864]] by $X(x)\,T(t)$ produces two decoupled ODEs with constant energy eigenvalue $E$ ([[#^586f4f]]).

$$
-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m} \frac{X''(x)}{X(x)} +V(x)= i\hbar\,\frac{\dot{T}(t)}{T(t)}=E
$$ 
^586f4f

The first equation is: $(-\hbar^{2} /2m)\,X''(x)+V(x)\,X(x)=E\,X(x)$, which is just the energy eigenvalue equation where $X(x)$ is the energy eigenfunction.

The second equation is: $i\hbar\,{\dot{T}(t)}=E_{n}\,T(t)$. Note $1/i=-i$, so:

$$ \begin{align}

 \frac{d\psi_{n}}{dt}+\left( \frac{iE_{n}}{\hbar} \right)\,\psi_{n}(t)&=0
\end{align}$$

^76f296

Solving [[#^76f296]] as a [[4.1.1 - First Order Linear ODEs#^homogeneous-solution-of-a-first-order-linear-ode|Homogeneous First Order Linear ODE]] with $p(t)=iE_{n} / \hbar$ gives:

$$
\psi_{n}(t)=c_{n}\,e^{-iE_{n}t/\hbar}
$$
`\end{proof}`

> [!def] Energy Basis
> The eigenbasis of the [[#^time-invariant-hamiltonian]] $\hat{H}$ consists of energy eigenstates $\ket{E_n}$ that diagonalise $\hat{H}$. Any state can be written as a sum over these eigenstates, with each component evolving with the [[#^time-invariant-schrdinger-equation]] $e^{-iE_{n}t/\hbar}$.
>
> The basis is complete and orthonormal:
> 
> $$\sum_n\ket{E_n}\bra{E_n}=\hat{I},\qquad \braket{E_m|E_n}=\delta_{mn}$$
>
> In the [[6.4 - Position and Momentum#^position-basis|Position Basis]], the eigenfunction is $\phi_n(x)=\braket{x|E_n}$.

^energy-basis

### Matrix Elements

> [!thm] Hamiltonian Matrix Element
> The [[6.0 - Postulates#^discrete-observable|matrix]] element $\bra{\psi_{1}}\hat{H}\ket{\psi_{2}}$ is the complex coupling strength with which the [[#^hamiltonian|Hamiltonian]] connects two states. 
> 
> In the [[2.8 - Linear Operators#^matrix-representation-of-a-linear-operator|Matrix Representation]] of $\hat{H}$, off-diagonal entries measure the failure of the chosen basis to diagonalize $\hat{H}$, vanishing if and only if the basis is the [[#^energy-basis|Energy Basis]]:
> $$H_{ij}=\bra{e_{i}}\hat{H}\ket{e_{j}},\qquad H_{ij}=E_{i}\delta_{ij}\iff\{\ket{e_{n}}\}=\{\ket{E_{n}}\}$$

^hamiltonian-matrix-element

### Symmetric Potentials

> [!thm] Symmetric Potentials Share Parity Eigenstates
> If the potential $V(x)=V(-x)$ is symmetric, the [[#^time-invariant-hamiltonian|Time Invariant Hamiltonian]] commutes with the [[2.8 - Linear Operators#^parity-operator|Parity Operator]] $\hat{P}$. 
> 
> Because [[6.0 - Postulates#^commuting-operators-share-eigenstates|Commuting Operators share Eigenstates]], every energy eigenstate can be chosen as a parity eigenstate, splitting the spectrum into **even** ($\hat{P}\psi=+\psi$) and **odd** ($\hat{P}\psi=-\psi$) bands.
> $$V(-x)=V(x)\implies[\hat{H},\hat{P}]=0\implies\hat{H}\psi=E\psi\text{ with }\hat{P}\psi=\pm\psi$$

^symmetric-potential-parity
