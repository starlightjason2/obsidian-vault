# 6.4 - Position and Momentum

> [!axm] Wave Particle Duality
> A quantum particle behaves like a wave, with momentum $p$ quantized by the de Broglie wavelength $k$ and energy $E$ quantized by angular frequency $\omega$.
> 1. $$p=\hbar k$$
> 2. $$E=\hbar \omega$$
>

^aa67dd


> [!thm] Position–Momentum Duality
> The [[3.8 - Fourier Transform#^24e1d5|Fourier Transform]] performs a change of basis between the [[#^764904|Position Basis]] and the [[#^redkvt|Momentum Basis]] with the [[2.7 - Function Spaces#^3e433d]].
>
> $$\braket{x|p}=\frac{e^{ipx/\hbar}}{\sqrt{2\pi\hbar}},\qquad\braket{p|x}=\frac{e^{-ipx/\hbar}}{\sqrt{2\pi\hbar}}$$
>
> $$\psi(x)=\braket{x|\psi}=\int\braket{x|p}\braket{p|\psi}\,dp=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\hbar}}\int e^{ipx/\hbar}\,\tilde{\psi}(p)\,dp\quad\text{(inverse FT)}$$
>
>
>
> $$\tilde{\psi}(p)=\braket{p|\psi}=\int\braket{p|x}\braket{x|\psi}\,dx=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\hbar}}\int e^{-ipx/\hbar}\,\psi(x)\,dx\quad\text{(FT)}$$
>
>

^pos-mom-duality

## Position Space

> [!def] Position Observable
> Position is a [[6.0 - Postulates#^7e5b0a]] represented with the operator $\hat{x}$. In the [[#^764904|Position]] and [[#^redkvt|Momentum]] bases, $\hat{x}$ is expressed as:
>
> $$\begin{align}
\hat{x}&\equiv x \tag{Position basis}\\
\hat{x}&\equiv i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial p}\tag{Momentum basis}
\end{align}$$
> The measured position eigenvalues $x$ obey the eigenvalue equation:
>
> $$\hat{x}\,\psi(x)=x\,\psi(x)$$

^f3f4cf

> [!def] Position Basis
> The eigenbasis of the [[#^f3f4cf|Position Observable]] consists of position eigenstates $\ket{x}$ satisfying $\hat{x}\ket{x}=x\ket{x}$.
> 1. $$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\ket{x}\bra{x}\,dx=\hat{I}$$
> 2. $$\braket{x_1|x_2}=\delta(x_1-x_2)$$

^764904

### Eigenstates

> [!def] Position Eigenstate
> The position eigenstate $\ket{x_0}$ expressed in the [[#^764904|Position Basis]] and [[#^redkvt|Momentum Basis]]:
> $$\begin{align}
\braket{x|x_{0}}&=\delta(x-x_{0}) \tag{Position basis}\\[0.5em]
\braket{p|x_{0}}&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\hbar}}\,e^{-ix_{0}p/\hbar}\tag{Momentum basis}
\end{align}$$

^xeigen

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^xeigen]]
The position eigenvalue equation in position space is
$$x\,\braket{x|x_0}=x_0\,\braket{x|x_0}$$
so $(x-x_0)\braket{x|x_0}=0$, which is satisfied only by $\braket{x|x_0}=\delta(x-x_0)$.

For the momentum basis representation, insert a complete set of position states using the [[#^764904|Position Basis]] completeness relation and apply the [[6.4 - Position and Momentum#^pos-mom-duality|Position-Momentum Duality]]:
$$\braket{p|x_0}=\int\braket{p|x}\braket{x|x_0}\,dx=\int\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\hbar}}\,e^{-ipx/\hbar}\,\delta(x-x_0)\,dx=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\hbar}}\,e^{-ipx_0/\hbar}$$
`\end{proof}`

> [!def] Position Expectation Value
> The [[6.2 - Uncertainty#^6591bb|Expectation Value]] of [[#^f3f4cf|Position]] is complementary in the two bases:
>
> $$\begin{align}
\langle\hat{x}\rangle &= \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} x\,|\psi(x)|^2\,dx \tag{Position basis} \\
\langle\hat{x}\rangle &= \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \tilde{\psi}^*(p)\,i\hbar\frac{\partial\tilde{\psi}}{\partial p}\,dp \tag{Momentum basis}
\end{align}$$

^xexpval

## Momentum Space

> [!def] Momentum Observable
> Momentum is a [[6.0 - Postulates#^7e5b0a]] represented with the operator $\hat{p}$. In the [[#^764904 |Position]] and [[#^redkvt]], $\hat{p}$ is expressed as:
>
> $$\begin{align}
\hat{p}&\equiv p \tag{Momentum basis}\\
\hat{p}&\equiv-i\hbar \frac{d}{dx}\tag{Position basis}
\end{align}$$
>
> The measured momentum eigenvalues $p=\hbar k$ obey [[#^aa67dd]], with the eigenvalue equation:
>
> $$\hat{p}\,\psi(x)=\hbar k\,\psi(x)$$
>

^aace22

> [!def] Momentum Basis
> The eigenbasis of the [[#^aace22|Momentum Observable]] consists of momentum eigenstates $\ket{p}$ satisfying $\hat{p}\ket{p}=p\ket{p}$.
> 1. $$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\ket{p}\bra{p}\,dp=\hat{I}$$
> 
> 2. $$\braket{p_1|p_2}=\delta(p_1-p_2)$$

^redkvt

### Eigenstates

> [!def] Momentum Eigenstate
> The momentum eigenstate $\ket{p_0}$ expressed in the [[#^764904|Position Basis]] and [[#^redkvt|Momentum Basis]]:
> $$\begin{align}
\braket{x|p_{0}}&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\hbar}}\,e^{ip_{0}x/\hbar} \tag{Position basis}\\[0.5em]
\braket{p|p_{0}}&=\delta(p-p_{0})\tag{Momentum basis}
\end{align}$$

^peigen

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^peigen]]
The momentum eigenvalue equation in position space is
$$-i\hbar\,\frac{\partial\psi_p}{\partial x}=p\,\psi_p$$
Use the [[4.1.1 - First Order Linear ODEs#^00894f]]: $\psi_p(x)=Ce^{ipx/\hbar}$. The normalization constant $C=1/\sqrt{2\pi\hbar}$ follows $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left\lvert{\psi_{p}}\right\rvert^{2}\,dx=1$.

Furthermore, with the completeness relation and [[3.8 - Fourier Transform#^32f6cb]], we see:
$$\int\braket{x_1|p}\braket{p|x_2}\,dp=\delta(x_1-x_2)$$

`\end{proof}`

> [!def] Momentum Expectation Value
> The [[6.2 - Uncertainty#^6591bb|Expectation Value]] of [[#^aace22|Momentum]] is complementary in the two bases:
>
> $$\begin{align}
\langle\hat{p}\rangle &= \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} p\,|\tilde{\psi}(p)|^2\,dp \tag{Momentum basis} \\
\langle\hat{p}\rangle &= \int_{-\infty}^{\infty} \psi^*(x)\left(-i\hbar\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x}\right)dx \tag{Position basis}
\end{align}$$

^pexpval

### Commutation with Hamiltonian

> [!thm] Momentum-Hamiltonian Commutator
> For a particle in potential $V(x)$, the [[2.7 - Function Spaces#^cb348c|Commutator]] of the [[6.3 - Energy#^362b2b|Hamiltonian]] and [[#^aace22|Momentum]] is:
> $$[\hat{H},\hat{p}]=i\hbar\frac{\partial V}{\partial x}$$
>

^hp-comm

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^hp-comm]]
The kinetic term $\hat{p}^2/2m$ commutes with $\hat{p}$ since $[\hat{p}^2,\hat{p}]=0$, so only the potential contributes:
$$[\hat{H},\hat{p}]=[V(x),\hat{p}]$$
Applying to a test function $\psi$:
$$[V(x),-i\hbar\partial_x]\psi=V(-i\hbar\psi')-(-i\hbar)(V\psi)'=-i\hbar V\psi'+i\hbar(V'\psi+V\psi')=i\hbar V'\psi$$
So $[\hat{H},\hat{p}]=i\hbar\frac{\partial V}{\partial x}$.
`\end{proof}`

> [!cor] Energy and Momentum Share Eigenfunctions
> For a [[#^freewave|Free Matter Wave]], the [[#^hp-comm]] is $\frac{\partial V}{\partial x}=0$. Therefore $\hat{H}$ and $\hat{p}$ [[6.0 - Postulates#^848264|Share Eigenstates]].
>
> The [[#^redkvt|Momentum Eigenfunctions]] $e^{ipx/\hbar}$ are simultaneously [[6.3 - Energy#^energy-basis|Energy Eigenfunctions]] with $E=\frac{p^2}{2m}$.

^hp-comm-cor

### Commutation with Position

> [!thm] Canonical Commutation Relation
> The [[#^f3f4cf|Position]] and [[#^aace22|Momentum]] observables satisfy:
> $$[\hat{x},\hat{p}]=i\hbar$$

^xp-comm

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^xp-comm]]
Apply the commutator to a test function $\psi$:

$$[\hat{x},\hat{p}]\psi=x\!\left(-i\hbar\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x}\right)-\left(-i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\right)(x\psi)=-i\hbar x\psi'+i\hbar(\psi+x\psi')=i\hbar\psi$$
`\end{proof}`

## Uncertainty

> [!cor] Position-Momentum Uncertainty Principle
> Applying the [[6.2 - Uncertainty|Uncertainty Principle]] with the [[6.4 - Position and Momentum#^xp-comm]] $[\hat{x},\hat{p}]=i\hbar$ gives:
> $$\Delta x\,\Delta p\geq\frac{\hbar}{2}$$
> Position and momentum cannot both be sharply defined simultaneously.

^xp-uncertainty
