# 6.5 - Potential Wells

> [!def] Potential Well
> A region of attractive potential $V(x)$ that confines a particle to bound [[6.0 - Postulates#^eigenstate-postulate|Eigenstates]] of the [[6.3 - Energy#^hamiltonian|Hamiltonian]] via the [[6.3 - Energy#^time-invariant-schrdinger-equation|Time-Invariant Schrödinger Equation]].
>
> Eigenstates are constructed piecewise on each region of constant $V$, then joined by [[4.0 - ODEs#^boundary-value-problem|Boundary Conditions]] at region interfaces (continuity of $\psi$ and $\psi'$, decay at $\pm\infty$, derivative jumps at delta spikes).
> $$-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\psi''(x)+V(x)\,\psi(x)=E\,\psi(x)$$
>
> Each well is solved in four steps: write the potential $V(x)$, write the general solution in each region, list the boundary conditions, and apply them to fix the constants and quantize $E$.

^potential-well

## Infinite Square Well

> [!exm] Infinite Square Well
> Particle in a [[#^potential-well|Potential Well]] with infinite walls at $x=0,L$.
>
> The potential is zero inside the box and infinite outside:
> $$V(x)=\begin{cases}0 & 0\leq x\leq L \\ \infty & \text{otherwise}\end{cases}$$
>
> Inside the well, $\psi''+k^{2}\psi=0$ with $k^{2}=2mE/\hbar^{2}$ is a [[4.1 - Simple Harmonic Oscillation#^d9cde8|Simple Harmonic ODE]] with general solution:
> $$\psi(x)=A\cos(kx)+B\sin(kx)$$
>
> The infinite walls impose [[4.0 - ODEs#^dirichlet-boundary-conditions|Dirichlet Boundary Conditions]]:
> 1. $$\psi(0)=0$$
> 2. $$\psi(L)=0$$
> 
> Applying the conditions and normalizing on $[0,L]$ gives the exact solution:
> $$\psi_{n}(x,t)=\sqrt{\frac{2}{L}}\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)e^{-iE_{n}t/\hbar},\qquad E_{n}=\frac{n^{2}\pi^{2}\hbar^{2}}{2mL^{2}}$$

^2411f8

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^2411f8]]
Applying BC 1, $\psi(0)=A\cos(0)+B\sin(0)=A=0$, so $\psi(x)=B\sin(kx)$.

Applying BC 2, $\psi(L)=B\sin(kL)=0$. The trivial $B=0$ is excluded, so $\sin(kL)=0$ requires $kL=n\pi$ for $n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}$.

Solving $k=n\pi/L=\sqrt{2mE_{n}}/\hbar$ for energy:

$$E_{n}=\frac{k^{2}\hbar^{2}}{2m}=\frac{n^{2}\pi^{2}\hbar^{2}}{2mL^{2}}$$

Normalizing on $[0,L]$, $\int_{0}^{L}|B\sin(n\pi x/L)|^{2}dx=B^{2}L/2=1$ gives $B=\sqrt{2/L}$.
`\end{proof}`

> [!cor] Momentum-Space Wavefunction of Infinite Square Well
> The Fourier transform of the $n$th [[#^2411f8|Infinite Square Well]] energy eigenstate via [[6.4 - Position and Momentum#^position-momentum-duality|Position-Momentum Duality]] is peaked at the two counter-propagating plane waves $p=\pm p_{n}=\pm n\pi\hbar/L$ that compose $\sin(n\pi x/L)$.
> $$\tilde{\psi}_{n}(p)=\sqrt{\frac{p_{n}}{\pi L}}\,\frac{p_{n}\!\left(1-(-1)^{n}e^{-ipL/\hbar}\right)}{p_{n}^{2}-p^{2}}\begin{cases}\cos(pL/2\hbar) & n\text{ odd} \\ i\sin(pL/2\hbar) & n\text{ even}\end{cases}$$

^infinite-well-momentum-wavefunction

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^infinite-well-momentum-wavefunction]]
With $k=p/\hbar$ and $k_{n}=n\pi/L$, the Fourier transform of $\psi_{n}(x,0)=\sqrt{2/L}\sin(k_{n}x)$ on $[0,L]$ is:

$$\tilde{\psi}_{n}(p)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\hbar}}\int_{0}^{L}\sqrt{\frac{2}{L}}\sin(k_{n}x)e^{-ikx}dx=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi\hbar L}}\int_{0}^{L}\frac{e^{i(k_{n}-k)x}-e^{-i(k_{n}+k)x}}{2i}dx$$

Each piece evaluates as:

$$\int_{0}^{L}e^{i(k'\mp k)x}dx=\frac{1-e^{i(k'\mp k)L}}{-i(k'\mp k)}$$

Using $k_{n}L=n\pi$ so $e^{\pm ik_{n}L}=(-1)^{n}$ extracts the common factor $(1-(-1)^{n}e^{-ikL})$ and the result follows.
`\end{proof}`

### Ground State

> [!cor] Ground State Position Uncertainty
> The $n=1$ ground state of the [[#^2411f8|Infinite Square Well]] is centered on the well with [[6.4 - Position and Momentum#^xexpval]] $L/2$ and [[6.2 - Uncertainty#^uncertainty]]:
> $$\langle\hat{x}\rangle=\frac{L}{2},\qquad \langle\hat{x}^{2}\rangle=\frac{L^{2}}{6}\!\left(2-\frac{3}{\pi^{2}}\right),\qquad \Delta\hat{x}=L\sqrt{\frac{1}{12}-\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}}\approx 0.18\,L$$

^infinite-well-ground-state-position-uncertainty

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^infinite-well-ground-state-position-uncertainty]]
The ground state $\psi_{1}(x)=\sqrt{2/L}\sin(\pi x/L)$ is symmetric about $x=L/2$, so by inspection $\langle\hat{x}\rangle=L/2$. For the second moment,

$$\langle\hat{x}^{2}\rangle=\frac{2}{L}\int_{0}^{L}x^{2}\sin^{2}\!\left(\frac{\pi x}{L}\right)dx=\frac{2}{L}\int_{0}^{L}x^{2}\,\frac{1-\cos(2\pi x/L)}{2}\,dx$$

The first piece gives $L^{2}/3$. Integrating $x^{2}\cos(2\pi x/L)$ by parts twice and using $\sin(2\pi)=0$, $\cos(2\pi)=1$ leaves $L^{3}/(2\pi^{2})$. Combining,

$$\langle\hat{x}^{2}\rangle=\frac{L^{2}}{3}-\frac{L^{2}}{2\pi^{2}}=\frac{L^{2}}{6}\!\left(2-\frac{3}{\pi^{2}}\right)$$

Subtracting $\langle\hat{x}\rangle^{2}=L^{2}/4$ gives $(\Delta\hat{x})^{2}=L^{2}\big(1/12-1/(2\pi^{2})\big)\approx 0.0327\,L^{2}$.
`\end{proof}`

> [!cor] Ground State Momentum Uncertainty
> The ground state of the [[#^2411f8|Infinite Square Well]] has zero mean momentum and momentum [[6.2 - Uncertainty#^uncertainty]] set by the energy of the eigenstate.
> $$\langle\hat{p}\rangle=0,\qquad \langle\hat{p}^{2}\rangle=\frac{\pi^{2}\hbar^{2}}{L^{2}}=2mE_{1},\qquad \Delta\hat{p}=\frac{\pi\hbar}{L}$$

^infinite-well-ground-state-momentum-uncertainty

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^infinite-well-ground-state-momentum-uncertainty]]
In the [[6.4 - Position and Momentum#^position-basis|Position Basis]], the [[6.4 - Position and Momentum#^aace22|Momentum Operator]] gives

$$\langle\hat{p}\rangle=-i\hbar\int_{0}^{L}\psi_{1}^{*}\,\psi_{1}'\,dx=-\frac{i\hbar\pi}{L^{2}}\int_{0}^{L}\sin\!\left(\frac{2\pi x}{L}\right)dx=0$$

The integrand completes a full period over $[0,L]$, vanishing identically. The ground state is an [[6.3 - Energy#^time-invariant-schrdinger-equation|Energy Eigenstate]] of the free-particle Hamiltonian inside the well, so $\hat{p}^{2}\psi_{1}=2mE_{1}\psi_{1}$ and therefore

$$\langle\hat{p}^{2}\rangle=2mE_{1}=2m\cdot \frac{\pi^{2}\hbar^{2}}{2mL^{2}}=\frac{\pi^{2}\hbar^{2}}{L^{2}}$$

Taking the square root gives $\Delta\hat{p}=\pi\hbar/L$.
`\end{proof}`

> [!cor] Ground State Saturates Heisenberg
> Combining the ground state [[#^infinite-well-ground-state-position-uncertainty|Position]] and [[#^infinite-well-ground-state-momentum-uncertainty|Momentum]] uncertainties, the ground state of the [[#^2411f8|Infinite Square Well]] satisfies the [[6.2 - Uncertainty|Uncertainty Principle]] with room to spare.
> $$\Delta\hat{x}\,\Delta\hat{p}=\pi\hbar\sqrt{\frac{1}{12}-\frac{1}{2\pi^{2}}}\approx 0.568\,\hbar>\frac{\hbar}{2}$$

^ground-state-saturates-heisenberg

## Finite Square Well

> [!exm] Finite Square Well
> Particle in a symmetric [[#^potential-well|Potential Well]] of depth $V_{0}$ and half-width $a$.
>
> The potential is constant in three regions:
> $$V(x)=\begin{cases}0 & |x|>a \\ -V_{0} & |x|\leq a\end{cases}$$
>
> For bound states $E<0$, define $\gamma=\sqrt{-2mE}/\hbar$ and $k=\sqrt{2m(E+V_{0})}/\hbar$. Outside the well the eigenvalue equation is a [[4.1.3 - Constant Coefficient ODEs#^382f29|Constant Coefficient ODE]] with real roots; inside it is a [[4.1 - Simple Harmonic Oscillation#^d9cde8|Simple Harmonic ODE]]. The piecewise general solution is:
> $$\psi(x)=\begin{cases}Ce^{\gamma x}+C'e^{-\gamma x} & x<-a \\ B\cos(kx)+A\sin(kx) & |x|\leq a \\ De^{-\gamma x}+D'e^{\gamma x} & x>a\end{cases}$$
>
> The boundary conditions are:
> 1. $$\psi(\pm\infty)=0\implies C'=D'=0$$
> 2. $$\psi(\pm a^{-})=\psi(\pm a^{+})$$
> 3. $$\psi'(\pm a^{-})=\psi'(\pm a^{+})$$
> 4. $$V(-x)=V(x)\implies A=0\text{ or }B=0$$
> 
> Applying conditions 2 and 3 simultaneously through the logarithmic derivative $\psi'/\psi$ at $x=a$ yields transcendental equations:
> $$\frac{\gamma}{k}=\tan(ka)\text{ (even)},\qquad\frac{\gamma}{k}=-\cot(ka)\text{ (odd)},\qquad \gamma^{2}+k^{2}=\frac{2mV_{0}}{\hbar^{2}}$$
>
> Each circle of radius $r=\sqrt{2mV_{0}}/\hbar$ in the $(k,\gamma)$ plane intersects the tangent and cotangent branches at the bound state energies. Even solutions exist for any depth; odd solutions require $r>\pi/(2a)$.

^finite-square-well

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^finite-square-well]]
Applying BC 1, normalizability requires the growing exponentials in the outer regions to vanish: $C'=0$ for $x<-a$ and $D'=0$ for $x>a$.

Applying BC 4, by [[6.3 - Energy#^symmetric-potential-parity|Symmetric Potentials Share Parity Eigenstates]] the eigenstates split into even ($\psi_{\text{in}}=B\cos(kx)$, $A=0$) and odd ($\psi_{\text{in}}=A\sin(kx)$, $B=0$) cases, with the symmetry $C=\pm D$ linking the outer coefficients.

Applying BCs 2 and 3 simultaneously: matching $\psi'/\psi$ at $x=a$ enforces both continuity conditions in one equation. For even states,

$$\frac{\psi'}{\psi}\bigg|_{a^{-}}=\frac{-Bk\sin(ka)}{B\cos(ka)}=-k\tan(ka),\qquad\frac{\psi'}{\psi}\bigg|_{a^{+}}=\frac{-D\gamma e^{-\gamma a}}{De^{-\gamma a}}=-\gamma$$

Equating gives $-k\tan(ka)=-\gamma$, so $\gamma/k=\tan(ka)$. Repeating for odd states yields $k\cot(ka)=-\gamma$, so $\gamma/k=-\cot(ka)$.

Adding the definitions of $\gamma^{2}$ and $k^{2}$ cancels $E$:

$$\gamma^{2}+k^{2}=\frac{-2mE}{\hbar^{2}}+\frac{2m(E+V_{0})}{\hbar^{2}}=\frac{2mV_{0}}{\hbar^{2}}$$

This is a circle of radius $r=\sqrt{2mV_{0}}/\hbar$ in the $(k,\gamma)$ plane. Bound states are intersections with $\gamma/k=\tan(ka)$ or $\gamma/k=-\cot(ka)$. The first $\tan$ branch passes through the origin, so an even bound state exists for any $r>0$; the first $-\cot$ branch starts at $ka=\pi/2$, so odd bound states require $r>\pi/(2a)$.
`\end{proof}`

## Delta Well

> [!exm] Delta Well
> Particle in an attractive [[3.5 - Step and Delta#^delta-function|Dirac Delta]] [[#^potential-well|Potential Well]] of strength $\gamma>0$.
>
> The potential is a single inward spike at the origin:
> $$V(x)=-\gamma\,\delta(x),\qquad \gamma>0$$
>
> Away from the origin $V=0$, so for bound states $E<0$ define $q=\sqrt{-2mE}/\hbar$ and solve $\psi''-q^{2}\psi=0$ on each side:
> $$\psi(x)=\begin{cases}Ae^{qx}+A'e^{-qx} & x<0 \\ Be^{qx}+B'e^{-qx} & x>0\end{cases}$$
>
> The boundary conditions are:
> 1. $$\psi(\pm\infty)=0\implies A'=0,\ B=0$$
> 2. $$\psi(0^{-})=\psi(0^{+})$$
> 3. $$\left.\frac{d\psi}{dx}\right|_{0^{+}}-\left.\frac{d\psi}{dx}\right|_{0^{-}}=-\frac{2m\gamma}{\hbar^{2}}\psi(0)$$
> 
> Applying the conditions yields a single bound state:
> $$\psi(x)=\sqrt{q_{0}}\,e^{-q_{0}|x|},\qquad q_{0}=\frac{m\gamma}{\hbar^{2}},\qquad E=-\frac{m\gamma^{2}}{2\hbar^{2}}$$

^delta-function-well

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^delta-function-well]]
Applying BC 1, normalizability discards the growing exponentials, leaving $\psi(x)=Ae^{qx}$ for $x<0$ and $\psi(x)=B'e^{-qx}$ for $x>0$.

Applying BC 2, $\psi(0^{-})=A=B'=\psi(0^{+})$, so $\psi(x)=Ae^{-q|x|}$.

Applying BC 3 by integrating $-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\psi''-\gamma\delta(x)\psi=E\psi$ from $-\varepsilon$ to $+\varepsilon$ and taking $\varepsilon\to 0$:

$$-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\big[\psi'(0^{+})-\psi'(0^{-})\big]-\gamma\,\psi(0)=0\implies\psi'(0^{+})-\psi'(0^{-})=-\frac{2m\gamma}{\hbar^{2}}\psi(0)$$

The energy integral on the right vanishes since $\psi$ is bounded and the window shrinks to zero width. The potential integral picks out $\psi(0)$ via the [[3.5 - Step and Delta#^integrating-the-delta-function|Dirac Delta]] sifting property.

Differentiating $\psi=Ae^{-q|x|}$ piecewise gives $\psi'(0^{+})=-qA$ and $\psi'(0^{-})=qA$, so the jump is $-2qA$ and $\psi(0)=A$. Cancelling $A$:

$$-2q=-\frac{2m\gamma}{\hbar^{2}}\implies q_{0}=\frac{m\gamma}{\hbar^{2}}$$

The energy follows from $q_{0}=\sqrt{-2mE}/\hbar$:

$$E=-\frac{\hbar^{2}q_{0}^{2}}{2m}=-\frac{m\gamma^{2}}{2\hbar^{2}}$$

Normalizing, $\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}A^{2}e^{-2q_{0}|x|}dx=A^{2}/q_{0}=1$ gives $A=\sqrt{q_{0}}$.
`\end{proof}`

### Double Delta Well

> [!exm] Double Delta Well
> Particle in a [[#^potential-well|Potential Well]] of two attractive [[3.5 - Step and Delta#^delta-function|Dirac Deltas]] separated by $2a$, modeling the H$_{2}^{+}$ hydrogen molecular ion (electron bound between two protons).
>
> The potential is symmetric:
> $$V(x)=-\gamma\big[\delta(x+a)+\delta(x-a)\big],\qquad\gamma>0$$
>
> Away from the deltas $V=0$, so for bound states $E<0$ define $q=\sqrt{-2mE}/\hbar$ and solve $\psi''-q^{2}\psi=0$ in three regions, discarding the diverging exponentials in the outer regions:
> $$\psi(x)=\begin{cases}Ae^{qx} & x<-a \\ Be^{qx}+Ce^{-qx} & |x|<a \\ De^{-qx} & x>a\end{cases}$$
>
> The boundary conditions are:
> 1. $$\psi(\pm\infty)=0$$
> 2. $$\psi(\pm a^{-})=\psi(\pm a^{+})$$
> 3. $$\left.\frac{d\psi}{dx}\right|_{(\pm a)^{+}}-\left.\frac{d\psi}{dx}\right|_{(\pm a)^{-}}=-\frac{2m\gamma}{\hbar^{2}}\psi(\pm a)$$
> 4. By [[6.3 - Energy#^symmetric-potential-parity|Symmetric Potentials Share Parity Eigenstates]], $\psi$ is even ($A=D$, $B=C$) or odd ($A=-D$, $B=-C$).
> 
> Two bound states emerge, an even **bonding** state and an odd **antibonding** state. The interior collapses to $\cosh$ or $\sinh$ via [[3.7.1 - Sinusoidals#^cosh|Hyperbolic Cosine]] and [[3.7.1 - Sinusoidals#^sinh|Hyperbolic Sine]]:
> $$\psi_{e}(x)=\begin{cases}Ae^{qx} & x<-a \\ 2B\cosh(qx) & |x|<a \\ Ae^{-qx} & x>a\end{cases},\qquad \psi_{o}(x)=\begin{cases}Ae^{qx} & x<-a \\ 2B\sinh(qx) & |x|<a \\ -Ae^{-qx} & x>a\end{cases}$$
>
> The even branch satisfies the transcendental equation $\hbar^{2}q/(m\gamma)=1+e^{-2qa}$.

^double-delta-well

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^double-delta-well]]
Applying BC 1 discards the divergent exponentials so the outer regions have a single decaying coefficient each. Applying BC 4, parity sets $A=\pm D$ and $B=\pm C$, collapsing the interior to $2B\cosh(qx)$ for the even branch and $2B\sinh(qx)$ for the odd.

Applying BC 2 at $x=-a$ for the even branch:

$$Ae^{-qa}=B(e^{qa}+e^{-qa})=2B\cosh(qa)\implies A=\frac{2B\cosh(qa)}{e^{-qa}}$$

Applying BC 3 at $x=-a$ for the even branch, where the interior derivative is $\psi_{e}'(x)=Bq(e^{qx}-e^{-qx})=2Bq\sinh(qx)$ and the outer derivative is $Aq\,e^{qx}$:

$$2Bq\sinh(-qa)-Aq\,e^{-qa}=-\frac{2m\gamma}{\hbar^{2}}\,Ae^{-qa}$$

Substituting $Ae^{-qa}=2B\cosh(qa)$ and $\sinh(-qa)=-\sinh(qa)$:

$$-2Bq\sinh(qa)-2Bq\cosh(qa)=-\frac{4m\gamma}{\hbar^{2}}B\cosh(qa)$$

The left side simplifies via $\sinh(qa)+\cosh(qa)=e^{qa}$:

$$-2Bq\,e^{qa}=-\frac{4m\gamma}{\hbar^{2}}B\cosh(qa)\implies q\,e^{qa}=\frac{2m\gamma}{\hbar^{2}}\cosh(qa)$$

Multiplying numerator and denominator on the right by $2/e^{qa}$:

$$q=\frac{m\gamma}{\hbar^{2}}\big(1+e^{-2qa}\big)\iff\frac{\hbar^{2}q}{m\gamma}=1+e^{-2qa}$$

`\end{proof}`
