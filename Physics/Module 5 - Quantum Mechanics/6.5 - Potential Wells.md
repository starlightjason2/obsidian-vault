# 6.5 - Potential Wells

## Particle in a Box

> [!exm] Infinite Square Well
> Consider a particle in a potential well with infinite walls.
>
> $$V(x)=\begin{cases} 0 & 0\leq x\leq L \\ \infty & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$
>
> Solve the [[6.3 - Energy#^ecd6eb]]. Let initial state $\psi(x,0)=\sum c_{n}\,\phi(x)$ be in the energy eigenbasis. The energy eigenvalue equation is:
> $$\phi''(x) +\left( \frac{2mE}{\hbar^{2}} \right) \phi(x) = 0$$
>
> This is a [[4.1 - Simple Harmonic Oscillation#^0fkxyq]] where $k^2 = {2mE_{n}} / {\hbar^2}$. By [[4.0 - ODEs#^dirichlet]] $\psi(0,t)=\psi(L,t)=0$, we get $n\pi=kL$, which we solve for the energy $E_{n}$.
>
>
> $$\psi(x,t) = \sqrt{\frac{2}{L}}\,\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} c_n\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)\,e^{-iE_nt/\hbar}, \qquad E_n = \frac{n^{2}\pi ^{2}\hbar ^{2}}{2mL^2}$$
>
> Applying [[6.4 - Position and Momentum#^pos-mom-duality]], a Fourier transform on the $n$th energy state $\psi_n(x)$ gives the momentum-space wave function:
>
> $$
> \begin{align}
\psi_n(x)&=\sqrt{\frac{2}{L}}\sin\left( \frac{n\pi x}{L} \right) \implies\\[1em]
> \tilde{\psi}_n(p)&=\left( \sqrt{\frac{p_n}{\pi L}}\,\frac{p_n\!\left(1-(-1)^n e^{-ipL/\hbar}\right)}{p_n^2-p^2} \right)\begin{cases}\cos(pL/2\hbar) & n\text{ odd}\\\,i\sin(pL/2\hbar) & n\text{ even}\end{cases}\quad \text{where } p_n=\frac{n\pi\hbar}{L}
\end{align}
> $$
>
> The probability density $|\tilde{\psi}_n(p)|^2$ gives the momentum distribution of the eigenstate — peaked at $p=\pm p_n=\pm n\pi\hbar/L$ (the two counter-propagating plane waves making up $\sin(n\pi x/L)$).
>
>

^2411f8

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^2411f8]]
We use the known SHO solution for $k^2 = {2mE} / {\hbar^2}$. Inside the well, $V(x)=0$, and we assume the particle is never outside the well due to infinite walls.

$$
\begin{align}
\phi''(x) +\left( \frac{2mE_{n}}{\hbar^{2}} \right) \phi(x) &= 0 \implies\\[0.5em]
 A\cos(kx) + B\sin(kx)&=\phi(x)
\end{align}
$$

Applying boundary conditions $X(0) = X(L) = 0$: $A=0$ because $\cos(0)=1$, and $B\sin(kL)=0 \implies \sin(kL)=0$, so $kL=n\pi$. Solving for $E_{n}$,

$$k = \frac{n\pi}{L}=\frac{\sqrt{2mE}}{\hbar}\implies  E=\frac{k^{2}\hbar^{2}}{2m}\implies E_{n}=\frac{n^{2}\pi^{2}\hbar^{2}}{2mL^{2}}$$

We can find $B$ by normalizing over the box: $\int_0^L |B\sin\!\left(\frac{n\pi x}{L}\right)|^2\, dx = 1 \implies B = \sqrt{\frac{2}{L}}$.

Apply the Fourier transform with $k=p/\hbar$, $k_n=n\pi/L$:

$$\tilde{\psi}_n(p)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\hbar}}\int_0^L\sqrt{\frac{2}{L}}\sin(k_n x)\,e^{-ikx}\,dx=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi\hbar L}}\int_0^L\frac{e^{i(k_n-k)x}-e^{-i(k_n+k)x}}{2i}\,dx$$

Evaluating both integrals in one:

$$\int_0^L e^{i(k'\mp k)x}dx=\frac{1-e^{i(k'\mp k)L}}{-i(k'\mp k)}$$

 Using $k_n L=n\pi$ (so $e^{\pm ik_n L}=(-1)^n$) gives a common factor $(1-(-1)^n e^{-ikL})$, from which the result follows after simplification.
`\end{proof}`

## Finite Well

> [!exm] Finite Square Well
> Consider a particle in a symmetric finite potential well.
>
> $$V(x)=\begin{cases}0 & |x|>a \\ -V_0 & |x|\leq a\end{cases}$$
>
> Solve the [[6.3 - Energy#^ecd6eb]]. For bound states ($E<0$), define
> $$\gamma=\frac{\sqrt{-2mE}}{\hbar}, \qquad k=\frac{\sqrt{2m(E+V_0)}}{\hbar}$$
> Outside the well the eigenvalue equation is a [[4.1.3 - Constant Coefficient ODEs#^382f29|Constant Coefficient ODE]] with real roots; inside it is a [[4.1 - Simple Harmonic Oscillation#^d9cde8|Simple Harmonic ODE]]. The general solution is:
> $$\phi(x)=\begin{cases}Ce^{\gamma x} & x<-a \\ B\cos(kx)+A\sin(kx) & |x|\leq a \\ De^{-\gamma x} & x>a\end{cases}$$
>
> Since $V(x)$ is symmetric, solutions have definite parity. Even parity sets $A=0$; continuity at $x=a$ gives:
> $$\frac{\gamma}{k}=\tan(ka),\quad \frac{\gamma}{k}>0$$
>
> Odd parity sets $B=0$; continuity at $x=a$ gives:
> $$\frac{\gamma}{k}=-\cot(ka),\quad \frac{\gamma}{k}>0$$
>
> Both are transcendental equations solved graphically using:
>
> $$\gamma^2+k^2=\frac{2mV_0}{\hbar^2}$$
>
> Odd solutions only exist above a minimum well depth; even solutions always have at least one bound state.

^finite-well

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^finite-well]]
The [[6.3 - Energy#^ecd6eb|Time-Invariant Schrödinger Equation]] takes a different form in each region depending on the local potential.

Outside the well where $|x|>a$ and $V=0$, the bound-state condition $E<0$ gives
$$\phi''-\gamma^2 \phi=0, \qquad \gamma=\frac{\sqrt{-2mE}}{\hbar}>0$$
The exponentially growing solution must be discarded for normalizability, leaving:

$$\phi(x)=\begin{cases}Ce^{\gamma x} & x<-a \\ De^{-\gamma x} & x>a\end{cases}$$

Inside the well where $|x|\leq a$ and $V=-V_0$, the equation becomes
$$\phi''+k^2 \phi=0, \qquad k=\frac{\sqrt{2m(E+V_0)}}{\hbar}>0$$
giving oscillatory solutions. Since $V(x)$ is symmetric, solutions have definite parity: even solutions take the form $\phi(x)=B\cos(kx)$ and odd solutions $\phi(x)=A\sin(kx)$.

Continuity of $u$ and $\phi'$ at $x=a$ is enforced by matching the logarithmic derivative $\phi'/u$ on both sides. For even parity:

$$\frac{\phi'}{\psi}\bigg|_{\text{inside}}=\frac{\phi'}{\psi}\bigg|_{\text{outside}}\implies \frac{-Bk\sin(ka)}{B\cos(ka)}=\frac{-D\gamma e^{-\gamma a}}{De^{-\gamma a}}\implies -k\tan(ka)=-\gamma$$

$$\therefore\quad \frac{\gamma}{k}=\tan(ka)$$

For odd parity the same procedure gives $k\cot(ka)=-\gamma$, i.e.

$$\therefore\quad \frac{\gamma}{k}=-\cot(ka)$$

Both equations require $\gamma/k>0$, which selects specific branches of $\tan$ and $-\cot$. Adding $\gamma^2$ and $k^2$ from their definitions cancels $E$:

$$\gamma^2+k^2=\frac{-2mE}{\hbar^2}+\frac{2m(E+V_0)}{\hbar^2}=\frac{2mV_0}{\hbar^2}$$

This is a circle of radius $r=\sqrt{2mV_0}/\hbar$ in the $(k,\gamma)$ plane. Solutions are found where this circle intersects $\gamma/k=\tan(ka)$ or $\gamma/k=-\cot(ka)$. Each even solution exists for $r>(n-1)\pi/a$ and each odd solution for $r>(2n-1)\pi/(2a)$, so there is always at least one even bound state regardless of well depth.
`\end{proof}`

## Delta Well

> [!exm] Delta Function Well
> Consider a particle in an attractive delta function potential.
>
> $$V(x)=-\gamma\,\delta(x), \qquad \gamma>0$$
>
> Solve the [[6.3 - Energy#^ecd6eb]]. Away from the origin, the energy eigenvalue equation for bound states $E<0$ is a [[4.1.3 - Constant Coefficient ODEs#^382f29|Constant Coefficient ODE]] with real roots:
> $$\phi''-q^2\phi=0, \qquad q=\frac{\sqrt{-2mE}}{\hbar}$$
>
> so the general solution is:
> $$\phi(x)=\begin{cases}Ae^{qx} & x<0 \\ Ae^{-qx} & x>0\end{cases}$$
>
> Integrating the Schrödinger equation across the origin gives a derivative jump condition:
> $$\left.\frac{d\phi}{dx}\right|_{0^+}-\left.\frac{d\phi}{dx}\right|_{0^-}=-\frac{2m\gamma}{\hbar^2}\phi(0)$$
>
> This potential supports exactly one bound state:
> $$\phi(x)=\sqrt{\frac{m\gamma}{\hbar^2}}\,\begin{cases}e^{m\gamma x/\hbar^2} & x<0 \\ e^{-m\gamma x/\hbar^2} & x>0\end{cases}, \qquad E=-\frac{m\gamma^2}{2\hbar^2}$$

^delta-well

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^delta-well]]
Away from the origin $V=0$, so the bound-state condition $E<0$ gives
$$\phi''-q^2 \phi=0, \qquad q=\frac{\sqrt{-2mE}}{\hbar}>0$$
Discarding the exponentially growing terms on each side leaves $\phi(x)=Ae^{-q|x|}$, which is continuous at $x=0$ by construction.

To find the constraint on $q$, integrate the energy eigenvalue ODE over $[-\varepsilon,+\varepsilon]$ and take $\varepsilon\to 0$:

$$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\int_{-\varepsilon}^{+\varepsilon}\phi''\,dx+\int_{-\varepsilon}^{+\varepsilon}V(x)u\,dx=E\int_{-\varepsilon}^{+\varepsilon}u\,dx$$

By the fundamental theorem of calculus the kinetic term becomes $\phi'(0^+)-\phi'(0^-)$. The potential term gives $-\gamma\int\delta(x)u\,dx=-\gamma \phi(0)$. The right side vanishes as $\varepsilon\to 0$ since $u$ is bounded. Therefore:

$$-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\left[\phi'(0^+)-\phi'(0^-)\right]-\gamma \phi(0)=0\implies \phi'(0^+)-\phi'(0^-)=-\frac{2m\gamma}{\hbar^2}\phi(0)$$

With $\phi(x)=Ae^{-q|x|}$, the one-sided derivatives at the origin are $\phi'(0^+)=-qA$ and $\phi'(0^-)=+qA$. Substituting into the jump condition:

$$-2qA=-\frac{2m\gamma}{\hbar^2}A\implies q=\frac{m\gamma}{\hbar^2}$$

The bound state energy then follows directly from the definition of $q$:

$$E=-\frac{\hbar^2 q^2}{2m}=-\frac{m\gamma^2}{2\hbar^2}$$

For normalization:

$$\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-2q|x|}\,dx=2\int_0^\infty e^{-2qx}\,dx=\frac{1}{q}\implies A^2\cdot\frac{1}{q}=1\implies A=\sqrt{q}=\sqrt{\frac{m\gamma}{\hbar^2}}$$
`\end{proof}`
