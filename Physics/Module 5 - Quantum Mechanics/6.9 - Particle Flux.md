# 6.9 - Particle Flux

Classically, particle flux in 3D is the flow rate of particles through an area $A$: with $N=\rho Av\,dt$ particles passing through in time $dt$, the flux is $S=\rho v$. For a quantum particle, $\rho=|\psi|^2$ and the velocity is given by the momentum operator, leading to the probability current.

## Conservation of Probability

> [!def] Probability Current
> The **probability current** $S$ describes the flow of [[6.0 - Postulates#^probability-postulate|Probability Density]] through space, analogous to fluid flow. It measures how much probability passes a point per unit time, given a wavefunction $\psi(x,t)$.
>
> In one and three dimensions:
> $$S=\frac{\hbar}{2mi}\left(\psi^*\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x}-\psi\frac{\partial\psi^*}{\partial x}\right),\qquad \mathbf{S}=\frac{\hbar}{2mi}(\psi^*\nabla\psi-\psi\nabla\psi^*)$$
>
> For a plane wave $\psi=Ae^{ikx}$ this reduces to $S=\hbar k|A|^{2}/m=\rho v$, recovering the classical flux.

^probability-current

> [!def] Continuity Equation
> The [[3.6 - Vector Derivatives#^divergence-in-orthogonal-coordinates |Divergence]] of the [[#^probability-current]] $\nabla\cdot \mathbf{S}$ is equal to the negative time derivative of the [[6.0 - Postulates#^probability-postulate |Probability]] $\rho(x,t)=\left\lvert{\psi}\right\rvert^{2}$.
>
> $$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{S}(x,t)=-\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t}$$
>
> Or in one dimension,
>
> $$\frac{\partial S}{\partial x}=-\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t}$$
>

^continuity-equation

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^continuity-equation]]
Multiply the [[6.0 - Postulates#^597a72|Schrödinger Equation]] $i\hbar\partial_t\psi=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\partial_x^2\psi+V\psi$ by $\psi^*$, and multiply its complex conjugate by $\psi$:

$$\begin{align}
i\hbar\psi^*\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}&=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\psi^*\frac{\partial^2\psi}{\partial x^2}+V|\psi|^2 \\[1em]
-i\hbar\psi\frac{\partial\psi^*}{\partial t}&=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\psi\frac{\partial^2\psi^*}{\partial x^2}+V|\psi|^2
\end{align}$$

Subtracting the second equation from the first cancels both $V|\psi|^{2}$ terms on the right and combines the two time derivatives on the left through the product rule $\partial_{t}|\psi|^{2}=\psi^{*}\partial_{t}\psi+\psi\,\partial_{t}\psi^{*}$:

$$i\hbar\!\left(\psi^{*}\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}+\psi\frac{\partial\psi^{*}}{\partial t}\right)=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\!\left(\psi^{*}\frac{\partial^{2}\psi}{\partial x^{2}}-\psi\frac{\partial^{2}\psi^{*}}{\partial x^{2}}\right)$$

The left side collapses to $i\hbar\,\partial_{t}|\psi|^{2}$. The right side is the product rule on $\psi^{*}\partial_{x}\psi-\psi\,\partial_{x}\psi^{*}$:

$$\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\!\left(\psi^{*}\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x}-\psi\frac{\partial\psi^{*}}{\partial x}\right)=\psi^{*}\frac{\partial^{2}\psi}{\partial x^{2}}-\psi\frac{\partial^{2}\psi^{*}}{\partial x^{2}}$$

Where the cross terms $\partial_{x}\psi^{*}\partial_{x}\psi$ cancel. Substituting back:

$$i\hbar\frac{\partial|\psi|^2}{\partial t}=-\frac{\hbar^2}{2m}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(\psi^*\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial x}-\psi\frac{\partial\psi^*}{\partial x}\right)$$

Dividing by $i\hbar$ and recognising $S$ on the right gives $\partial_t\rho+\partial_x S=0$.
`\end{proof}`
