# 5.1 - Galilean Relativity

There is no absolute reference frame in the universe, because everything is moving relative to something. We can only measure motions in a **reference frame**. The earth rotates at $\pu{1000m/s}$, and the sun rotates around the center of the earth at $\pu{200m/s}$.

A non-accelerating reference frame is called an **inertial reference frame**. Usually, we measure objects relative to the Earth's reference frame. If a reference frame has a velocity, then the net force will be the velocity of the reference frame and the velocity of the object within that reference frame.

## Galilean Relativity

Consider $n$ reference frames, each with a velocity $v_{i}$ and a clock measuring time $t_{i}$. Each clock is initially synced at $t=0$. In Galilean, or classical, relativity, the **clocks always agree**.

> [!thm] Galilean Transformation
> Consider an observer in a rest frame $S$ measuring time and space $t\\,\mathbf{x}$. The observer sees frame $S_{2}$ measuring spacetime $\left<t_{2},x_{2}\right>$ and moving at **constant velocity $v\,\hat{\boldsymbol{\imath}}$**. The **boost matrix** transforms the rest frame into the moving frame.
>
>
>$$\begin{align} \\
\begin{bmatrix}
t_{2} \\
x_{2} \\
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 0 \\
-v & 1
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}
t_{1}\\
x_{1}\\
\end{bmatrix}
\end{align}$$
>
> $$\begin{align} \\
\begin{bmatrix}
t_{1}\\
x_{1}\\
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}
1 & 0 \\
v & 1
\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}
t_{2} \\
x_{2} \\
\end{bmatrix}
\end{align}$$
