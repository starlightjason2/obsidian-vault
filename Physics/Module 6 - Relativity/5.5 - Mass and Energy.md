# 5.5 - Mass and Energy

## Energy

> [!def] Relativistic Energy
>$$
>E=p_{4}\,c=\gamma\,mc^{2}
>$$

### Rest Energy

> [!def] Rest Energy
> The rest energy $E$ of a [[5.4 - Velocity and Momentum#^rest-mass]] $m$ is
>
> $$E=mc^{2}$$
>

^rest-energy

Let's make sense of relativistic energy by expanding the [[5.2 - Relativistic Effects#^lorentz-factor]] with the [[6.2 - Taylor Series#^0bbd72]].

$$
\gamma=\left( 1-\frac{v^{2}}{c^{2}} \right)^{-1/2} =1+\frac{1}{2}\frac{v^{2}}{c^{2}}+\dots
$$

so

$$
E=mc^{}\left( 1+\frac{1}{2}\frac{v^{2}}{c^{2}}+\dots\, \right) =mc+\frac{1}{c}\frac{1}{2}mv^{2}+\dots
$$

Multiply both sides by $c$ to get the relativistic kinetic energy

$$
p\,c=\gamma \,mc^{2} =mc^{2}+\frac{1}{2}mv^{2} +\dots
$$

So the relativistic kinetic energy is the **rest energy** $mc^{2}$, plus the Newtonian [[1.5 - Work and Energy#^kinetic-energy]], plus higher order **relativistic corrections**.

> [!def] Relativistic Kinetic Energy
> The relativistic [[1.5 - Work and Energy#^kinetic-energy]] of a [[5.4 - Velocity and Momentum#^rest-mass]] $m$ with a [[5.2 - Relativistic Effects#^lorentz-factor]] $\gamma$ is
>
> $$\mathbf{u}=\frac{d\mathbf{s}}{dt_{0}}=\gamma\begin{bmatrix}
c\\
\mathbf{v}
\end{bmatrix}$$

^b9156a

`\begin{proof}`@[[#^b9156a]]

Let there be a rest frame $S$ and a moving frame $S'$. Let there be an object moving at constant velocity $\mathbf{v}=\frac{d\mathbf{x}}{dt}$ in $S$.

Because of [[5.2 - Relativistic Effects#^time-dilation]], $t=\gamma\,t_{0}$ or $dt_{0}=\gamma^{-1}\,dt$. We can rewrite $\mathbf{u}$ as $\frac{dx}{\gamma^{-1}\,dt}=\gamma\,\frac{dx}{dt}$.

$$\frac{d\mathbf{s}}{dt_{0}}=\begin{bmatrix} 
\frac{d}{dt_{0}}\gamma\,c t_{0}\\
\frac{d\mathbf{x}}{dt_{0}}
\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}
\gamma\,c\\
\gamma\,\mathbf{v}
\end{bmatrix}$$

`\end{proof}`
